## dn30:1.1.7  `ms8D_398, msdiv199`
- **PLI**: “Dvattiṁsimāni, bhikkhave, mahāpurisassa mahāpurisalakkhaṇāni, yehi samannāgatassa mahāpurisassa dveva gatiyo bhavanti anaññā.
- **EN**: “There are thirty-two marks of a great man. A great man who possesses these has only two possible destinies, no other.
- **NOTE**: This is one of the latest suttas in the Pali Nikāyas. Typically this topic only comes up in relation to the Buddha’s fulfillment of Brahmanical prophecies. Here it is developed with a series of ornate verses, none of which have parallels. Nonetheless, it is worth noting that nowhere in the extensive discussions of the Buddha’s past good deeds is there any mention of the later concepts of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāramīs</i> (“perfections”) or the Bodhisatta path.

## dn30:1.1.8
- **PLI**: Sace agāraṁ ajjhāvasati, rājā hoti cakkavattī dhammiko dhammarājā cāturanto vijitāvī janapadatthāvariyappatto sattaratanasamannāgato.
- **EN**: If he stays at home he becomes a king, a wheel-turning monarch, a just and principled king. His dominion extends to all four sides, he achieves stability in the country, and he possesses the seven treasures.

## dn30:1.1.9
- **PLI**: Tassimāni satta ratanāni bhavanti;
- **EN**: He has the following seven treasures:

## dn30:1.1.10
- **PLI**: Seyyathidaṁ—cakkaratanaṁ hatthiratanaṁ assaratanaṁ maṇiratanaṁ itthiratanaṁ gahapatiratanaṁ pariṇāyakaratanameva sattamaṁ.
- **EN**: the wheel, the elephant, the horse, the jewel, the woman, the householder, and the commander as the seventh treasure.

## dn30:1.1.11
- **PLI**: Parosahassaṁ kho panassa puttā bhavanti sūrā vīraṅgarūpā parasenappamaddanā.
- **EN**: He has over a thousand sons who are valiant and heroic, crushing the armies of his enemies.

## dn30:1.1.12
- **PLI**: So imaṁ pathaviṁ sāgarapariyantaṁ adaṇḍena asatthena dhammena abhivijiya ajjhāvasati.
- **EN**: After conquering this land girt by sea, he reigns by principle, without rod or sword.

## dn30:1.1.13
- **PLI**: Sace kho pana agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajati, arahaṁ hoti sammāsambuddho loke vivaṭṭacchado.
- **EN**: But if he goes forth from the lay life to homelessness, he becomes a perfected one, a fully awakened Buddha, who has cast off the world’s veil.
