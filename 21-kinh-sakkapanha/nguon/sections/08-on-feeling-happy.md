- **NOTE**: “Nectar” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ojā</i>, in Vedic texts called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>soma</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>amṛta</i> (“ambrosia” of immortality). ## dn21:2.7.29
- **PLI**: So kho pana me, bhante, vedapaṭilābho somanassapaṭilābho sadaṇḍāvacaro sasatthāvacaro na nibbidāya na virāgāya na nirodhāya na upasamāya na abhiññāya na sambodhāya na nibbānāya saṁvattati.
- **EN**: But sir, that joy and happiness is in the sphere of the rod and the sword. It doesn’t lead to disillusionment, dispassion, cessation, peace, insight, awakening, and extinguishment.

## dn21:2.7.30
- **PLI**: Yo kho pana me ayaṁ, bhante, bhagavato dhammaṁ sutvā vedapaṭilābho somanassapaṭilābho, so adaṇḍāvacaro asatthāvacaro ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattatī”ti.
- **EN**: But the joy and happiness I feel listening to the Buddha’s teaching is not in the sphere of the rod and the sword. It does lead to disillusionment, dispassion, cessation, peace, insight, awakening, and extinguishment.”
- **NOTE**: Here Sakka makes a clean break from the violent delights he enjoyed in former (Vedic) times.
