## dn21:2.6.2  `ms7D_901`
- **PLI**: “Sabbeva nu kho, mārisa, samaṇabrāhmaṇā ekantavādā ekantasīlā ekantachandā ekantaajjhosānā”ti?
- **EN**: “Good fellow, do all ascetics and brahmins have a single doctrine, ethics, desire, and attachment?”
- **NOTE**: The Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ekanta</i> (“single”) here is a little tricky; it could mean either “the same” or “of one goal”. The Sanskrit has just <i lang='pi' translate='no'>eka</i> (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ekakāmā ekacchandāḥ</i>, “one desire, one wish”), while the commentary explains the first and last terms as “one goal” and the middle terms as “one”.

## dn21:2.6.9  `dr10.314, ms7D_903, pts-vp-pli2.283`
- **PLI**: “Sabbeva nu kho, mārisa, samaṇabrāhmaṇā accantaniṭṭhā accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosānā”ti?

- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Yogakkhema</i> (“sanctuary from the yoke”) is a metaphor for Nibbāna that draws from the literal sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khema</i> as an oasis or sanctuary for men and beasts. After a hard day’s journey, a caravan would stop to rest, “unyoke” the draft animals, lay down the burden, and refresh themselves in the cool waters.
