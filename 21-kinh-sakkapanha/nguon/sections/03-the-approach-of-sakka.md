## dn21:1.9.3
- **PLI**: “acchariyamidaṁ āyasmato kosiyassa, abbhutamidaṁ āyasmato kosiyassa tāva bahukiccassa bahukaraṇīyassa yadidaṁ idhāgamanan”ti.
- **EN**: “It’s incredible and amazing that you, the venerable Kosiya, who has so many duties and so much to do, should come here.”
- **NOTE**: It is rare for the Buddha to address a deity as “venerable” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyasmā</i>), a term usually reserved for monks. Moggallāna addresses Sakka as “the venerable Kosiya” at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn37/en/sujato#10.6'>MN 37:10.6</a>; Mahākassapa calls him Kosiya without honorific at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud3.7/en/sujato#3.12'>Ud 3.7:3.12</a>. | The word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kosiya</i> is explained by the commentaries as “owl”, which, if correct, would have been the totem for a clan of that name. It is, however, a patronymic: Rig Veda 1.10.11 has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>indra kauśika</i> which means “Indra, son of Kuśika (or Kuśa)”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Kuśa</i> grass is critical to the performance of Vedic rites, and the label probably initially implied “Brahmanized”, i.e. a king whose reign was authorized according to Vedic ritual. Kosiya is said to be a low class family name (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc2/en/sujato#2.1.18'>Bu Pc 2:2.1.18</a>).
