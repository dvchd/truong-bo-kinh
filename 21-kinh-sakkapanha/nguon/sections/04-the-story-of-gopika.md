## dn21:1.11.5  `bj8.408, cck10.323, csp1ed7.217, csp2ed7.217, dr10.302, mc10.232, ms7D_851, msdiv353, ndp7.203, pts-vp-pli2.272, sya10.306, vri2.200`
- **PLI**: Idheva, bhante, kapilavatthusmiṁ gopikā nāma sakyadhītā ahosi buddhe pasannā dhamme pasannā saṅghe pasannā sīlesu paripūrakārinī.
- **EN**: Right here in Kapilavatthu there was a Sakyan lady named Gopikā who had confidence in the Buddha, the teaching, and the Saṅgha, and had fulfilled her ethics.
- **NOTE**: This implies she was a stream-enterer.

## dn21:1.11.6
- **PLI**: Sā itthittaṁ virājetvā purisattaṁ bhāvetvā kāyassa bhedā paraṁ maraṇā sugatiṁ saggaṁ lokaṁ upapannā.
- **EN**: Losing her attachment to femininity, she developed masculinity. When her body broke up, after death, she was reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm.
- **NOTE**: In Buddhism, sex is mutable between lives and even within one life (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato#10.6.1'>Bu Pj 1:10.6.1</a>). Later Buddhists sometimes held that the status of a male was preferable. Here this is not stated outright, but the narrative implies that she thought her station was improved when becoming a male. Notably, it is not the Buddha who says this, but Sakka, relaying the story of another deity. That the proponent was a woman, at least formerly, is a trope of Indian literature: a woman is the ideal misogynist (see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja61/en/sujato'>Ja 61</a> or Śivapurāna chapter 4). Here, though, the situation is nuanced, because despite her (presumed) inferior status, she has better rebirth than the monks she served.

## dn21:1.11.13
- **PLI**: ‘kutomukhā nāma tumhe, mārisā, tassa bhagavato dhammaṁ assuttha—
- **EN**: ‘Where on earth were you at, good fellows, when you heard the Buddha’s teaching!
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Kutomukhā</i>, which occurs only in this sutta, contrasts with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sammukhā</i> (“in the presence of”, “from the mouth of”). The commentary explains it as a rhetorical question, “Were you distracted or falling asleep?” Alternatively, it might mean “From whom did you learn the Buddha’s teaching?” It recurs later in the verses. This is not the only such case: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>duddiṭṭharūpaṁ</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāyaṁ brahmapurohitaṁ</i> are other examples of terms found nowhere else in the Pali canon, yet found here both in prose and in verse. Clearly one has been copied from the other; it seems to me that the rarity of these terms and the greater coherence of the verses suggests that the prose is a (somewhat altered) summary of the verses.

- **EN**: When scolded by Gopaka, two of those gods regained their memory right away. They went to the host of the priests of Divinity, but one god remained attached to sensual pleasures.
- **NOTE**: The commentary explains the verb “gained” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭilabhiṁsu</i>) as serving dual roles here: “gaining” of “absorption mindfulness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhānasatiṁ</i>), upon which they “gain” rebirth in the Brahmā realm. However, the verses below speak of “recollecting” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anussaraṁ</i>) the Buddha’s teachings at this juncture. Compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-np23/en/sujato#1.1.20'>Bu NP 23:1.1.20</a>, where the king “remembered” his long-forgotten promise (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vissaritvā cirena satiṁ paṭilabhitvā</i>). <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.191/en/sujato#2.24'>AN 4.191:2.24</a> speaks of the “arising of memory” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>satuppādo</i>) in a deity when reminded of Dhamma passages they once recited as a monk. | The phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāyaṁ brahmapurohitaṁ</i> is syntactically incongruous; I think it was clumsily copied from the verses below, where the required verb (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ajjhagaṁsu</i>) is present. The commentary explains it as “a Minister of Brahmā’s body” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmapurohitasarīraṁ</i>), but in such contexts <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāya</i> usually means “host”.
