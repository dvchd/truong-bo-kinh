# Muc 11 — The Earth’s Nectar Appears  (14 đoạn)

## dn27:11.0
- **PLI**: 2. Rasapathavipātubhāva
- **EN**: 2. The Earth’s Nectar Appears

## dn27:11.1  `dr11.93, ms8D_253, msdiv120, ndp8.67, pts-cs11, sya11.93, vri3.63`
- **PLI**: Ekodakībhūtaṁ kho pana, vāseṭṭha, tena samayena hoti andhakāro andhakāratimisā.
- **EN**: But the single mass of water at that time was utterly dark.
- **NOTE**: Creation myths often begin with a dark, formless world of water, and describe the emergence of divisions and structures of the world. They do not differentiate the physical, biological, ecological, or social, as such distinctions came later. | Aitareya Upaniṣad 1.3.3 says the Divinity “brooded over the waters” and created food.

## dn27:11.2
- **PLI**: Na candimasūriyā paññāyanti, na nakkhattāni tārakarūpāni paññāyanti, na rattindivā paññāyanti, na māsaḍḍhamāsā paññāyanti, na utusaṁvaccharā paññāyanti, na itthipumā paññāyanti, sattā sattātveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchanti.
- **EN**: The moon and sun were not found, nor were stars and constellations, day and night, months and fortnights, years and seasons, or male and female. Beings were simply known as ‘beings’.
- **NOTE**: Throughout, the discourse has a special interest in tracing the origins of language and how different usages came about. Here the term “beings” is presented as a natural choice: they are “beings” because they are alive. Later, words are selected to convey a certain political or moral stance.

## dn27:11.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho tesaṁ, vāseṭṭha, sattānaṁ kadāci karahaci dīghassa addhuno accayena rasapathavī udakasmiṁ samatani;
- **EN**: After a very long period had passed, the earth’s nectar curdled in the water.
- **NOTE**: The reading and sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samatani</i> is obscure. I think it is connected with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>santānaka</i> in the next line, in the sense of “networks” or tendrils appearing in the water as it curdles. The Sanskrit at SF 277 and Mūlasarvāstvāda Vinaya 17 has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁmūrcchitaḥ saṁtanoti</i> (“curdled tendrils”). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Rasapathavī</i> (“earth’s nectar”) is also obscure. We would expect <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pathavīrasa</i> (cp. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>phalarasa</i> “fruit juice”, etc.), which is indeed found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn5.9/en/sujato#5.3'>SN 5.9:5.3</a> in the sense “nutriments in soil”. Since this reading also appears here in the Sanskrit sources, I assume it is the correct sense. For another example of a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tappurisa</i> compound with reversed order, see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn4/en/sujato#6.46'>DN 4:6.46</a>.

## dn27:11.4
- **PLI**: seyyathāpi nāma payaso tattassa nibbāyamānassa upari santānakaṁ hoti;
- **EN**: It appeared just like the curd on top of hot milk-rice as it cools.

## dn27:11.5
- **PLI**: evameva pāturahosi.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)

## dn27:11.6
- **PLI**: Sā ahosi vaṇṇasampannā gandhasampannā rasasampannā, seyyathāpi nāma sampannaṁ vā sappi sampannaṁ vā navanītaṁ evaṁvaṇṇā ahosi.
- **EN**: It was beautiful, fragrant, and delicious, like ghee or butter.

## dn27:11.7
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi nāma khuddamadhuṁ aneḷakaṁ;
- **EN**: And it was as sweet as pure dwarf-bee honey.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Khuddā</i> (literally “small one”) is said to be a species of small bee, also known in Sanskrit as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣudrā</i>. The “dwarf bee” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>apis florea</i>) fits the bill, as it is a small wild honeybee found in India.

## dn27:11.9
- **PLI**: Atha kho, vāseṭṭha, aññataro satto lolajātiko:
- **EN**: Now, one of those beings was reckless.

## dn27:11.10
- **PLI**: ‘ambho, kimevidaṁ bhavissatī’ti rasapathaviṁ aṅguliyā sāyi.
- **EN**: Thinking, ‘Oh my, what might this be?’ they tasted the earth’s nectar with their finger.
- **NOTE**: Ghee and honey were among the finest offerings to the gods. The soma was often described as “honeyed”. Our text is suggesting that the Vedic sacrifices led to the corruption of divinity.

## dn27:11.11
- **PLI**: Tassa rasapathaviṁ aṅguliyā sāyato acchādesi, taṇhā cassa okkami.
- **EN**: They enjoyed it, and craving was born in them.
- **NOTE**: For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>acchadesi</i> read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>assādesi</i> (“enjoyed”) after Mu Kd 17’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āsvādayati</i>. The commentary explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>acchadesi</i> as “becomes suffused”, so if it is a misreading it is an old one.

## dn27:11.12
- **PLI**: Aññepi kho, vāseṭṭha, sattā tassa sattassa diṭṭhānugatiṁ āpajjamānā rasapathaviṁ aṅguliyā sāyiṁsu.
- **EN**: And other beings, following that being’s example, tasted the earth’s nectar with their fingers.

## dn27:11.13
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ rasapathaviṁ aṅguliyā sāyataṁ acchādesi, taṇhā ca tesaṁ okkami.
- **EN**: They too enjoyed it, and craving was born in them.
