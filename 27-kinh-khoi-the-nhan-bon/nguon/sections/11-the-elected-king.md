# Muc 20 — The Elected King  (15 đoạn)

## dn27:20.0
- **PLI**: 10. Mahāsammatarājā
- **EN**: 10. The Elected King

## dn27:20.1  `cck11.104, csp1ed8.77, csp2ed8.77, ms8D_271, msdiv130, pts-cs20`
- **PLI**: Atha kho te, vāseṭṭha, sattā sannipatiṁsu, sannipatitvā anutthuniṁsu:
- **EN**: Then those beings gathered together and bemoaned,

## dn27:20.2
- **PLI**: ‘pāpakā vata bho dhammā sattesu pātubhūtā, yatra hi nāma adinnādānaṁ paññāyissati, garahā paññāyissati, musāvādo paññāyissati, daṇḍādānaṁ paññāyissati.
- **EN**: ‘Oh, how wicked things have appeared among beings, in that stealing is found, and blaming and lying and the taking up of rods!

## dn27:20.3
- **PLI**: Yannūna mayaṁ ekaṁ sattaṁ sammanneyyāma, yo no sammā khīyitabbaṁ khīyeyya, sammā garahitabbaṁ garaheyya, sammā pabbājetabbaṁ pabbājeyya.
- **EN**: Why don’t we elect one being who would rightly accuse those who deserve it, blame those who deserve it, and expel those who deserve it?
- **NOTE**: “Accuse” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khīyeyya</i>. Here the beings are taking charge of their social evolution, deciding among themselves how to manage their society. They are active agents in their own story, not merely passive subjects of evolution. And the primary decision they make is to form a democracy by election (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sammata</i>). Doing so, they create a two-class system: rulers and subjects.

## dn27:20.4
- **PLI**: Mayaṁ panassa sālīnaṁ bhāgaṁ anuppadassāmā’ti.
- **EN**: We shall pay them with a share of rice.’
- **NOTE**: The origin of upper class, white-collar workers who do not have to till the soil. Taxes are not coerced, but voluntarily offered in recognition of services rendered.

## dn27:20.5  `dr11.103, ms8D_272, ndp8.73, pts-vp-pli3.93, sya11.102`
- **PLI**: Atha kho te, vāseṭṭha, sattā yo nesaṁ satto abhirūpataro ca dassanīyataro ca pāsādikataro ca mahesakkhataro ca taṁ sattaṁ upasaṅkamitvā etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: Then those beings approached the being among them who was most attractive, good-looking, lovely, and illustrious, and said,
- **NOTE**: The emphasis on appearance reflects a wider acknowledgement of the potency of beauty in shaping human conduct. The Buddha rejected the notion that beauty indicates worth (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn21.6/en/sujato'>SN 21.6</a>), yet was himself regarded as especially beautiful. Unfair it may be, but beautiful people are listened to.

## dn27:21.1  `bj9.158, ms8D_273, msdiv131, pts-cs21, vri3.69`
- **PLI**: Mahājanasammatoti kho, vāseṭṭha, ‘mahāsammato, mahāsammato’ tveva paṭhamaṁ akkharaṁ upanibbattaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Elected by the populace’, Vāseṭṭha, is the meaning of ‘popularly elected’, the first term applied to them.
- **NOTE**: Note that “popularly elected” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahāsammata</i>) is not a name but a term describing their position. The passage suggests that the prefix <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahā</i> does not mean that the elected one is “great”, but that they were elected by the “populace” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahājana</i>). | “Applied to them” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>upanibbattaṁ</i>) is unique. The Sanskrit texts have <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁjñodapādi</i>, “arisen by agreement”. It is distinct from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn27/en/sujato#11.2'>DN 27:11.2</a> above, where beings “come to be called” beings (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkhyaṁ gacchanti</i>). Clearly it cannot mean the first word that appeared. I take it to mean this was a specialized term invented and applied just for them. The commentary says, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>na kevalaṁ akkharameva</i>, “not just a universal term”.

## dn27:21.2
- **PLI**: Khettānaṁ adhipatīti kho, vāseṭṭha, ‘khattiyo, khattiyo’ tveva dutiyaṁ akkharaṁ upanibbattaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Lord of the fields’ is the meaning of ‘aristocrat’, the second term applied to them.
- **NOTE**: “Aristocrat” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya</i>; Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣatriya</i>) is related to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khatta</i> (“authority”, “power”; in eg. Rig Veda 8.25.8 it is an adjective, “powerful”), hence “ruling class”. The sutta, however, relates it to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khetta</i>, “field”. The two senses possibly stem from the same root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kṣi</i> in the sense “owner”, “master” with the “field” being the dominion owned by the chief. In Pali suttas, the primary connotation of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya</i> was of an aristocratic land-owning class, so the connection with “field” is apt. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Khattiyas</i> are often said to be a “warrior” caste, but that is not a primary association in the suttas. They are rarely depicted as having anything to do with the military, which had become professionalized by the time of the Buddha. Nonetheless, at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn96/en/sujato#10.7'>MN 96:10.7</a> a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya’s</i> wealth is said to be the bow and arrow. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.24/en/sujato'>SN 3.24</a>, the Buddha argues that someone from any caste could be as proficient a warrior as someone from the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya</i> class.

## dn27:21.3
- **PLI**: Dhammena pare rañjetīti kho, vāseṭṭha, ‘rājā, rājā’ tveva tatiyaṁ akkharaṁ upanibbattaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘They please others with principle’ is the meaning of ‘king’, the third term applied to them.
- **NOTE**: Modern linguists trace the Proto-Indo-European root of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rāja</i> as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>*reg</i> in the sense “one who leads people in a straight line”, literally “ruler” or “regulator”. The pun <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rañjeti</i> (“they please”) is meant to emphasize the obligations that a ruler has for their people. As here, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājā</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya</i> can have the same meaning, although commonly a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājā</i> is an actual king or chief, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiya</i> is the class from which the rulers come.

## dn27:21.4
- **PLI**: Iti kho, vāseṭṭha, evametassa khattiyamaṇḍalassa porāṇena aggaññena akkharena abhinibbatti ahosi tesaṁyeva sattānaṁ, anaññesaṁ. Sadisānaṁyeva, no asadisānaṁ. Dhammeneva, no adhammena.
- **EN**: And that, Vāseṭṭha, is how the ancient primordial terms for the circle of aristocrats were created; for those very beings, not others; for those like them, not unlike; by virtue of principle, not against principle.

## dn27:21.5
- **PLI**: Dhammo hi, vāseṭṭha, seṭṭho janetasmiṁ diṭṭhe ceva dhamme abhisamparāyañca.
- **EN**: For principle, Vāseṭṭha, is the best thing for people in both this life and the next.
