# Muc 16.6 — Sex Appears  (10 đoạn)

## dn27:16.6.0
- **PLI**: 7. Itthipurisaliṅgapātubhāva
- **EN**: 7. Sex Appears

## dn27:16.7  `ms8D_262, msdiv126, sya11.97`
- **PLI**: Yathā yathā kho te, vāseṭṭha, sattā akaṭṭhapākaṁ sāliṁ paribhuñjantā tambhakkhā tadāhārā ciraṁ dīghamaddhānaṁ aṭṭhaṁsu tathā tathā tesaṁ sattānaṁ bhiyyoso mattāya kharattañceva kāyasmiṁ okkami, vaṇṇavevaṇṇatā ca paññāyittha, itthiyā ca itthiliṅgaṁ pāturahosi purisassa ca purisaliṅgaṁ.
- **EN**: But so long as they ate that ripe untilled rice, their bodies became more solid and they diverged in appearance. And female characteristics appeared on women, while male characteristics appeared on men.
- **NOTE**: The commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato#10.6.1'>Bu Pj 1:10.6.1</a> explains “male characteristics” as “beard and whiskers, etc.” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>massudāṭhikādi</i>), so this is not just genitals but also secondary sex characteristics.

## dn27:16.8
- **PLI**: Itthī ca purisaṁ ativelaṁ upanijjhāyati puriso ca itthiṁ.
- **EN**: Women spent too much time gazing at men, and men at women.

## dn27:16.9
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ ativelaṁ aññamaññaṁ upanijjhāyataṁ sārāgo udapādi, pariḷāho kāyasmiṁ okkami.
- **EN**: They became lustful, and their bodies burned with fever.

## dn27:16.10
- **PLI**: Te pariḷāhapaccayā methunaṁ dhammaṁ paṭiseviṁsu.
- **EN**: Due to this fever they had sex with each other.

## dn27:16.11  `dr11.98, ms8D_263, ndp8.70, pts-vp-pli3.89, vri3.66`
- **PLI**: Ye kho pana te, vāseṭṭha, tena samayena sattā passanti methunaṁ dhammaṁ paṭisevante, aññe paṁsuṁ khipanti, aññe seṭṭhiṁ khipanti, aññe gomayaṁ khipanti:
- **EN**: Those who saw them having sex pelted them with dirt, clods, or cow-dung, saying,
- **NOTE**: Pali <i lang='pi' translate='no'>seṭṭhi</i> is explained by the commentary as “ash” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>chārikā</i>), although this sense does not seem to be attested anywhere else. Mahāvastu has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>leṣṭu</i> while Mu Kd 17 has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>loṣṭa</i>, both explained as “clod”. Given that it is easier to throw a clod than ash, I think these probably convey the correct sense.

## dn27:16.12
- **PLI**: ‘nassa asuci, nassa asucī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Get lost, filth! Get lost, filth!
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Nassa</i> could mean “die!” or “get lost”. I take the softer meaning, as killing has not yet appeared among these beings. | This behavior looks much like scapegoating rituals, where certain members of the community are deemed to bear the sins of all and are sacrificed to erase the sin. Often the victim was expelled from the community, temporarily or permanently.

## dn27:16.13
- **PLI**: ‘Kathañhi nāma satto sattassa evarūpaṁ karissatī’ti.
- **EN**: How on earth can one being do that to another?’
- **NOTE**: Mob shaming over perceived sexual transgression is the most primitive form of moralizing.

## dn27:16.14
- **PLI**: Tadetarahipi manussā ekaccesu janapadesu vadhuyā nibbuyhamānāya aññe paṁsuṁ khipanti, aññe seṭṭhiṁ khipanti, aññe gomayaṁ khipanti.
- **EN**: And even today people in some countries, when carrying a bride off, pelt her with dirt, clods, or cow-dung.
- **NOTE**: “Carrying off” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbuyhamānā</i>) like being swept away by a river. Mu Kd 17 says they throw powder, scent, garlands, and nets, while wishing the bride happiness and well-being.

## dn27:16.15
- **PLI**: Tadeva porāṇaṁ aggaññaṁ akkharaṁ anusaranti, na tvevassa atthaṁ ājānanti.
- **EN**: They’re just remembering an ancient primordial saying, but they don’t understand what it means.
