# Mo dau  (35 đoạn)

## dn27:1.1  `bj9.136, cck11.89, csp1ed8.66, csp2ed8.66, dr11.87, ms8D_241, msdiv111, ndp8.63, pts-cs1, pts-vp-pli3.80, sya11.87, vri3.59`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta is the closest we come to a Buddhist myth of origins. It draws on features of Vedic mythology, while giving its own spin. Related stories may be found in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato'>DN 26</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp2.7/en/sujato'>Snp 2.7</a>. The Buddha describes a natural process of evolution that requires no metaphysical intervention, yet differs strikingly from Darwinian evolution in prioritizing the role of desire over selection.

## dn27:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in the stilt longhouse of Migāra’s mother in the Eastern Monastery.
- **NOTE**: After the Jetavana, this was the best-known monastery in Sāvatthī. It was offered by the lady Visākhā, known as Migāra’s mother.

## dn27:1.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena vāseṭṭhabhāradvājā bhikkhūsu parivasanti bhikkhubhāvaṁ ākaṅkhamānā.
- **EN**: Now at that time Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja were living on probation among the mendicants in hopes of being ordained.
- **NOTE**: We met Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja as the students of Pokkharasāti and Tārukkha respectively in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn13/en/sujato'>DN 13</a>, at the end of which they went for refuge. Some time later they must have asked for ordination. The commentary says that probation (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paravasati</i>) was required, not because they had previously gone forth in other sects (as the naked ascetic Kassapa at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn8/en/sujato#24.1'>DN 8:24.1</a> or the wanderer Subhadda at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#5.29.1'>DN 16:5.29.1</a>), nor because they had committed an offense, but because they were not yet the required twenty years of age.

## dn27:1.4

- **EN**: Then in the late afternoon, the Buddha came downstairs from the longhouse and was walking mindfully in the open air, beneath the shade of the longhouse.

## dn27:3.1  `pts-cs3`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā vāseṭṭhaṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: Then the Buddha said to Vāseṭṭha,

## dn27:3.2
- **PLI**: “tumhe khvattha, vāseṭṭha, brāhmaṇajaccā brāhmaṇakulīnā brāhmaṇakulā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajitā, kacci vo, vāseṭṭha, brāhmaṇā na akkosanti na paribhāsantī”ti?
- **EN**: “Vāseṭṭha, you are both brahmins by birth and family, and have gone forth from the lay life to homelessness from a brahmin family. I hope you don’t have to suffer abuse and insults from the brahmins.”

## dn27:3.3
- **PLI**: “Taggha no, bhante, brāhmaṇā akkosanti paribhāsanti attarūpāya paribhāsāya paripuṇṇāya, no aparipuṇṇāyā”ti.
- **EN**: “Actually, sir, the brahmins do insult and abuse us with their typical insults to the fullest extent, holding nothing back.”

## dn27:3.4
- **PLI**: “Yathā kathaṁ pana vo, vāseṭṭha, brāhmaṇā akkosanti paribhāsanti attarūpāya paribhāsāya paripuṇṇāya, no aparipuṇṇāyā”ti?
- **EN**: “But how do the brahmins insult you?”

## dn27:3.5
- **PLI**: “Brāhmaṇā, bhante, evamāhaṁsu:
- **EN**: “Sir, the brahmins say:

## dn27:3.6
- **PLI**: ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīnā aññe vaṇṇā.
- **EN**: ‘Only brahmins are the best class; other classes are inferior.
- **NOTE**: This verse is also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn84/en/sujato#9.19'>MN 84:9.19</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn93/en/sujato#5.4'>MN 93:5.4</a>. | For “best class” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>seṭṭho vaṇṇo</i>) see <i lang='pi' translate='no'>varṇaśreṣṭhaḥ</i> at Mahābhārata 1.24.4c.

## dn27:3.7
- **PLI**: Brāhmaṇova sukko vaṇṇo, kaṇhā aññe vaṇṇā.
- **EN**: Only brahmins are the light class; other classes are dark.
- **NOTE**: Mahābhārata 12.181.5 says that brahmins are the white class, aristocrats red, peasants yellow, and menials black. Underlying this is an assumption of the superiority of the fair-skinned northerners of Indo-Aryan descent over the dark-skinned natives (see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.10.3'>DN 3:1.10.3</a>).

## dn27:3.8
- **PLI**: Brāhmaṇāva sujjhanti, no abrāhmaṇā.
- **EN**: Only brahmins are purified, not others.
- **NOTE**: I cannot trace this statement to Brahmanical texts, and indeed they have a strong tendency to push back against the idea that only brahmins can find purity.

## dn27:3.9
- **PLI**: Brāhmaṇāva brahmuno puttā orasā mukhato jātā brahmajā brahmanimmitā brahmadāyādā.
- **EN**: Only brahmins are the Divinity’s bosom sons, born from his mouth, born of the Divinity, created by the Divinity, heirs of the Divinity.

## dn27:3.10
- **PLI**: Te tumhe seṭṭhaṁ vaṇṇaṁ hitvā hīnamattha vaṇṇaṁ ajjhupagatā, yadidaṁ muṇḍake samaṇake ibbhe kaṇhe bandhupādāpacce.
- **EN**: You’ve both abandoned the best class to join an inferior class, namely these shavelings, fake ascetics, primitives, black spawn from the feet of our kinsman.
- **NOTE**: An allusion to the famous Puruṣasūkta (Rig Veda 10.90.12): “His (the cosmic Man’s) mouth became the brahmin, his arms became the ruler (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājanya</i>, an alternate name for the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiyas</i>), his thighs became the peasant (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vaiśya</i>); the menial (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sūdra</i>) was born from his feet.” This belongs to the latest portion of the Rig Veda, and probably represents a time when the system of four castes was established in the late Vedic period, some centuries before the Buddha. Apart from this, the Rig Veda hardly mentions any of these as castes, let alone all four together.

## dn27:3.11
- **PLI**: Tayidaṁ na sādhu, tayidaṁ nappatirūpaṁ, yaṁ tumhe seṭṭhaṁ vaṇṇaṁ hitvā hīnamattha vaṇṇaṁ ajjhupagatā yadidaṁ muṇḍake samaṇake ibbhe kaṇhe bandhupādāpacce’ti.
- **EN**: This is not right, it’s not proper!’

## dn27:3.12
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho no, bhante, brāhmaṇā akkosanti paribhāsanti attarūpāya paribhāsāya paripuṇṇāya, no aparipuṇṇāyā”ti.
- **EN**: That’s how the brahmins insult us.”

## dn27:4.6
- **PLI**: Dissanti kho pana, vāseṭṭha, brāhmaṇānaṁ brāhmaṇiyo utuniyopi gabbhiniyopi vijāyamānāpi pāyamānāpi.
- **EN**: For brahmin women are seen menstruating, being pregnant, giving birth, and breast-feeding.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha gives a rationalist critique of Vedic myth (see also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn93/en/sujato#5.9'>MN 93:5.9</a>), the fairness of which is not easy to assess. Within the Brahmanical world, especially the progressive Kosalan brahmins to whom Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja belonged, there had been a shift toward a more nuanced symbolic understanding of such claims. Yet the subtle distinctions of the philosophers rarely influence the more straightforward beliefs of the common practitioner.
