## dn27:7.8
- **PLI**: Imesañhi, vāseṭṭha, catunnaṁ vaṇṇānaṁ yo hoti bhikkhu arahaṁ khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṁyojano sammadaññāvimutto, so nesaṁ aggamakkhāyati dhammeneva, no adhammena.
- **EN**: Because any mendicant from these four classes who is perfected—with defilements ended, who has completed the spiritual journey, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, achieved their heart’s goal, utterly ended the fetter of continued existence, and is rightly freed through enlightenment—is said to be foremost by virtue of principle, not against principle.
- **NOTE**: Vedic theory claimed that the caste system was part of the order of nature (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i>) established by Brahmā, and hence to follow the obligations of caste was a duty (also <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i>). The Buddha posited a higher “principle” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i>) beyond caste. The English word “principle” carries the senses of a fundamental law; a code of conduct; a devotion to right living; a source; and an underlying faculty, all of which are aspects of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i> in this passage. Given the nuances of this Sutta, much of it in a non-Buddhist context, I use “principle” throughout rather than my normal “teaching”.
