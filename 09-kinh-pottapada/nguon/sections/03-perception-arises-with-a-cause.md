## dn9:20.1  `bj7.408, cck9.231, ms6D_707, msdiv416, pts-cs20`
- **PLI**: “Saññā nu kho, bhante, paṭhamaṁ uppajjati, pacchā ñāṇaṁ, udāhu ñāṇaṁ paṭhamaṁ uppajjati, pacchā saññā, udāhu saññā ca ñāṇañca apubbaṁ acarimaṁ uppajjantī”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, does perception arise first and knowledge afterwards? Or does knowledge arise first and perception afterwards? Or do they both arise at the same time?”
- **NOTE**: Poṭṭhapāda’s distinction between “perception” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññā</i>) and “knowledge” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñāṇa</i>) echoes Yājñavalkya’s distinction between “perception” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁjā</i>) and “consciousness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vijñāna</i>, or in the repeated passage at 4.5.13, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>prajñāna</i>).

## dn9:20.2
- **PLI**: “Saññā kho, poṭṭhapāda, paṭhamaṁ uppajjati, pacchā ñāṇaṁ, saññuppādā ca pana ñāṇuppādo hoti.
- **EN**: “Perception arises first and knowledge afterwards. The arising of perception leads to the arising of knowledge.
- **NOTE**: Perception has been described in terms of the progress through more refined meditations (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>). Only then does the “knowledge” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñāṇa</i>) of insight arise.

## dn9:20.4
- **PLI**: ‘idappaccayā kira me ñāṇaṁ udapādī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘My knowledge arose from a specific condition.’
- **NOTE**: The term “specific condition” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>idappaccayā</i>) is well known from dependent origination (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.20/en/sujato#2.3'>SN 12.20:2.3</a>), where it applies to the general situation of ongoing existence in transmigration. When developing insight, a meditator takes their own meditation experience as their primary locus. This is then generalized to an understanding of the nature of conscious existence.
