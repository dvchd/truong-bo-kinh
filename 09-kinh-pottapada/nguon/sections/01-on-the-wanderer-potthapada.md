# Muc 1 — On the Wanderer Poṭṭhapāda  (28 đoạn)

- **PLI**: 1. Poṭṭhapādaparibbājakavatthu
- **EN**: 1. On the Wanderer Poṭṭhapāda

## dn9:1.1  `bj7.388, cck9.223, csp1ed6.167, csp2ed6.167, dr9.238, km15.109, mc9.176, ms6D_685, msdiv406, ndp6.150, pts-cs1, pts-vp-pli1.178, sya9.223, vri1.160`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.

## dn9:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.

## dn9:1.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako samayappavādake tindukācīre ekasālake mallikāya ārāme paṭivasati mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṁ tiṁsamattehi paribbājakasatehi.
- **EN**: Now at that time the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda was residing together with three hundred wanderers in Mallikā’s single-halled monastery for philosophical debates, hedged by pale-moon ebony trees.
- **NOTE**: Poṭṭhapāda appears only here; he was named for a month of the lunar calendar (August/September). | Mallikā was the chief queen of Pasenadi, and her hall is mentioned in a similar context at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn78/en/sujato#1.3'>MN 78:1.3</a>. The commentary explains that the brahmins, Jains, and others would assemble there to “debate their beliefs” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samayaṁ pavadanti</i>). Evidently the monastery grounds had accommodation for many ascetics of different beliefs, but only “one hall” where they would gather for debate. We hear many times of such debates, but here we catch a glimpse of a place that was set up to facilitate them. For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>-ācīra</i>, read in the sense of “boundary, hedge” (commentary: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>timbarūrukkhapantiyā parikkhittattā</i>; cf. Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>prācīra</i>, “enclosure, hedge, fence, wall”).

## dn9:1.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṁ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṁ piṇḍāya pāvisi.
- **EN**: Then the Buddha robed up in the morning and, taking his bowl and robe, entered Sāvatthī for alms.

## dn9:2.1  `km15.110, ms6D_686, msdiv407, pts-cs2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavato etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then it occurred to him,

## dn9:2.2
- **PLI**: “atippago kho tāva sāvatthiyaṁ piṇḍāya carituṁ.
- **EN**: “It’s too early to wander for alms in Sāvatthī.
- **NOTE**: According to the commentary, when he approached the vicinity of the city gate, he decided to check the position of the sun and noticed that it was too early to enter. The commentary and sub-commentary explain that it only sounds like the Buddha was in doubt, for Buddhas deliberate before deciding on a course of action.

## dn9:2.3
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo, yena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako tenupasaṅkameyyan”ti.
- **EN**: Why don’t I go to Mallikā’s monastery to visit the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda?”
- **NOTE**: This can be understood as answering the criticism voiced in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn8/en/sujato#22.2'>DN 8:22.2</a>, that the Buddha was afraid to speak in an assembly.

## dn9:2.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo tenupasaṅkami.
- **EN**: So that’s what he did.

## dn9:3.1  `cck9.224, dr9.239, ms6D_687, msdiv408, pts-cs3, pts-vp-pli1.179`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṁ nisinno hoti unnādiniyā uccāsaddamahāsaddāya anekavihitaṁ tiracchānakathaṁ kathentiyā.
- **EN**: Now at that time, Poṭṭhapāda was sitting together with a large assembly of wanderers making an uproar, a colossal racket. They engaged in all kinds of low talk, such as
- **NOTE**: In contrast with the silence of the Buddha’s community at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#10.7'>DN 2:10.7</a>.

## dn9:3.2
- **PLI**: Seyyathidaṁ—rājakathaṁ corakathaṁ mahāmattakathaṁ senākathaṁ bhayakathaṁ yuddhakathaṁ annakathaṁ pānakathaṁ vatthakathaṁ sayanakathaṁ mālākathaṁ gandhakathaṁ ñātikathaṁ yānakathaṁ gāmakathaṁ nigamakathaṁ nagarakathaṁ janapadakathaṁ itthikathaṁ sūrakathaṁ visikhākathaṁ kumbhaṭṭhānakathaṁ pubbapetakathaṁ nānattakathaṁ lokakkhāyikaṁ samuddakkhāyikaṁ itibhavābhavakathaṁ iti vā.
- **EN**: talk about kings, bandits, and chief ministers; talk about armies, threats, and wars; talk about food, drink, clothes, and beds; talk about garlands and fragrances; talk about family, vehicles, villages, towns, cities, and countries; talk about women and heroes; talk at the street market and the place for water pots; talk about the departed; motley talk; tales of land and sea; and talk about being reborn in this or that place.

## dn9:4.1  `km15.111, ms6D_688, msdiv409, pts-cs4, sya9.224, vri1.161`
- **PLI**: Addasā kho poṭṭhapādo paribbājako bhagavantaṁ dūratova āgacchantaṁ;
- **EN**: Poṭṭhapāda saw the Buddha coming off in the distance,

## dn9:4.2
- **PLI**: disvāna sakaṁ parisaṁ saṇṭhapesi:
- **EN**: and hushed his own assembly,

## dn9:4.3
- **PLI**: “appasaddā bhonto hontu, mā bhonto saddamakattha.
- **EN**: “Be quiet, good fellows, don’t make a sound.

## dn9:4.4
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ samaṇo gotamo āgacchati.
- **EN**: Here comes the ascetic Gotama.

## dn9:4.5
- **PLI**: Appasaddakāmo kho so āyasmā appasaddassa vaṇṇavādī.
- **EN**: The venerable likes quiet and praises quiet.

## dn9:4.6
- **PLI**: Appeva nāma appasaddaṁ parisaṁ viditvā upasaṅkamitabbaṁ maññeyyā”ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully if he sees that our assembly is quiet he’ll see fit to approach.”
- **NOTE**: The Buddha encourages quiet for the sake of mental development; Poṭṭhapāda does it for the sake of reputation.

## dn9:4.7
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṁ.
- **EN**: Then those wanderers fell silent.
