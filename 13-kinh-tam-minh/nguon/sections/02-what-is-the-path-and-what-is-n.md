## dn13:10.2
- **PLI**: Kiñcāpi, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā nānāmagge paññāpenti, addhariyā brāhmaṇā tittiriyā brāhmaṇā chandokā brāhmaṇā bavhārijjhā brāhmaṇā, atha kho sabbāni tāni niyyānikā niyyanti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya.
- **EN**: Even though brahmins describe different paths—the Adhvaryu brahmins, the Taittirīya brahmins, the Chāndogya brahmins, the Cāndrāyaṇa brahmins, and the Bahvṛca brahmins—all of them still lead someone who practices them to the company of Divinity.
- **NOTE**: Identified by Wijesekera (<em>A Pali Reference to Brāhmaṇa-Caraṇas</em>, Adyar Library Bulletin, vol 20, 1956; reprinted in <em>Buddhist and Vedic Studies</em>) and Jayatilleke (<em>Early Buddhist Theory of Knowledge</em>, p. 480). I use the familiar Sanskrit forms, as the Pali has several dubious spellings and variants. Their texts and corresponding Vedas are respectively: Adhvaryu = Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (incl. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad; White Yajur Veda); Taittirīya = Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (Black Yajur Veda); Chāndogya = Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa (Sāman Veda); Cāndrāyaṇa (omitted from MS edition) = Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa (Rig Veda; spelling established by Wijesekera; see below at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn13/en/sujato#16.2'>DN 13:16.2</a>); Bahvṛca = Bahvṛca Brāhmaṇa (Rig Veda; incorporated in Aitareya and Kauśītaki.) This is the only time the Pali canon mentions these schools, but in some cases we can identify them with brahmins in the canon. Examples include the murmuring Chāndogya brahmin (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud1.4/en/sujato'>Ud 1.4</a>); or the Buddha’s former teachers, who evidently hailed from the Addhariya tradition of the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#15.1'>MN 26:15.1</a>ff.). | Jayatilleke notes that the Śatapatha describes its own adherents as Adhvaryu (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>addhariyā</i>), those priests of the Yajur Veda responsible for the physical acts at the ritual. Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 10.5.2.20 and Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa 6.5.6–10 list the Adhvaryu, Chandoga, and Bahvṛca, thus including the same schools in the same order, except the Pali has split the Yajur and Rig followers into two.

## dn13:10.3  `csp1ed6.224, csp2ed6.224, dr9.321, mc9.235, ms6D_897, vri1.216`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, gāmassa vā nigamassa vā avidūre bahūni cepi nānāmaggāni bhavanti, atha kho sabbāni tāni gāmasamosaraṇāni bhavanti;
- **EN**: It’s like a village or town that has many different roads nearby, yet all of them meet at that village.
- **NOTE**: Earlier they were arguing over which one of the paths was correct, whereas now they have shifted to a more universalist “many roads up the same mountain” position. When speaking with each other they saw each other’s views as contradictory, but when speaking with an outsider they adopted a more conciliatory position.

## dn13:10.4
- **PLI**: evameva kho, bho gotama, kiñcāpi brāhmaṇā nānāmagge paññāpenti, addhariyā brāhmaṇā tittiriyā brāhmaṇā chandokā brāhmaṇā bavhārijjhā brāhmaṇā, atha kho sabbāni tāni niyyānikā niyyanti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāyā”ti.
- **EN**: In the same way, even though brahmins describe different paths—the Adhvaryu brahmins, the Taittirīya brahmins, the Chāndogya brahmins, the Cāndrāyaṇa brahmins, and the Bahvṛca brahmins—all of them still lead someone who practices them to the company of Divinity.”
