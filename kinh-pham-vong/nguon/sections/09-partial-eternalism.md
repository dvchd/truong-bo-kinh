# P3_1_2  (69 segments)

## dn1:2.1.0
- **PLI**: 3.1.2. Ekaccasassatavāda 
- **EN**: 3.1.2. Partial Eternalism 

## dn1:2.1.1  `mc9.18, ms6D_74, msdiv38, ndp6.17, pts-cs2.1, sya9.23`
- **PLI**: Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā ekaccasassatikā ekaccaasassatikā ekaccaṁ sassataṁ ekaccaṁ asassataṁ attānañca lokañca paññapenti catūhi vatthūhi. 
- **EN**: There are some ascetics and brahmins who are partial eternalists, who assert that the self and the cosmos are partially eternal and partially not eternal on four grounds. 
- **NOTE**: Despite being views of the “self and the cosmos”, the main focus in the next four views is the self. 

## dn1:2.1.2
- **PLI**: Te ca bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā kimāgamma kimārabbha ekaccasassatikā ekaccaasassatikā ekaccaṁ sassataṁ ekaccaṁ asassataṁ attānañca lokañca paññapenti catūhi vatthūhi? 
- **EN**: And what are the four grounds on which they rely? 

## dn1:2.2.1  `bj7.30, cck9.23, km14.44, lv1.43, ms6D_75, msdiv39, pts-cs2.2`
- **PLI**: Hoti kho so, bhikkhave, samayo, yaṁ kadāci karahaci dīghassa addhuno accayena ayaṁ loko saṁvaṭṭati. 
- **EN**: There comes a time when, after a very long period has passed, this cosmos contracts. 
- **NOTE**: This is the end of an eon. It might be compared with what the physicists call the “big crunch”. 

## dn1:2.2.2
- **PLI**: Saṁvaṭṭamāne loke yebhuyyena sattā ābhassarasaṁvattanikā honti. 
- **EN**: As the cosmos contracts, sentient beings are mostly headed for the realm of sublime luminosity. 
- **NOTE**: The human and similar realms are destroyed in the conflagration at the end of the universe, but sentient beings are sustained by the power of their past kamma. | The “realm of sublime radiance” is a Brahmā heaven corresponding to the second <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. The Sanskrit spelling ₋ābhāsvara<i lang='pi' translate='no'> suggests the derivation </i>ābhā<i lang='pi' translate='no'> + </i>vara<i lang='pi' translate='no'>, “sublime radiance”. The commentary derives this (and related forms </i>bhassara<i lang='pi' translate='no'> and </i>pabhassara<i lang='pi' translate='no'>) from </i>sara<i lang='pi' translate='no'> (“stream”), but this is ruled out by the Sanskrit, which has </i>vara<i lang='pi' translate='no'> in all cases, whereas “stream” is </i>sara_. 

## dn1:2.2.3
- **PLI**: Te tattha honti manomayā pītibhakkhā sayaṁpabhā antalikkhacarā subhaṭṭhāyino, ciraṁ dīghamaddhānaṁ tiṭṭhanti. 
- **EN**: There they are mind-made, feeding on rapture, self-radiant, wandering in midair, steadily glorious, and they remain like that for a very long time. 
- **NOTE**: “Mind-made” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>manomaya</i>) beings are spontaneously born due to past kamma, not by sex. | “Rapture” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pīti</i>) is a joyous emotional response to pleasure, usually a spiritual sense of elevation or uplift in meditation. | “Self-lumious” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sayaṁpabhā</i>) as an external representation of the light of absorption through which they attained their exalted status. In the Rig Veda it is used of the divine light of the sun (1.52.9 <i lang='pi' translate='no'>svaścandram</i>; 6.64.4 <i lang='pi' translate='no'>svabhāna</i>) or the lightning (5.87.5 <i lang='pi' translate='no'>svarociṣa</i>; 1.82.2 <i lang='pi' translate='no'>svabhāna</i>). 

## dn1:2.3.1  `csp1ed6.17, csp2ed6.17, dr9.23, ms6D_76, msdiv40, pts-cs2.3`
- **PLI**: Hoti kho so, bhikkhave, samayo, yaṁ kadāci karahaci dīghassa addhuno accayena ayaṁ loko vivaṭṭati. 
- **EN**: There comes a time when, after a very long period has passed, this cosmos expands. 
- **NOTE**: This might be compared with the “big bang” of a cyclic universe. 

## dn1:2.3.2
- **PLI**: Vivaṭṭamāne loke suññaṁ brahmavimānaṁ pātubhavati. 
- **EN**: As it expands an empty mansion of divinity appears. 
- **NOTE**: The realms into which beings are reborn exist interdependently with the beings themselves. The different dimensions correspond with different kinds of kamma. 

## dn1:2.3.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho aññataro satto āyukkhayā vā puññakkhayā vā ābhassarakāyā cavitvā suññaṁ brahmavimānaṁ upapajjati. 
- **EN**: Then a certain sentient being—due to the running out of their life-span or merit—passes away from that host of luminous deities and is reborn in that empty mansion of divinity. 
- **NOTE**: That is, they pass from a world corresponding to the second <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> to one corresponding to the first <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. 

## dn1:2.3.4
- **PLI**: So tattha hoti manomayo pītibhakkho sayampabho antalikkhacaro subhaṭṭhāyī, ciraṁ dīghamaddhānaṁ tiṭṭhati. 
- **EN**: There they are mind-made, feeding on rapture, self-radiant, wandering in midair, steadily glorious, and they remain like that for a very long time. 

## dn1:2.4.1  `km14.45, lv1.44, ms6D_77, msdiv41, pts-cs2.4, pts-vp-pli1.18`
- **PLI**: Tassa tattha ekakassa dīgharattaṁ nivusitattā anabhirati paritassanā uppajjati: 
- **EN**: But after staying there all alone for a long time, they become dissatisfied and anxious: 
- **NOTE**: This passage echoes the creation myth in Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4. At 1.4.2, the self-created Divinity feels fearful and alone, and at 1.4.3 and 1.4.17 wishes for a partner. From a Buddhist point of view, this simply shows how even God is trapped by emotional attachments in the cycle of transmigration. 

## dn1:2.4.2
- **PLI**: ‘aho vata aññepi sattā itthattaṁ āgaccheyyun’ti. 
- **EN**: ‘Oh, if only other beings would come to this place.’ 

## dn1:2.4.3
- **PLI**: Atha aññepi sattā āyukkhayā vā puññakkhayā vā ābhassarakāyā cavitvā brahmavimānaṁ upapajjanti tassa sattassa sahabyataṁ. 
- **EN**: Then other sentient beings—due to the running out of their life-span or merit—pass away from that host of luminous deities and are reborn in that mansion of divinity in company with that being. 
- **NOTE**: These beings are reborn according to their own kamma, and it is just a coincidence that they appear after the first being made their wish. 

## dn1:2.4.4
- **PLI**: Tepi tattha honti manomayā pītibhakkhā sayaṁpabhā antalikkhacarā subhaṭṭhāyino, ciraṁ dīghamaddhānaṁ tiṭṭhanti. 
- **EN**: There they too are mind-made, feeding on rapture, self-radiant, wandering in midair, steadily glorious, and they remain like that for a very long time.

This is not due to their conduct but to the circumstances of their creation.
