## dn1:1.21.1  `bj7.16, cck9.12, csp1ed6.9, csp2ed6.9, dr9.12, km14.22, km14.23, lv1.22, mc9.10, ms6D_48, msdiv21, pts-cs1.21, pts-vp-pli1.9, sya9.12`
- **PLI**: ‘Yathā vā paneke bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā saddhādeyyāni bhojanāni bhuñjitvā te evarūpāya tiracchānavijjāya micchājīvena jīvitaṁ kappenti, 
- **EN**: ‘There are some ascetics and brahmins who, while enjoying food given in faith, still earn a living by low lore, by wrong livelihood. 
- **NOTE**: This section focuses on practices that are wrong livelihood for a mendicant, though not for lay people. The Vinaya explains “low lore” as whatever is non-Buddhist or useless (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bi-vb-pc49/en/sujato'>Bi Pc 49</a>), while the commentary says it leads not to emancipation but to heaven. Most of the items in this and the following sections are detailed in later astrological texts such as Varāhamihira’s Bṛhatsaṁhitā. There are too many correspondences to cite, so I’ll only mention those that impact the translation. 

## dn1:1.21.2
- **PLI**: seyyathidaṁ—aṅgaṁ nimittaṁ uppātaṁ supinaṁ lakkhaṇaṁ mūsikacchinnaṁ aggihomaṁ dabbihomaṁ thusahomaṁ kaṇahomaṁ taṇḍulahomaṁ sappihomaṁ telahomaṁ mukhahomaṁ lohitahomaṁ aṅgavijjā vatthuvijjā khattavijjā sivavijjā bhūtavijjā bhūrivijjā ahivijjā visavijjā vicchikavijjā mūsikavijjā sakuṇavijjā vāyasavijjā pakkajjhānaṁ saraparittāṇaṁ migacakkaṁ 
- **EN**: This includes such fields as augury, omenology, divining portents, interpreting dreams, divining features of men and women, divining holes in cloth gnawed by mice, fire offerings, ladle offerings, offerings of husks, rice powder, rice, ghee, or oil; offerings from the mouth, blood sacrifices, limb-reading; geomancy for building sites, fields, and cemeteries; exorcisms, earth magic, snake charming, poisons; the lore of the scorpion, the rat, the bird, and the crow; prophesying lifespan, chanting for protection, and divining omens from wild animals. 
- **NOTE**: The list has both <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅga</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅgavijjā</i>, and it’s hard to disambiguate them; the commentary tries, unpersuasively. Bṛhatsaṁhitā 50 has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅgavidyā</i>, which is various kinds of “limb-reading” (compare modern “palmistry”) but no equivalent to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅga</i>. Perhaps it is a misspelling of the less-familiar <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aṅka</i>, “mark, sign”, hence “augury”. | “Portents” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>uppāta</i> are discussed at Bṛhatsaṁhitā 45. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Lakkhaṇa</i> is detailed at Bṛhatsaṁhitā 67–69 as a thorough assessment of the features of men and women, including physical marks, character, voice, gait, and so on. | Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattavijjā</i> (“political science”) per variant as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khettavijjā</i> (“geomancy”). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sara</i> in <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saraparitta</i> means “sound” not “arrow”; compare with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sarabhañña</i> “chanting”. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Migacakka</i> is explained in the commentary, supported by Bṛhatsaṁhitā, as interpretation of the cries and behaviors of wild animals. Here the suffix <i lang='pi' translate='no'>-cakka</i> refers to the field of study. See also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mil5.3.7/en/sujato#6.1'>Mil 5.3.7:6.1</a>: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sācakkaṁ migacakkaṁ antaracakkaṁ</i> (“divining omens from dogs, wild animals, and the directions around”), terms which are also found at Bṛhatsaṁhitā 2.17.
