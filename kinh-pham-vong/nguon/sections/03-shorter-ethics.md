## dn1:1.7.0.2
- **PLI**: 2.1. Cūḷasīla 
- **EN**: 2.1. The Shorter Section on Ethics 

## dn1:1.7.1  `dr9.5, lv1.8, mc9.4, ms6D_9, msdiv7, pts-cs1.7, sya9.5`
- **PLI**: Appamattakaṁ kho panetaṁ, bhikkhave, oramattakaṁ sīlamattakaṁ, yena puthujjano tathāgatassa vaṇṇaṁ vadamāno vadeyya. 
- **EN**: When an ordinary person speaks praise of the Realized One, they speak only of trivial, trifling details of mere ethics. 
- **NOTE**: Ethics (or morality or virtue, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sīla</i>) is important, but it pales in comparison with the higher dimensions of the Buddha’s path. For an example of this kind of praise see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#8.1'>MN 77:8.1</a>. | The Buddha often referred to himself as “the Realized One” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tathāgata</i>). 

## dn1:1.7.2
- **PLI**: Katamañca taṁ, bhikkhave, appamattakaṁ oramattakaṁ sīlamattakaṁ, yena puthujjano tathāgatassa vaṇṇaṁ vadamāno vadeyya? 
- **EN**: And what are the trivial, trifling details of mere ethics that an ordinary person speaks of? 
- **NOTE**: Here the Buddha lays out in detail the ethical conduct for mendicant followers. 

## dn1:1.8.1  `csp1ed6.4, csp2ed6.4, km14.8, ms6D_10, msdiv8, pts-cs1.8, pts-vp-pli1.4, vri1.4`
- **PLI**: ‘Pāṇātipātaṁ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo nihitadaṇḍo, nihitasattho, lajjī, dayāpanno, sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī viharatī’ti—
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama has given up killing living creatures. He has renounced the rod and the sword. He’s scrupulous and kind, living full of sympathy for all living beings.’ 
- **NOTE**: The first and most important precept. It is not just the negative injunction to avoid killing, but also the positive injunction to have compassion for all creatures. | The Buddha is called “the ascetic Gotama” by non-Buddhists. 

## dn1:1.8.2
- **PLI**: iti vā hi, bhikkhave, puthujjano tathāgatassa vaṇṇaṁ vadamāno vadeyya. 
- **EN**: Such is an ordinary person’s praise of the Realized One. 
- **NOTE**: An “ordinary person” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>puthujjana</i>) is anyone who has not, at minimum, entered the path to stream-entry.

## dn1:1.8.5  `lv1.9, ms6D_12`
- **PLI**: ‘Abrahmacariyaṁ pahāya brahmacārī samaṇo gotamo ārācārī virato methunā gāmadhammā’ti—
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama has given up unchastity. He is chaste, set apart, refraining from the vulgar act of sex.’ 
- **NOTE**: “Chastity” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmacariya</i>, literally “divine conduct”. Here it is used in the narrow sense of refraining from sex, but more commonly it has a broader sense of “spiritual life” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn42.7/en/sujato#2.6'>SN 42.7:2.6</a>).

## dn1:1.9.1  `km14.9, ms6D_13, msdiv9, pts-cs1.9`
- **PLI**: ‘Musāvādaṁ pahāya musāvādā paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo saccavādī saccasandho theto paccayiko avisaṁvādako lokassā’ti—
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama has given up lying. He speaks the truth and sticks to the truth. He’s honest and dependable, and doesn’t trick the world with his words.’ 
- **NOTE**: This is the first of the four kinds of right speech. Just as the precept of not killing implies the positive injunction to live with compassion, the precepts on speech enjoin a positive and constructive use of speech.

## dn1:1.9.3  `bj7.8, cck9.6, dr9.6, lv1.10, ms6D_14, sya9.6`
- **PLI**: ‘Pisuṇaṁ vācaṁ pahāya pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo, ito sutvā na amutra akkhātā imesaṁ bhedāya, amutra vā sutvā na imesaṁ akkhātā amūsaṁ bhedāya. Iti bhinnānaṁ vā sandhātā, sahitānaṁ vā anuppadātā samaggārāmo samaggarato samagganandī samaggakaraṇiṁ vācaṁ bhāsitā’ti—
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama has given up backbiting. He doesn’t repeat in one place what he heard in another so as to divide people against each other. Instead, he reconciles those who are divided and support those who are united, delighting in harmony, loving harmony, speaking words that promote harmony.’ 
- **NOTE**: “Harmony” (or “unanimity”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samagga</i>) does not excuse untrue, bigoted, or otherwise harmful speech. True harmony is only achieved in the presence of the Dhamma.

## dn1:1.9.7  `km14.10, ms6D_16, pts-vp-pli1.5`
- **PLI**: ‘Samphappalāpaṁ pahāya samphappalāpā paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo kālavādī bhūtavādī atthavādī dhammavādī vinayavādī, nidhānavatiṁ vācaṁ bhāsitā kālena sāpadesaṁ pariyantavatiṁ atthasaṁhitan’ti—
- **EN**: ‘The ascetic Gotama has given up talking nonsense. His words are timely, true, and meaningful, in line with the teaching and training. He says things at the right time which are valuable, reasonable, succinct, and beneficial.’ 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Attha</i> is a polyvalent term, here taking the senses  “meaningful” and “beneficial”. Elsewhere it means “goal”, “need”, “purpose”, “lawsuit”, or “ending”, and the senses are not always easy to untangle.

## dn1:1.10.3  `lv1.11, ms6D_18`
- **PLI**: ‘Ekabhattiko samaṇo gotamo rattūparato virato vikālabhojanā …. 
- **EN**: ‘He eats in one part of the day, abstaining from eating at night and food at the wrong time.’ 
- **NOTE**: From <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn66/en/sujato#6.4'>MN 66:6.4</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn70/en/sujato#4.8'>MN 70:4.8</a> we can see that “at night” means after dark, while “at the wrong time” means in the afternoon. More explicitly, these are the “wrong time at night” and the “wrong time in the day”, in which case they are both the “wrong time”. 

## dn1:1.10.4  `ms6D_19`
- **PLI**: Naccagītavāditavisūkadassanā paṭivirato samaṇo gotamo …. 
- **EN**: ‘He refrains from seeing shows of dancing, singing, and music.’ 
- **NOTE**: Such sensual entertainments distract and excite the mind. This and the next three precepts encourage peace of mind for meditation.
