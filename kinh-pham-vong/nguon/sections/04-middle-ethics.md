## dn1:1.13.2
- **PLI**: seyyathidaṁ—naccaṁ gītaṁ vāditaṁ pekkhaṁ akkhānaṁ pāṇissaraṁ vetāḷaṁ kumbhathūṇaṁ sobhanakaṁ caṇḍālaṁ vaṁsaṁ dhovanaṁ hatthiyuddhaṁ assayuddhaṁ mahiṁsayuddhaṁ usabhayuddhaṁ ajayuddhaṁ meṇḍayuddhaṁ kukkuṭayuddhaṁ vaṭṭakayuddhaṁ daṇḍayuddhaṁ muṭṭhiyuddhaṁ nibbuddhaṁ uyyodhikaṁ balaggaṁ senābyūhaṁ anīkadassanaṁ 
- **EN**: This includes such things as dancing, singing, music, performances, and storytelling; clapping, gongs, and kettledrums; beauty pageants; pole-acrobatics and bone-washing displays of the corpse-workers; battles of elephants, horses, buffaloes, bulls, goats, rams, chickens, and quails; staff-fights, boxing, and wrestling; combat, roll calls of the armed forces, battle-formations, and regimental reviews. 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sobhanaka</i> (“beauty pageant”) is explained by the commentary as the movement (or “sprinkling”) of dancers, or their beautification and painting. The PTS reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sobha-nagarakaṁ</i>, supported by an unrelated reference to a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gandhabba</i> city of that name, is spurious. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Caṇḍālaṁ vaṁsaṁ dhovanaṁ</i> should be a compound, as shown by the prose to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja498/en/sujato'>Ja 498</a>, where it is a performance by corpse-workers (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>caṇḍāla</i>) in Ujjenī. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vaṁsa</i> is the bamboo used by <i lang='pi' translate='no'>caṇḍāla</i> acrobats (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.19/en/sujato'>SN 47.19</a>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dhovana</i> is referred by the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.107/en/sujato'>AN 10.107</a>, where it is a southern ceremony accompanied by drink and dance. The commentaries to AN and DN say it was the ritual washing of the bones of the buried dead after the decomposition of the flesh. Such “second funeral” rites have been observed world-wide. From the Jātaka it appears that the tradition had declined to a mere display for passers-by, perhaps featuring naked tribal girls. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Uyodhika</i> is sometimes said to be “sham fights”, but at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.30/en/sujato'>AN 10.30</a> it is not a sham. And the definition at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc50/en/sujato'>Bu Pc 50</a> says “where strife is seen”.

## dn1:1.17.1  `bj7.14, km14.19, lv1.18, ms6D_43, msdiv17, pts-cs1.17, pts-vp-pli1.8`
- **PLI**: ‘Yathā vā paneke bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā saddhādeyyāni bhojanāni bhuñjitvā te evarūpaṁ tiracchānakathaṁ anuyuttā viharanti, 
- **EN**: ‘There are some ascetics and brahmins who, while enjoying food given in faith, still engage in low talk. This includes such topics as 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Tiracchānakathā</i> literally means “animal talk”. The Pali word for animal, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tiracchāna</i> has the sense of “moving horizontally”, and “low talk” is that which does not elevate. 

## dn1:1.17.2

- **EN**: talk about kings, bandits, and chief ministers; talk about armies, threats, and wars; talk about food, drink, clothes, and beds; talk about garlands and fragrances; talk about family, vehicles, villages, towns, cities, and countries; talk about women and heroes; talk at the street market and the place for water pots; talk about the departed; motley talk; tales of land and sea; and talk about being reborn in this or that place.

Indian religious texts are full of discussions about different heavens and hells. | The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>visikhā</i> was a street where gold was exchanged and diversions were to be found, i.e. a “street market” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn31/en/sujato#9.1'>DN 31:9.1</a>, Arthaśāstra 1.1.4c, 2.13.2). | The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumbhaṭṭhāna</i> was a place where water pots were set up for public drinking water.
