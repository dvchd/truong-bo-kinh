## dn1:2.23.1  `bj7.44, dr9.32, ms6D_104, msdiv61, pts-cs2.23`
- **PLI**: Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā amarāvikkhepikā, tattha tattha pañhaṁ puṭṭhā samānā vācāvikkhepaṁ āpajjanti amarāvikkhepaṁ catūhi vatthūhi. 
- **EN**: There are some ascetics and brahmins who are endless flip-floppers. Whenever they’re asked a question, they resort to verbal flip-flops and endless flip-flops on four grounds. 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vikkhepa</i> is “ﬂip-flopping”. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Amarā</i> is explained in the commentary as either “undying” or “eel-like”. However, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>amarā</i> in the sense of “eel” is found only in the commentary to this term so is probably spurious. The feminine form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>amarā</i> is attested in Sanskrit (eg. Manu 2.148). See also note to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn4.1/en/sujato#5.2'>SN 4.1:5.2</a>.

- **EN**: So from fear and disgust with false speech they avoid stating whether something is skillful or unskillful. Whenever they’re asked a question, they resort to verbal flip-flops and endless flip-flops: 
- **NOTE**: Despite their dullness, they have a genuine sense of conscience and wish to avoid breaking precepts. 

## dn1:2.24.8
- **PLI**: ‘evantipi me no; tathātipi me no; aññathātipi me no; notipi me no; no notipi me no’ti. 
- **EN**: ‘I don’t say it’s like this. I don’t say it’s like that. I don’t say it’s otherwise. I don’t say it’s not so. And I don’t deny it’s not so.’ 
- **NOTE**: A wise teacher avoids making pronouncements about what they do not understand, but these teachers use this as a cover to hide the fact that they do not understand anything.

## dn1:2.27.4

- **EN**: ‘Suppose you were to ask me whether there is another world. If I believed that to be the case, I would say so. 
- **NOTE**: Here begins a series of four tetrads that are commonly encountered in the suttas. The first is the belief in an afterlife. 

## dn1:2.27.5
- **PLI**: ‘evantipi me no, tathātipi me no, aññathātipi me no, notipi me no, no notipi me no’ti. 
- **EN**: But I don’t say it’s like this. I don’t say it’s like that. I don’t say it’s otherwise. I don’t say it’s not so. And I don’t deny it’s not so. 

## dn1:2.27.6
- **PLI**: ‘Natthi paro loko …pe… 
- **EN**: Suppose you were to ask me whether there is no other world … 
- **NOTE**: The denial of an afterlife. 

## dn1:2.27.7
- **PLI**: ‘atthi ca natthi ca paro loko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there both is and is not another world … 
- **NOTE**: This could include a belief that eternal life is offered only to adherents of a certain religion. 

## dn1:2.27.8
- **PLI**: ‘nevatthi na natthi paro loko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there neither is nor is not another world … 
- **NOTE**: This could include the idea that our intrinsic nature is one with the cosmos, and our separation from that infinitude in this life is only a veil of delusion. Thus there is no other world, because all worlds are this world, but it is also not the case that there is nothing after death. 

## dn1:2.27.9
- **PLI**: ‘atthi sattā opapātikā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there are beings who are reborn spontaneously … 
- **NOTE**: This is beings such as the gods or various ghosts and spirits, which are not born organically. 

## dn1:2.27.10
- **PLI**: ‘natthi sattā opapātikā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there are not beings who are reborn spontaneously … 
- **NOTE**: This denies the existence of such beings. Not everyone in ancient India believed in the various orders of beings. 

## dn1:2.27.11
- **PLI**: ‘atthi ca natthi ca sattā opapātikā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there both are and are not beings who are reborn spontaneously … 
- **NOTE**: The belief that beings are both spontaneously born and organically born. Perhaps this denies that such a distinction can be made clearly, because both kinds of birth take place within the same order of beings. 

## dn1:2.27.12
- **PLI**: ‘nevatthi na natthi sattā opapātikā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there neither are nor are not beings who are reborn spontaneously … 
- **NOTE**: Beings are reborn in other ways. 

## dn1:2.27.13
- **PLI**: ‘atthi sukatadukkaṭānaṁ kammānaṁ phalaṁ vipāko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there is fruit and result of good and bad deeds … 
- **NOTE**: This is the standard Buddhist view of kamma, shared with some, but not all, of the other Indian religions of the time. 

## dn1:2.27.14
- **PLI**: ‘natthi sukatadukkaṭānaṁ kammānaṁ phalaṁ vipāko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there is no fruit and result of good and bad deeds … 
- **NOTE**: Doing good or bad has no result; moral nihilism. 

## dn1:2.27.15
- **PLI**: ‘atthi ca natthi ca sukatadukkaṭānaṁ kammānaṁ phalaṁ vipāko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there both is and is not fruit and result of good and bad deeds … 
- **NOTE**: Sometimes good and bad deeds have results, other times not. 

## dn1:2.27.16
- **PLI**: ‘nevatthi na natthi sukatadukkaṭānaṁ kammānaṁ phalaṁ vipāko …pe… 
- **EN**: whether there neither is nor is not fruit and result of good and bad deeds … 
- **NOTE**: The results of actions are too subtle to be described as good or bad. 

## dn1:2.27.17
- **PLI**: ‘hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether a realized one still exists after death … 
- **NOTE**: An awakened one, whether the Buddha or anyone else, exists after death, for example in an eternal state of Nirvana. 

## dn1:2.27.18
- **PLI**: ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether a realized one no longer exists after death … 
- **NOTE**: A sage ceases to exist at the time of death. This assumes there is an enduring “self” that is done away with. 

## dn1:2.27.19
- **PLI**: ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā …pe… 
- **EN**: whether a realized one both still exists and no longer exists after death … 
- **NOTE**: For example, their body does not exist but their mind does. 

## dn1:2.27.20
- **PLI**: ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā’ti iti ce maṁ pucchasi, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā’ti iti ce me assa, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṁ maraṇā’ti iti te naṁ byākareyyaṁ, 
- **EN**: whether a realized one neither still exists nor no longer exists after death. If I believed that to be the case, I would say so. 
- **NOTE**: A sage persists in a subtle state that cannot be characterized in terms of existence or non-existence. 

## dn1:2.27.21
- **PLI**: ‘evantipi me no, tathātipi me no, aññathātipi me no, notipi me no, no notipi me no’ti. 
- **EN**: But I don’t say it’s like this. I don’t say it’s like that. I don’t say it’s otherwise. I don’t say it’s not so. And I don’t deny it’s not so.’
