# Muc 6.1 — The Buddha’s Last Words  (34 đoạn)

## dn16:6.1.0
- **PLI**: 35. Tathāgatapacchimavācā
- **EN**: 35. The Buddha’s Last Words

## dn16:6.1.1  `bj8.242, cck10.192, csp1ed7.127, csp2ed7.127, mc10.135, ms7D_484, msdiv216, pts-cs6.1, pts-vp-pli2.154`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā āyasmantaṁ ānandaṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: Then the Buddha addressed Venerable Ānanda:

## dn16:6.1.2
- **PLI**: “siyā kho panānanda, tumhākaṁ evamassa:
- **EN**: “Now, Ānanda, some of you might think:

## dn16:6.1.3
- **PLI**: ‘atītasatthukaṁ pāvacanaṁ, natthi no satthā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The teacher’s dispensation has passed. Now we have no Teacher.’

## dn16:6.1.4
- **PLI**: Na kho panetaṁ, ānanda, evaṁ daṭṭhabbaṁ.
- **EN**: But you should not see it like this.

## dn16:6.1.5
- **PLI**: Yo vo, ānanda, mayā dhammo ca vinayo ca desito paññatto, so vo mamaccayena satthā.
- **EN**: The teaching and training that I have taught and pointed out for you shall be your Teacher after my passing.
- **NOTE**: This is similar to the idea of the Four Great references, and sets the scene for the First Council at which the teachings were recited. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Paññatto</i> here means “pointed out” rather than “laid down”, as can be seen from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn9/en/sujato#33.21'>DN 9:33.21</a>, where the same phrase refers to the four noble truths.

## dn16:6.2.1  `pts-cs6.2`
- **PLI**: Yathā kho panānanda, etarahi bhikkhū aññamaññaṁ āvusovādena samudācaranti, na kho mamaccayena evaṁ samudācaritabbaṁ.
- **EN**: After my passing, mendicants ought not address each other as ‘reverend’, as they do today.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Āvuso</i> is from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyu</i> (“age”) and thus has a respectful sense and does not mean “friend” as it is often translated. Like <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhante</i>, it is an indeclinable vocative of address that may be used with or without the name (see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn5/en/sujato#31.2'>MN 5:31.2</a>).

## dn16:6.2.2
- **PLI**: Theratarena, ānanda, bhikkhunā navakataro bhikkhu nāmena vā gottena vā āvusovādena vā samudācaritabbo.
- **EN**: A more senior mendicant ought to address a more junior mendicant by name or clan, or by saying ‘reverend’.

## dn16:6.2.3
- **PLI**: Navakatarena bhikkhunā therataro bhikkhu ‘bhante’ti vā ‘āyasmā’ti vā samudācaritabbo.
- **EN**: A more junior mendicant ought to address a more senior mendicant using ‘sir’ or ‘venerable’.
- **NOTE**: I render <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhante</i> as “sir” when it stands alone and “honorable” when it prefixes a name. | Unlike <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhante</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyasmā</i> (“venerable”) is declinable, so it is used in parts of speech other than direct address. It is from the same root as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āvuso</i> but more Sanskritic and hence elevated in tone. The difference between <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyasmā</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āvuso</i> is like that between the English “governor” and “guv’nor” or “my lord” and “m’lud”.

## dn16:6.3.1  `pts-cs6.3`
- **PLI**: Ākaṅkhamāno, ānanda, saṅgho mamaccayena khuddānukhuddakāni sikkhāpadāni samūhanatu.
- **EN**: If it wishes, after my passing the Saṅgha may abolish the lesser and minor training rules.
- **NOTE**: These are not defined here, and the senior monks at the First Council were unable to agree on them (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd21/en/sujato#1.9.3'>Kd 21:1.9.3</a>). Nonetheless, the Pali Vinaya consistently labels the Pācittiya rules as “lesser” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>khuddaka</i>; <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc92/en/sujato#2.2.22'>Bu Pc 92:2.2.22</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bi-vb-pc96/en/sujato#2.2.22'>Bi Pc 96:2.2.22</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-pvr1.1/en/sujato#219.3'>Pvr 1.1:219.3</a>), which would make the Pātidesanīyas “minor” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukhuddaka</i>). The Sekhiya rules are also “minor”, but they were not at this point reckoned among the training rules for recitation.

## dn16:6.4.1  `pts-cs6.4`
- **PLI**: Channassa, ānanda, bhikkhuno mamaccayena brahmadaṇḍo dātabbo”ti.
- **EN**: After my passing, give the divine punishment to the mendicant Channa.”
- **NOTE**: “Divine punishment” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadaṇḍa</i>. Channa features often in the Vinaya as a monk who refuses correction and acts disrespectfully. The Saṅgha had already imposed an act of “ejection” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ukkhepanīyakamma</i>) on him due to his persistent bad behavior, but that was still not enough (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#25.1.1'>Kd 1:25.1.1</a>). The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadaṇḍa</i> was imposed at the First Council (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#1.12.1'>Kd 1:1.12.1</a>), upon which Channa finally saw the error of his ways. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Brahmadaṇḍa</i> is encountered in a different sense at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.23.21'>DN 3:1.23.21</a>.

## dn16:6.4.2
- **PLI**: “Katamo pana, bhante, brahmadaṇḍo”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, what is the divine punishment?”

## dn16:6.4.3
- **PLI**: “Channo, ānanda, bhikkhu yaṁ iccheyya, taṁ vadeyya.
- **EN**: “Channa may say what he likes,

## dn16:6.4.4
- **PLI**: So bhikkhūhi neva vattabbo, na ovaditabbo, na anusāsitabbo”ti.
- **EN**: but the mendicants should not correct, advise, or instruct him.”
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vattabba</i> in such contexts means “advise, correct” rather than more generally “speak to”. Thus the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadaṇḍa</i> is not the “silent treatment”.

## dn16:6.5.1  `bj8.244, cck10.193, dr10.180, ms7D_485, msdiv217, ndp7.119, pts-cs6.5, pts-vp-pli2.155, sya10.179, vri2.116`

## dn16:6.5.2

- **EN**: “Perhaps even a single mendicant has doubt or uncertainty regarding the Buddha, the teaching, the Saṅgha, the path, or the practice. So ask, mendicants! Don’t regret it later, thinking:

## dn16:6.5.3

- **EN**: ‘We were in the Teacher’s presence and we weren’t able to ask the Buddha a question.’”

## dn16:6.5.4

- **EN**: When this was said, the mendicants kept silent.

## dn16:6.6.9
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then Venerable Ānanda said to the Buddha,

## dn16:6.6.10
- **PLI**: “acchariyaṁ, bhante, abbhutaṁ, bhante, evaṁ pasanno ahaṁ, bhante, imasmiṁ bhikkhusaṅghe, ‘natthi ekabhikkhussāpi kaṅkhā vā vimati vā buddhe vā dhamme vā saṅghe vā magge vā paṭipadāya vā’”ti.
- **EN**: “It’s incredible, sir, it’s amazing! I am quite confident that there is not even a single mendicant in this Saṅgha who has doubt or uncertainty regarding the Buddha, the teaching, the Saṅgha, the path, or the practice.”

## dn16:6.6.11
- **PLI**: “Pasādā kho tvaṁ, ānanda, vadesi, ñāṇameva hettha, ānanda, tathāgatassa. Natthi imasmiṁ bhikkhusaṅghe ekabhikkhussāpi kaṅkhā vā vimati vā buddhe vā dhamme vā saṅghe vā magge vā paṭipadāya vā.
- **EN**: “Ānanda, you speak out of faith. But the Realized One knows that there is not even a single mendicant in this Saṅgha who has doubt or uncertainty regarding the Buddha, the teaching, the Saṅgha, the path, or the practice.

## dn16:6.6.12
- **PLI**: Imesañhi, ānanda, pañcannaṁ bhikkhusatānaṁ yo pacchimako bhikkhu, so sotāpanno avinipātadhammo niyato sambodhiparāyaṇo”ti.
- **EN**: Even the last of these five hundred mendicants is a stream-enterer, not liable to be reborn in the underworld, assured, destined for awakening.”
