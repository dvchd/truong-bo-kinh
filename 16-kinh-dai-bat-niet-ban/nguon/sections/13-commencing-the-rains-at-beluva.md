## dn16:2.22.1  `pts-cs2.22`

## dn16:2.22.2

## dn16:2.23.1  `cck10.126, csp1ed7.85, csp2ed7.85, dr10.119, ms7D_350, msdiv164, pts-cs2.23, sya10.118, vri2.78`

- **EN**: After the Buddha had commenced the rainy season residence, he fell severely ill, struck by dreadful pains, close to death.

## dn16:2.23.2
- **PLI**: Tā sudaṁ bhagavā sato sampajāno adhivāsesi avihaññamāno.
- **EN**: But he endured unbothered, with mindfulness and situational awareness.

## dn16:2.23.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavato etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then it occurred to the Buddha,

## dn16:2.23.4
- **PLI**: “na kho metaṁ patirūpaṁ, yvāhaṁ anāmantetvā upaṭṭhāke anapaloketvā bhikkhusaṅghaṁ parinibbāyeyyaṁ.
- **EN**: “It would not be appropriate for me to be fully extinguished before informing my supporters and taking leave of the mendicant Saṅgha.
- **NOTE**: Here <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upaṭṭhāka</i> refers to the lay devotees, who the Buddha informs below. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Apaloketi</i> (“take leave”) also has a literal sense of “glance back”; both senses are found in the sutta (see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#4.1.2'>DN 16:4.1.2</a>).

## dn16:2.23.5
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ imaṁ ābādhaṁ vīriyena paṭipaṇāmetvā jīvitasaṅkhāraṁ adhiṭṭhāya vihareyyan”ti.
- **EN**: Why don’t I forcefully suppress this illness, stabilize the life force, and live on?”
- **NOTE**: “Life force” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jīvitasaṅkhāra</i> whereas below we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyusaṅkhāra</i> (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.10.1'>DN 16:3.10.1</a>). They are evidently synonyms; <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ps1.6/en/sujato#4.3'>Ps 1.6:4.3</a> refers to the three <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jīvitasaṅkhāras</i> from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#24.2'>MN 43:24.2</a>, but there the text has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āyusaṅkhāra</i>. The sense is not “will to live” but “life force”, i.e. the vital energy that sustains life (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn20.6/en/sujato#3.2'>SN 20.6:3.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#23.1'>MN 43:23.1</a>). This leans on the old use of Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samas kurva</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁskāra</i> for the “reviving” or “rejuvenation” of the god Pajāpati when he is depleted by the act of creation, like a fire run low or a man after sex (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 7.2.1.5 <em>et passim</em>).

## dn16:2.24.1  `pts-cs2.24`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā taṁ ābādhaṁ vīriyena paṭipaṇāmetvā jīvitasaṅkhāraṁ adhiṭṭhāya vihāsi.
- **EN**: So that is what he did.

## dn16:2.24.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavato so ābādho paṭippassambhi.
- **EN**: Then the Buddha’s illness died down.

## dn16:2.24.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā gilānā vuṭṭhito aciravuṭṭhito gelaññā vihārā nikkhamma vihārapacchāyāyaṁ paññatte āsane nisīdi.
- **EN**: Soon after the Buddha had recovered from that sickness, he left his dwelling and sat in the shade of the porch on the seat spread out.

## dn16:2.24.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā ānando yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then Venerable Ānanda went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him,

## dn16:2.24.5
- **PLI**: “diṭṭho me, bhante, bhagavato phāsu; diṭṭhaṁ me, bhante, bhagavato khamanīyaṁ, api ca me, bhante, madhurakajāto viya kāyo, disāpi me na pakkhāyanti, dhammāpi maṁ na paṭibhanti bhagavato gelaññena.
- **EN**: “Sir, it’s fantastic that the Buddha is comfortable and well. Because when the Buddha was sick, my body felt like it was drugged. I was disorientated, and the teachings didn’t spring to mind.
- **NOTE**: This is the first indication of Ānanda’s fragile emotional state in the days to come. | Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṭṭhā</i> (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṣṭyā</i>), “fantastic, how fortunate”, per <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato#21.9'>DN 26:21.9</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja81/en/sujato#1.4'>Ja 81:1.4</a>.

## dn16:2.24.6
- **PLI**: Api ca me, bhante, ahosi kācideva assāsamattā:
- **EN**: Still, at least I was consoled by the thought that

## dn16:2.24.7
- **PLI**: ‘na tāva bhagavā parinibbāyissati, na yāva bhagavā bhikkhusaṅghaṁ ārabbha kiñcideva udāharatī’”ti.
- **EN**: the Buddha won’t be fully extinguished without bringing something up regarding the Saṅgha of mendicants.”
- **NOTE**: Previously the Buddha had spoken of “taking leave” of the Saṅgha, but here something more specific is meant. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Udāharati</i> is typically used when a previously-mentioned matter (often unpleasant) is “brought up”, as at the monastic procedures for confession or invitation to admonish (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#16.2.5'>Kd 1:16.2.5</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#2.1.9'>Kd 1:2.1.9</a>). Ānanda is implying that there is some unfinished disciplinary business that needs attention. Despite the Buddha’s response, he did in fact go on to make a number of statements and rulings.

## dn16:2.25.1  `bj8.158, cck10.127, cck10.128, csp1ed7.86, csp2ed7.86, dr10.120, mc10.91, ms7D_351, msdiv165, ndp7.80, pts-cs2.25, pts-vp-pli2.100, sya10.119`
- **PLI**: “Kiṁ panānanda, bhikkhusaṅgho mayi paccāsīsati?
- **EN**: “But what could the mendicant Saṅgha expect from me, Ānanda?
- **NOTE**: For forty-five years he had been teaching and supporting the community.

## dn16:2.25.2

- **EN**: I’ve taught the Dhamma without making any distinction between secret and public teachings.

## dn16:2.25.3

## dn16:2.25.4

- **EN**: If there’s anyone who thinks:

## dn16:2.25.5

- **EN**: ‘I shall lead the mendicant Saṅgha,’ or ‘the Saṅgha of mendicants is meant for me,’ let them bring something up regarding the Saṅgha.

## dn16:2.25.6
- **PLI**: Tathāgatassa kho, ānanda, na evaṁ hoti:
- **EN**: But the Realized One doesn’t think like this,

## dn16:2.25.7
- **PLI**: ‘ahaṁ bhikkhusaṅghaṁ pariharissāmī’ti vā ‘mamuddesiko bhikkhusaṅgho’ti vā.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)

## dn16:2.25.8
- **PLI**: Sakiṁ, ānanda, tathāgato bhikkhusaṅghaṁ ārabbha kiñcideva udāharissati.
- **EN**: so why should he bring something up regarding the Saṅgha?
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pariharissāmi</i> needs careful parsing with regard to tenses. The general meaning is to “carry about” or “maintain, nurture”. The Buddha elsewhere said that he “leads” the Saṅgha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato#25.6'>DN 26:25.6</a>: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pariharāmi</i>). When the Buddha was about to go on retreat, Moggallāna said that he and Sāriputta “shall lead” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pariharissāmi</i>), to which the Buddha said they “should lead” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn67/en/sujato#13.7'>MN 67:13.7</a>: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parihareyyaṁ</i>). Devadatta, on the other hand, ensured his downfall when he determined that he “shall lead” the Saṅgha, having taken over from the Buddha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.100/en/sujato#2.3'>AN 5.100:2.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#2.1.23'>Kd 1:2.1.23</a>). Thus the future tense indicates the determination to lead. But at this point, the Buddha’s mind is already set on letting go. He is not saying that no one should lead the Saṅgha, but that it is up to whoever leads it to address the issues.

- **EN**: I’m now old, elderly and senior. I’m advanced in years and have reached the final stage of life.

## dn16:2.25.10

- **EN**: I’m currently eighty years old.

## dn16:2.25.11
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, ānanda, jajjarasakaṭaṁ veṭhamissakena yāpeti;
- **EN**: Just as a decrepit old cart is kept going by a rope,
- **NOTE**: The reading and derivation of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>veṭhamissakena</i> are unclear. As a form of violence we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>veṭhamissena</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag2.12/en/sujato#1.1'>Thag 2.12:1.1</a>, notably also in instrumental. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Veṭha</i> means “twist, strap, turban”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Missa</i> means “mixed” or “plaited” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-ss2/en/sujato#2.1.21'>Bu Ss 2:2.1.21</a>). Thus it probably refers to a kind of strong twisted material used to tie or bind, i.e. rope.

## dn16:2.25.12
- **PLI**: evameva kho, ānanda, veṭhamissakena maññe tathāgatassa kāyo yāpeti.
- **EN**: in the same way, the Realized One’s body is kept going as if by a rope.
- **NOTE**: Compare Rig Veda 8.48.5: “As leather binds a chariot, soma knits my joints together.” Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.35 compares the labored breathing of one near death with the creaking of a heavily-laden cart. Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 13.2.7.8 says a handsome cart is well-finished with ropes. Indian carts were bound with ropes down to modern times. See too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.5/en/sujato#5.5'>SN 41.5:5.5</a> and note.

## dn16:2.25.13
- **PLI**: Yasmiṁ, ānanda, samaye tathāgato sabbanimittānaṁ amanasikārā ekaccānaṁ vedanānaṁ nirodhā animittaṁ cetosamādhiṁ upasampajja viharati, phāsutaro, ānanda, tasmiṁ samaye tathāgatassa kāyo hoti.
- **EN**: Sometimes the Realized One, not focusing on any signs, and with the cessation of certain feelings, enters and remains in the signless immersion of the heart. Only then does the Realized One’s body become more comfortable.
- **NOTE**: The suttas say little about this signless (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>animitta</i>) meditation. Its defining characteristic is that consciousness does not “follow after signs” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nimittānusāri viññāṇaṁ</i>, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.13/en/sujato#5.3'>AN 6.13:5.3</a>). This is explained in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn138/en/sujato#10.2'>MN 138:10.2</a> as not being distracted or affected by the features of sense impressions, as the “signs” are created by greed, hate, and delusion (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn43/en/sujato#37.1'>MN 43:37.1</a>). It is distinguished from the four kinds of mindfulness meditation (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.80/en/sujato#8.4'>SN 22.80:8.4</a>). The mental unification (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ekattaṁ</i>) based on this practice is listed after the form (and formless) <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhānas</i>, so it is very advanced. It is nonetheless a conditioned state (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn121/en/sujato#11.4'>MN 121:11.4</a>), so it is possible that a mendicant might fall from it and disrobe (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.60/en/sujato#8.10'>AN 6.60:8.10</a>). However it may also be used to describe the meditation of an arahant (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.7/en/sujato#6.12'>SN 41.7:6.12</a>). It is clear from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn121/en/sujato#10.5'>MN 121:10.5</a> that the six senses are still functioning, unlike in deep serenity meditations. It seems that in such a state, the Buddha was able to function normally while seeing through the pain in his body.

## dn16:2.26.1  `pts-cs2.26`
- **PLI**: Tasmātihānanda, attadīpā viharatha attasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā, dhammadīpā dhammasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā.
- **EN**: So Ānanda, live as your own island, your own refuge, with no other refuge. Let the teaching be your island and your refuge, with no other refuge.
