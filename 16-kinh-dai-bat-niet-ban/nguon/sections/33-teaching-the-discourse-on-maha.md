# Muc 5.17 — Teaching the Discourse on Mahāsudassana  (24 đoạn)

## dn16:5.17.0
- **PLI**: 32. Mahāsudassanasuttadesanā
- **EN**: 32. Teaching the Discourse on Mahāsudassana

## dn16:5.17.1  `cck10.183, dr10.171, mc10.129, ms7D_470, msdiv210, pts-cs5.17, sya10.170`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he said this, Venerable Ānanda said to the Buddha:

## dn16:5.17.2
- **PLI**: “mā, bhante, bhagavā imasmiṁ khuddakanagarake ujjaṅgalanagarake sākhānagarake parinibbāyi.
- **EN**: “Sir, please don’t be fully extinguished in this little hamlet, this jungle hamlet, this branch hamlet.

## dn16:5.17.3
- **PLI**: Santi, bhante, aññāni mahānagarāni, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: There are other great cities such as

## dn16:5.17.4
- **PLI**: campā rājagahaṁ sāvatthī sāketaṁ kosambī bārāṇasī;
- **EN**: Campā, Rājagaha, Sāvatthī, Sāketa, Kosambī, and Varanasi.
- **NOTE**: Campā, Rājagaha, Sāvatthī, and Kosambī were the capitals of Aṅga, Magadha, Kosala, and Vaccha respectively. Sāketa was the former capital of Kosala. Varanasi was formerly the capital of Kāsi, but at this time was contested by Kosala and Magadha, and had recently been won from Ajātasattu (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.14/en/sujato'>SN 3.14</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn3.15/en/sujato'>SN 3.15</a>).

## dn16:5.17.5
- **PLI**: ettha bhagavā parinibbāyatu.
- **EN**: Let the Buddha be fully extinguished there.

## dn16:5.17.6
- **PLI**: Ettha bahū khattiyamahāsālā, brāhmaṇamahāsālā gahapatimahāsālā tathāgate abhippasannā.
- **EN**: There are many well-to-do aristocrats, brahmins, and householders there who are devoted to the Buddha.

## dn16:5.17.7
- **PLI**: Te tathāgatassa sarīrapūjaṁ karissantī”ti
- **EN**: They will perform the rites of venerating the Realized One’s corpse.”

## dn16:5.17.8
- **PLI**: “mā hevaṁ, ānanda, avaca, mā hevaṁ, ānanda, avaca:
- **EN**: “Don’t say that Ānanda! Don’t say that

## dn16:5.17.9
- **PLI**: ‘khuddakanagarakaṁ ujjaṅgalanagarakaṁ sākhānagarakan’ti.
- **EN**: this is a little hamlet, a jungle hamlet, a branch hamlet.

## dn16:5.18.1  `bj8.230, dr10.172, ms7D_471, ndp7.114, pts-cs5.18, pts-vp-pli2.147, vri2.111`
- **PLI**: Bhūtapubbaṁ, ānanda, rājā mahāsudassano nāma ahosi cakkavattī dhammiko dhammarājā cāturanto vijitāvī janapadatthāvariyappatto sattaratanasamannāgato.
- **EN**: Once upon a time, there was a king named Mahāsudassana who was a wheel-turning monarch, a just and principled king. His dominion extended to all four sides, he achieved stability in the country, and he possessed the seven treasures.
- **NOTE**: This story is also found in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn17/en/sujato'>DN 17</a>. It seems that the Pali tradition extracted the story and greatly expanded it in an independent long discourse, whereas the Sanskrit tradition kept it at a more moderate length within the Mahāparinirvāṇasūtra itself.

## dn16:5.18.2
- **PLI**: Rañño, ānanda, mahāsudassanassa ayaṁ kusinārā kusāvatī nāma rājadhānī ahosi.
- **EN**: His capital was this Kusinārā, which at the time was named Kusāvatī.
- **NOTE**: Kusāvatī features in the Kusajātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja531/en/sujato'>Ja 531</a>), where the ugly but wise prince Kusa, son of the legendary Okkāka, wins the hand of the radiant Pabhāvatī. The Rāmāyaṇa also tells the story of a Kusa, son of Rāma, who ruled the city of Kusāvatī, although this city was located far to the south in the Vindhya ranges. Both stories are united by the detail that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kusa</i> grass, a prominent feature of Vedic ritual, ensured the kingly lineage.

## dn16:5.18.3
- **PLI**: puratthimena ca pacchimena ca dvādasayojanāni āyāmena; uttarena ca dakkhiṇena ca sattayojanāni vitthārena.
- **EN**: It stretched for twelve leagues from east to west, and seven leagues from north to south.
- **NOTE**: A “league” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>yojana</i>) is usually estimated at around fourteen kilometers (see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn24/en/sujato#14.11'>MN 24:14.11</a>). By way of comparison, even at its greatest extent under Ashoka, Pāṭaliputta was less than a league per side, so the dimensions of Kusāvatī here are strictly legendary.

## dn16:5.18.4
- **PLI**: Kusāvatī, ānanda, rājadhānī iddhā ceva ahosi phītā ca bahujanā ca ākiṇṇamanussā ca subhikkhā ca.
- **EN**: The royal capital of Kusāvatī was successful and prosperous, populous and full of people, with plenty of food.

## dn16:5.18.5
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, ānanda, devānaṁ āḷakamandā nāma rājadhānī iddhā ceva hoti phītā ca bahujanā ca ākiṇṇayakkhā ca subhikkhā ca;
- **EN**: It was just like Āḷakamandā, the royal capital of the gods, which is successful and prosperous, populous and full of spirits, with plenty of food.
- **NOTE**: According to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn32/en/sujato'>DN 32</a>, Āḷakamandā was one of the many cities of Kuvera in Uttarakuru.

## dn16:5.18.6
- **PLI**: evameva kho, ānanda, kusāvatī rājadhānī iddhā ceva ahosi phītā ca bahujanā ca ākiṇṇamanussā ca subhikkhā ca.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)

## dn16:5.18.7
- **PLI**: Kusāvatī, ānanda, rājadhānī dasahi saddehi avivittā ahosi divā ceva rattiñca, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: Kusāvatī was never free of ten sounds by day or night, namely:

## dn16:5.18.8
- **PLI**: hatthisaddena assasaddena rathasaddena bherisaddena mudiṅgasaddena vīṇāsaddena gītasaddena saṅkhasaddena sammasaddena pāṇitāḷasaddena ‘asnātha pivatha khādathā’ti dasamena saddena.
- **EN**: the sound of elephants, horses, chariots, drums, clay drums, arched harps, singing, horns, gongs, and handbells; and the cry: ‘Eat, drink, be merry!’ as the tenth.

## dn16:5.19.1  `cck10.184, csp1ed7.122, csp2ed7.122, ms7D_472, pts-cs5.19, sya10.171`
- **PLI**: Gaccha tvaṁ, ānanda, kusināraṁ pavisitvā kosinārakānaṁ mallānaṁ ārocehi:
- **EN**: Go, Ānanda, into Kusinārā and inform the Mallas:

## dn16:5.19.2
- **PLI**: ‘ajja kho, vāseṭṭhā, rattiyā pacchime yāme tathāgatassa parinibbānaṁ bhavissati.
- **EN**: ‘This very day, Vāseṭṭhas, in the last watch of the night, the Realized One will be fully extinguished.
- **NOTE**: Vāseṭṭha is a Vedic priestly clan. The Mallas adopted the name of their priest’s lineage, which was the normal custom for initiated <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khattiyas</i>. Other examples in the Pali are Saccaka who is called Aggivessana (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn35/en/sujato#4.2'>MN 35:4.2</a>), and the Buddha and his family who are called Gotama.

## dn16:5.19.3
- **PLI**: Abhikkamatha, vāseṭṭhā, abhikkamatha, vāseṭṭhā.
- **EN**: Come forth, Vāseṭṭhas! Come forth, Vāseṭṭhas!

## dn16:5.19.4
- **PLI**: Mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvattha—
- **EN**: Don’t regret it later, thinking:

## dn16:5.19.5
- **PLI**: amhākañca no gāmakkhette tathāgatassa parinibbānaṁ ahosi, na mayaṁ labhimhā pacchime kāle tathāgataṁ dassanāyā’”ti.
- **EN**: “The Realized One became fully extinguished in our own village district, but we didn’t get a chance to see him in his final hour.”’”

## dn16:5.19.6
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paṭissutvā nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya attadutiyo kusināraṁ pāvisi.
- **EN**: “Yes, sir,” replied Ānanda. Then he robed up and, taking his bowl and robe, entered Kusinārā with a companion.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Attadutiyo</i> “with self as second” is also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn146/en/sujato#4.6'>MN 146:4.6</a> where Nandaka visits the nuns. Sanskrit has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikṣuṇā paścācchramaṇena</i> (“with a mendicant as accompanying ascetic”). It was apparently evening (the “wrong time”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vikāla</i>), when it is inappropriate for a monk to be wandering the town alone.
