## dn16:5.4.1  `bj8.216, csp1ed7.115, csp2ed7.115, ms7D_449, msdiv200, pts-cs5.4, pts-vp-pli2.139, vri2.105`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā upavāṇo bhagavato purato ṭhito hoti bhagavantaṁ bījayamāno.
- **EN**: Now at that time Venerable Upavāṇa was standing in front of the Buddha fanning him.
- **NOTE**: Like Cundaka above, Upavāṇa shares the duties of an attendant with Ānanda; at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn29/en/sujato#41.1'>DN 29:41.1</a> he is also fanning the Buddha. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.166/en/sujato'>AN 5.166</a>, Ānanda goes to Upavāṇa for support when he feels he has disappointed the Buddha.

## dn16:5.6.1  `mc10.123, ms7D_451, msdiv201, pts-cs5.6`

## dn16:5.6.2
- **PLI**: “Santānanda, devatā ākāse pathavīsaññiniyo kese pakiriya kandanti, bāhā paggayha kandanti, chinnapātaṁ papatanti, āvaṭṭanti, vivaṭṭanti:
- **EN**: “There are, Ānanda, deities—both in space and on the earth—who are aware of the earth. With hair disheveled and arms raised, they fall down like their feet were chopped off, rolling back and forth, lamenting:
- **NOTE**: Elsewhere, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pathavīsaññī</i> refers to those who develop a form of meditation where they are “percipient of earth” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.6/en/sujato#1.2'>AN 10.6:1.2</a>). Here, however, it simply means those deities who are aware of events on the ground. | Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>chinnaṁpādaṁ viya papatanti</i>.

## dn16:5.6.3
- **PLI**: ‘atikhippaṁ bhagavā parinibbāyissati, atikhippaṁ sugato parinibbāyissati, atikhippaṁ cakkhuṁ loke antaradhāyissatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Too soon the Blessed One will be fully extinguished! Too soon the Holy One will be fully extinguished! Too soon the Eye of the World will vanish!’
- **NOTE**: The Buddha as “eye” evokes the common (eg. See <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.9/en/sujato#11.1'>Snp 3.9:11.1</a> = <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn98/en/sujato#7.23'>MN 98:7.23</a>. | Pali has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cakkhuṁ loke</i> (“eye in the world”), Sanskrit has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cakṣur lokasya</i> (“eye of the world”).

- **EN**: But the deities who are free of desire endure, mindful and aware, thinking:

## dn16:5.6.7
- **PLI**: ‘aniccā saṅkhārā, taṁ kutettha labbhā’”ti.
- **EN**: ‘Conditions are impermanent. How could it possibly be otherwise?’”
- **NOTE**: These two reactions—grief and equanimity—are depicted often in Buddhist art and narrative. They stimulated the two poles of development of the Buddhist community. The devotional tradition, feeling the need for an emotional connection with the Teacher, developed art, story, and doctrines to, as it were, keep him alive, resulting in the Jātakas and the Bodhisattva doctrine. A cooler, rational tradition developed a comprehensive system analyzing the impermanence of “conditions”, leading to the Abhidhamma.
