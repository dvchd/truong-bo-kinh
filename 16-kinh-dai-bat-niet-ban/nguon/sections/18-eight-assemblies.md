# Muc 3.21 — Eight Assemblies  (30 đoạn)

## dn16:3.21.0
- **PLI**: 17. Aṭṭhaparisā
- **EN**: 17. Eight Assemblies

## dn16:3.21.1  `cck10.138, csp1ed7.92, csp2ed7.92, dr10.129, mc10.98, ms7D_374, msdiv172, ndp7.86, pts-cs3.21, pts-vp-pli2.110, sya10.128`
- **PLI**: Aṭṭha kho imā, ānanda, parisā.
- **EN**: There are, Ānanda, these eight assemblies.
- **NOTE**: As at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.69/en/sujato'>AN 8.69</a>. The following series of “eight things” seems arbitrarily inserted here and breaks the flow of the narrative. They are absent from the Sanskrit Sarvāstivāda text edited by Waldschmidt, which goes directly to the conversation where Ānanda realizes that the Buddha is going to die.

## dn16:3.21.2
- **PLI**: Katamā aṭṭha?
- **EN**: What eight?

## dn16:3.21.3
- **PLI**: Khattiyaparisā, brāhmaṇaparisā, gahapatiparisā, samaṇaparisā, cātumahārājikaparisā, tāvatiṁsaparisā, māraparisā, brahmaparisā.
- **EN**: The assemblies of aristocrats, brahmins, householders, and ascetics. An assembly of the gods of the four great kings. An assembly of the gods of the thirty-three. An assembly of Māras. An assembly of divinities.
- **NOTE**: These “assemblies” were formal deliberative meetings, not just gatherings. Thus “householders” here does not mean “lay folk” in apposition to “ascetics”; rather it means “home owners”. Some of these assemblies are depicted or alluded to in the Dīghanikāya. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn6/en/sujato#1.3'>DN 6:1.3</a>, brahmins assemble in Vesālī; while at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.13.3'>DN 3:1.13.3</a>, Ambaṭṭha speaks of an assembly of Sakyans, who were aristocrats. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn18/en/sujato#12.1'>DN 18:12.1</a>, we get a glimpse of the proceedings at a meeting of the gods of the thirty-three.

## dn16:3.22.1  `pts-cs3.22`
- **PLI**: Abhijānāmi kho panāhaṁ, ānanda, anekasataṁ khattiyaparisaṁ upasaṅkamitā.
- **EN**: I recall having approached an assembly of hundreds of aristocrats.

## dn16:3.22.2
- **PLI**: Tatrapi mayā sannisinnapubbañceva sallapitapubbañca sākacchā ca samāpajjitapubbā.
- **EN**: There I used to sit with them, converse, and engage in discussion.

## dn16:3.22.3
- **PLI**: Tattha yādisako tesaṁ vaṇṇo hoti, tādisako mayhaṁ vaṇṇo hoti. Yādisako tesaṁ saro hoti, tādisako mayhaṁ saro hoti.
- **EN**: And my appearance and voice became just like theirs.

## dn16:3.22.4
- **PLI**: Dhammiyā kathāya sandassemi samādapemi samuttejemi sampahaṁsemi.
- **EN**: I educated, encouraged, fired up, and inspired them with a Dhamma talk.

## dn16:3.22.5
- **PLI**: Bhāsamānañca maṁ na jānanti:
- **EN**: But when I spoke they didn’t know:

## dn16:3.22.6
- **PLI**: ‘ko nu kho ayaṁ bhāsati devo vā manusso vā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Who is this that speaks? Is it a god or a human?’

## dn16:3.22.7
- **PLI**: Dhammiyā kathāya sandassetvā samādapetvā samuttejetvā sampahaṁsetvā antaradhāyāmi.
- **EN**: And when my Dhamma talk was finished I vanished.

## dn16:3.22.8
- **PLI**: Antarahitañca maṁ na jānanti:
- **EN**: But when I vanished they didn’t know:

## dn16:3.22.9
- **PLI**: ‘ko nu kho ayaṁ antarahito devo vā manusso vā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Who was that who vanished? Was it a god or a human?’
- **NOTE**: There do not appear to be any records of this happening in the early texts, although that is perhaps to be expected. It does feel out of character for the Buddha, as normally he is very up front. The commentary says that this ruse was adopted so the Buddha could plant subtle seeds (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vāsanā</i>) for the future. At Kena Upaniṣad 3, Brahma appears to the gods and tests them, but they do not know him.
