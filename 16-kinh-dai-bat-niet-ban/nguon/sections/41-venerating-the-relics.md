## dn16:6.28.5  `mc10.147, ms7D_530`
- **PLI**: Ekāhi dāṭhā tidivehi pūjitā,
- **EN**: One tooth is venerated <j>by the gods of the Third Heaven,
- **NOTE**: The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tidiva</i> was the realm of Indra/Sakka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn21/en/sujato#1.12.22'>DN 21:1.12.22</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.27.12'>DN 30:2.27.12</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag10.1/en/sujato#8.4'>Thag 10.1:8.4</a>), as it was from the time of the Vedas (Rig Veda 9.113.9, Atharva Veda 10.9.5, 17.1.10). In Buddhist cosmology, this agrees with the fact that Sakka’s realm is the third of the heavens (after the earth gods and the four great kings). Thus the Third Heaven is the heaven of the Thirty-Three. Both ideas spring from an old association of Indra, or divinity generally, with the number three. Agni Purāṇa lists <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tridiva</i> as a synonym for “heaven”, as does Amarakośa 1.1.11. Kṣīrasvāmin clarifies that three here means “third”, while also adding that some connect it with the Brahmā realm (Amarakośodghāṭana 1.1.6; see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn7.18/en/sujato#4.2'>SN 7.18:4.2</a>).
