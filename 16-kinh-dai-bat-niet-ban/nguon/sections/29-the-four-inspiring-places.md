# Muc 5.7 — The Four Inspiring Places  (14 đoạn)

## dn16:5.7.0
- **PLI**: 28. Catusaṁvejanīyaṭhāna
- **EN**: 28. The Four Inspiring Places

## dn16:5.7.1  `ms7D_454, msdiv202, pts-cs5.7, sya10.163`
- **PLI**: “Pubbe, bhante, disāsu vassaṁvuṭṭhā bhikkhū āgacchanti tathāgataṁ dassanāya.
- **EN**: “Previously, sir, when mendicants had completed the rainy season residence in various districts they came to see the Realized One.
- **NOTE**: As for example at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn24/en/sujato#2.1'>MN 24:2.1</a>.

## dn16:5.7.2
- **PLI**: Te mayaṁ labhāma manobhāvanīye bhikkhū dassanāya, labhāma payirupāsanāya.
- **EN**: We got to see the esteemed mendicants, and to pay homage to them.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Manobhāvanīya</i> is explained in the commentaries as “those who, when seen, cause the mind to grow in what is skillful.”

## dn16:5.7.3
- **PLI**: Bhagavato pana mayaṁ, bhante, accayena na labhissāma manobhāvanīye bhikkhū dassanāya, na labhissāma payirupāsanāyā”ti.
- **EN**: But when the Buddha has passed, we won’t get to see the esteemed mendicants or to pay homage to them.”

## dn16:5.8.1  `ms7D_455, ndp7.109, pts-cs5.8, pts-vp-pli2.141`
- **PLI**: “Cattārimāni, ānanda, saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyāni saṁvejanīyāni ṭhānāni.
- **EN**: “Ānanda, a faithful gentleman should go to see these four inspiring places.
- **NOTE**: These four sites are the primary destinations of modern Buddhist pilgrims in India. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṁvejanīya</i> is “stirring, provoking inspiration or urgency” such as when seeing an astonishing, disturbing, or amazing sight.

## dn16:5.8.2
- **PLI**: Katamāni cattāri?
- **EN**: What four?

## dn16:5.8.3
- **PLI**: ‘Idha tathāgato jāto’ti, ānanda, saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyaṁ saṁvejanīyaṁ ṭhānaṁ.
- **EN**: Thinking: ‘Here the Realized One was born!’—that is an inspiring place.
- **NOTE**: Lumbinī in modern Nepal, which today is a well-maintained and quiet place for devotion and meditation. The site is marked with an Ashokan pillar dated perhaps 150 years after this time. On the pillar is inscribed in Brahmī characters <i lang='pi' translate='no'>hida bhagavaṁ jāte ti</i>, which is a direct quote in Māgadhī of the Pali phrase here, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>idha tathāgato jāto ti</i>. (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>Tathāgata</i> “realized one” is how the Buddha referred to himself, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhagavā</i> “blessed one” is how his followers referred to him.) This is the oldest direct quote from the early canon preserved in the archaeological record.

## dn16:5.8.4
- **PLI**: ‘Idha tathāgato anuttaraṁ sammāsambodhiṁ abhisambuddho’ti, ānanda, saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyaṁ saṁvejanīyaṁ ṭhānaṁ.
- **EN**: Thinking: ‘Here the Realized One became awakened as a supreme fully awakened Buddha!’—that is an inspiring place.
- **NOTE**: Bodhgaya in modern Bihar, which is called Uruvelā in the Pali texts. It is a bustling center for pilgrims from all over the Buddhist world, at the center of which is the great stupa next to the Bodhi tree.

## dn16:5.8.5
- **PLI**: ‘Idha tathāgatena anuttaraṁ dhammacakkaṁ pavattitan’ti, ānanda, saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyaṁ saṁvejanīyaṁ ṭhānaṁ.
- **EN**: Thinking: ‘Here the supreme Wheel of Dhamma was rolled forth by the Realized One!’—that is an inspiring place.
- **NOTE**: Isipatana is modern Sarnath, in the northern part of Varanasi on the Ganges. In the temple next to the park, the first sermon is recited every night. | Note the passive voice, whereas the parallel at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.118/en/sujato'>AN 4.118</a> is active.

## dn16:5.8.6
- **PLI**: ‘Idha tathāgato anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbuto’ti, ānanda, saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyaṁ saṁvejanīyaṁ ṭhānaṁ.
- **EN**: Thinking: ‘Here the Realized One was fully quenched in the element of extinguishment with no residue!’—that is an inspiring place.
- **NOTE**: The phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbānadhātuyā parinibbuto</i>, “fully quenched in the element of extinguishment” shows how the Pali uses two roots simultaneously for Nibbāna. The better-known term, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i> (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nirvāṇa</i>), is from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vā</i> (“to blow”), with the basic sense of going out through being deprived of air. The past participle form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbuta</i> (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nirvṛta</i>), from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>var</i> (“to check or restrain”), has the basic sense of freedom from obstacles and constrictions. Generally, the Pali treats <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbuta</i> as the past participle form of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i>, and for the most part the difference is purely verbal. Yet I believe the ambiguity is deliberate, as each captures a slightly different nuance of Nibbāna. “Quenched” means both to go out like a flame and to be satisfied like a thirst. This positive feel appears in Sanskrit passages using <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nirvṛta</i>, such as Manusmṛti 1.54, where it describes the blissful absorption of all beings into the great divinity. By using “extinguishment” for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i> and “quenched” for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbuta</i> I hope to express this subtle distinction, or at least to alert the reader that it exists.

## dn16:5.8.7
- **PLI**: Imāni kho, ānanda, cattāri saddhassa kulaputtassa dassanīyāni saṁvejanīyāni ṭhānāni.
- **EN**: These are the four inspiring places that a faithful gentleman should go to see.

- **EN**: Anyone who passes away while on pilgrimage to these shrines will, when their body breaks up, after death, be reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm.”
