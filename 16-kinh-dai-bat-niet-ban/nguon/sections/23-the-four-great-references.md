## dn16:4.8.2
- **PLI**: ‘sammukhā metaṁ, āvuso, bhagavato sutaṁ sammukhā paṭiggahitaṁ,
- **EN**: ‘Reverend, I have heard and learned this in the presence of the Buddha:
- **NOTE**: The phrase used in the suttas when reporting a teaching heard directly from the Buddha, eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.52/en/sujato#5.1'>SN 55.52:5.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.90/en/sujato#9.1'>SN 22.90:9.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn47/en/sujato#10.7'>MN 47:10.7</a>, etc.

## dn16:4.8.3
- **PLI**: ayaṁ dhammo ayaṁ vinayo idaṁ satthusāsanan’ti.
- **EN**: this is the teaching, this is the training, this is the Teacher’s instruction.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dhamma</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i>, and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satthusāsana</i> are used in the suttas in the general sense of the entirety of the Buddha’s teachings. In his advice to Mahāpajāpatī (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.53/en/sujato'>AN 8.53</a>) and Upāli (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.83/en/sujato'>AN 7.83</a>), they are said to be whatever conforms with letting go. A stream-enterer who is a lay person is grounded in them (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.16/en/sujato#7.2'>AN 6.16:7.2</a>). They are more frequent in the Vinaya, being used as a general qualifier of a legitimate legal procedure (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc63/en/sujato#2.1.8'>Bu Pc 63:2.1.8</a>). Due to this, the fact that there are three items, and the fact that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> are different words, we should not take <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i> here as equivalent to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i> below. Rather, they are a general claim to authenticity and do not specify particular texts.

## dn16:4.8.4

- **EN**: You should neither approve nor reject that mendicant’s statement.
- **NOTE**: As at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn112/en/sujato#3.1'>MN 112:3.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn29/en/sujato#18.4'>DN 29:18.4</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.180/en/sujato#2.5'>AN 4.180:2.5</a>.

## dn16:4.8.5
- **PLI**: Anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā tāni padabyañjanāni sādhukaṁ uggahetvā sutte osāretabbāni, vinaye sandassetabbāni.
- **EN**: Instead, having carefully memorized those words and phrases, you should make sure they fit in the discourse and are exhibited in the training.
- **NOTE**: It is tempting to assume that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> here means the Suttapiṭaka and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i> means the Vinayapiṭaka, or at least an earlier version of these texts; but this is not supported by tradition. The late canonical Netti explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> as “the four noble truths” and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i> as “the removal of greed, hate, and delusion” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ne6/en/sujato'>Ne 6</a>). The commentaries discuss many interpretations; ultimately they agree with the Netti for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vinaya</i>, but say <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sutta</i> means the entire Tipiṭaka. I think the Netti is correct: teachings should “fit in” with the four noble truths (like other footprints fit in to an elephant’s footprint, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn28/en/sujato'>MN 28</a>), and they should “be exhibited” in that the results of practice should be apparent (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sandiṭṭhika</i>). | “Memorize” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>uggahetvā</i>), because in an oral tradition a text is not learned until it is known by heart; such claims must be taken seriously. | “Fit into” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>osāreti</i> or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>otāreti</i> per <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.180/en/sujato'>AN 4.180</a>; the two words have a similar meaning, “to flow down into”.

- **PLI**: Tāni ce sutte osāriyamānāni vinaye sandassiyamānāni na ceva sutte osaranti, na ca vinaye sandissanti, niṭṭhamettha gantabbaṁ:
- **EN**: If they do not fit in the discourse and are not exhibited in the training, you should draw the conclusion:
- **NOTE**: The text has <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ca</i> (“and”) rather than <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vā</i> (“or”), which would be expected if they were to be found in one or other textual collection. This is urging that any teaching fits in with the fundamental principles of the four noble truths, and is effective in removing defilements.

- **PLI**: Idaṁ, bhikkhave, paṭhamaṁ mahāpadesaṁ dhāreyyātha.
- **EN**: You should remember it. This is the first great reference.
- **NOTE**: The Pali text appears to say that one should remember the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahāpadesa</i>, whereas the Sanskrit clearly says one should remember the passage (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ayaṁ dharmo’yaṁ vinaya idaṁ śāstuḥ śāsanam iti viditvā dhārayitavyāḥ</i>). It seems as if this pattern should underlie the Pali too, for if the passage is false, one “should discard it” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>chaḍḍeyyātha</i>). In the case of a correct passage we should expect a parallel injunction that one “should remember it” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhāreyyātha</i>). But if this applies to the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahāpadesa</i> there is no such injunction. Thus it seems as if the Sanskrit is correct here and the Pali corrupt. I restore it by reversing the order of the phrases.
