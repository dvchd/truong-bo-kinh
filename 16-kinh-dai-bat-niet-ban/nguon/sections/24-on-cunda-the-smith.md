# Muc 4.13 — On Cunda the Smith  (40 đoạn)

## dn16:4.13.0
- **PLI**: 23. Kammāraputtacundavatthu
- **EN**: 23. On Cunda the Smith

## dn16:4.13.1  `cck10.159, mc10.112, ms7D_420, msdiv189, pts-cs4.13, pts-vp-pli2.127, sya10.148`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā bhoganagare yathābhirantaṁ viharitvā āyasmantaṁ ānandaṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: When the Buddha had stayed in Bhoga City as long as he pleased, he addressed Ānanda,

## dn16:4.13.2
- **PLI**: “āyāmānanda, yena pāvā tenupasaṅkamissāmā”ti.
- **EN**: “Come, Ānanda, let’s go to Pāvā.”
- **NOTE**: Pāvā, a town of the Mallas, has more significance for this narrative than appears at first sight. It was, according to Buddhist texts, the place where Mahāvīra had recently died, plunging the Jains into chaos. (The Jains, however, say this was another Pāvā, east of Nāḷandā.) Pāvā became associated with especially ascetic monks: thirty Pāvā monks became awakened on hearing a particularly strong discourse (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn15.13/en/sujato'>SN 15.13</a>); Mahākassapa heard the news of the Buddha’s passing at Pāvā; and monks from Pāvā allied with monks of “Avanti and the south” arguing for strict Vinaya in the Second Council (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd22/en/sujato#1.7.11.1'>Kd 22:1.7.11.1</a>).

## dn16:4.13.3
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi.
- **EN**: “Yes, sir,” Ānanda replied.

## dn16:4.13.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ yena pāvā tadavasari.
- **EN**: Then the Buddha together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants arrived at Pāvā,
- **NOTE**: This passage also at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud8.5/en/sujato'>Ud 8.5</a>.

## dn16:4.13.5
- **PLI**: Tatra sudaṁ bhagavā pāvāyaṁ viharati cundassa kammāraputtassa ambavane.
- **EN**: where he stayed in Cunda the smith’s mango grove.
- **NOTE**: Cunda was apparently a metal-worker, which was an advanced form of technological craftsmanship.

## dn16:4.14.1  `pts-cs4.14`

- **EN**: Cunda heard that the Buddha had arrived and was staying in his mango grove.

## dn16:4.14.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho cundo kammāraputto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi.
- **EN**: Then he went to the Buddha, bowed, and sat down to one side.

## dn16:4.14.3
- **PLI**: Ekamantaṁ nisinnaṁ kho cundaṁ kammāraputtaṁ bhagavā dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṁsesi.
- **EN**: The Buddha educated, encouraged, fired up, and inspired him with a Dhamma talk.
- **NOTE**: This is, according to the commentary, preserved as the other discourse taught to Cunda, which deals with proper asceticism (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.5/en/sujato'>Snp 1.5</a>).

## dn16:4.14.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho cundo kammāraputto bhagavatā dhammiyā kathāya sandassito samādapito samuttejito sampahaṁsito bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then Cunda said to the Buddha,

## dn16:4.15.1  `pts-cs4.15`
- **PLI**: “adhivāsetu me, bhante, bhagavā svātanāya bhattaṁ saddhiṁ bhikkhusaṅghenā”ti.
- **EN**: “Sir, would the Buddha, together with the mendicant Saṅgha, please accept tomorrow’s meal from me?”

## dn16:4.15.2
- **PLI**: Adhivāsesi bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena.
- **EN**: The Buddha consented with silence.

## dn16:4.16.1  `pts-cs4.16`

- **EN**: Then, knowing that the Buddha had consented, Cunda rose from his seat, bowed, and respectfully circled the Buddha, keeping him on his right, before leaving.

## dn16:4.17.1  `bj8.198, cck10.160, csp1ed7.106, csp2ed7.106, dr10.148, mc10.113, ms7D_421, ndp7.99, pts-cs4.17, sya10.149, vri2.97`
- **PLI**: Atha kho cundo kammāraputto tassā rattiyā accayena sake nivesane paṇītaṁ khādanīyaṁ bhojanīyaṁ paṭiyādāpetvā pahūtañca sūkaramaddavaṁ bhagavato kālaṁ ārocāpesi:
- **EN**: And when the night had passed Cunda had delicious fresh and cooked foods prepared in his own home, and plenty of pork on the turn. Then he had the Buddha informed of the time, saying,
- **NOTE**: The exact meaning of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sūkaramaddava</i> is unclear. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sūkara</i> is “pig”, but some Chinese translations suggest the sense “mushroom”; and it is true that there are several names of plants or plant dishes that begin with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sūkara</i> or other animals. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dhp377/en/sujato'>Dhp 377</a>, flowers are described as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>maddava</i>, meaning “overripe, withering on the vine”. It is a common practice to allow meat to sit for a while to become tender and “high” for extra flavor when cooked. But this can lead to a proliferation of dangerous bacteria unless properly cooked, and such seems to have been the case here. In any case, this disputed term is marginal in a consideration of meat-eating in early Buddhism, which is discussed more fully elsewhere.

## dn16:4.17.2

- **EN**: “Sir, it’s time. The meal is ready.”

## dn16:4.18.2

- **EN**: and addressed Cunda,

## dn16:4.18.3
- **PLI**: “yaṁ te, cunda, sūkaramaddavaṁ paṭiyattaṁ, tena maṁ parivisa.
- **EN**: “Cunda, please serve me with the pork on the turn that you’ve prepared.
- **NOTE**: If the Buddha knew the meal was dangerous, why ask to be served it? This is even more striking in light of the fact that it is a Vinaya offense to request fine food, including meat, and nowhere else is such a request recorded (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc39/en/sujato#2.10.1'>Bu Pc 39:2.10.1</a>). Such narrative ambiguities serve to escalate a sense of wonder.
