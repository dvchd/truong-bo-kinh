- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena sunidhavassakārā magadhamahāmattā pāṭaligāme nagaraṁ māpenti vajjīnaṁ paṭibāhāya.
- **EN**: Now at that time the Magadhan chief ministers Sunidha and Vassakāra were building a citadel at Pāṭali Village to keep the Vajjis out.
- **NOTE**: Vassakāra was no dawdler, as he preceded the Buddha to Pāṭaligāma. And not long after, he returned to Rājagaha to build more fortifications there (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn108/en/sujato#6.2'>MN 108:6.2</a>). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Nagara</i> here means “citadel, fortress”, not “city”. It has the same sense as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pura</i>. The construction is defensive and may well have begun before Ajātasattu began thinking of invading.

## dn16:1.26.2
- **PLI**: Tena samayena sambahulā devatāyo sahasseva pāṭaligāme vatthūni pariggaṇhanti.
- **EN**: At that time thousands of deities were taking possession of building sites in Pāṭali Village.
- **NOTE**: The relation between deities and building sites (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vatthu</i>) appears only in this passage in the early texts (repeated at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud8.6/en/sujato#15.2'>Ud 8.6:15.2</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#28.7.3'>Kd 1:28.7.3</a>). Divination and geomancy (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vatthuvijjā</i>) for building sites is said to be a wrong livelihood at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn1/en/sujato#1.21.2'>DN 1:1.21.2</a>.

## dn16:1.28.7
- **PLI**: Yāvatā, ānanda, ariyaṁ āyatanaṁ yāvatā vaṇippatho idaṁ agganagaraṁ bhavissati pāṭaliputtaṁ puṭabhedanaṁ.
- **EN**: As far as the civilized region extends, as far as the trading zone extends, this will be the chief city: the Pāṭaliputta trade center.
- **NOTE**: From this point, later texts always refer to Pāṭaliputta, but the reason for the change of name from Pāṭaligāma is not explained in the commentary. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Putta</i> is a suffix indicating the people of a certain clan or place, so <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāṭaliputta</i> means “a person from Pāṭalī (village)” or as we might say, “Pāṭalian”. As the city became well known and its identity as a village receded, it must have become known as the “city of the Pāṭaliputtas” and hence simply Pāṭaliputta. Compare the uses of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñātika</i> below. | The commentary explains that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>puṭabhedanaṁ</i> refers to the “opening of packages”, signifying that it was a center of trade. This usage is attested as late as the 13th century Jain Vividhatīrthakalpa of Jinaprabhasūri. | “Civilized region” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyaṁ āyatanaṁ</i>, the cultural region defined by use of the Ariyaka language (cf. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.96/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 6.96:1.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud8.6/en/sujato#17.9'>Ud 8.6:17.9</a> and note to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato#4.8'>DN 26:4.8</a>).

## dn16:1.28.8

- **EN**: But Pāṭaliputta will face three threats:

## dn16:1.28.9
- **PLI**: aggito vā udakato vā mithubhedā vā”ti.
- **EN**: from fire, flood, and dissension.”
- **NOTE**: Excavations reveal that Pāṭaliputta was constructed of wood, so the danger of fire was real. It is situated on a low-lying flood plain of the Ganges, so flooding would also have been an obvious danger. As for dissension (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mithubheda</i>), Vassakāra had indicated that this was a means of bringing down the Vajjis, so it would seem that kamma was lying in wait for them.
