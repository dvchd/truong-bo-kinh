# Muc 2.14 — Ambapālī the Courtesan  (51 đoạn)

## dn16:2.14.0
- **PLI**: 11. Ambapālīgaṇikā
- **EN**: 11. Ambapālī the Courtesan

## dn16:2.14.1  `bj8.152, cck10.122, csp1ed7.82, csp2ed7.82, dr10.115, ms7D_344, msdiv161, ndp7.77, pts-cs2.14`
- **PLI**: Assosi kho ambapālī gaṇikā: “bhagavā kira vesāliṁ anuppatto vesāliyaṁ viharati mayhaṁ ambavane”ti.
- **EN**: Ambapālī the courtesan heard that the Buddha had arrived and was staying in her mango grove.
- **NOTE**: A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gaṇikā</i> was a trained entertainer and escort whose company commanded a high fee (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato#1.1.7'>Kd 8:1.1.7</a>). It was a respected position, as we see the city guild of Rājagaha, with King Bimbisāra’s blessing, appoint the young woman Sālavatī in a similar position (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato#1.2.13'>Kd 8:1.2.13</a>).

## dn16:2.14.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho ambapālī gaṇikā bhaddāni bhaddāni yānāni yojāpetvā bhaddaṁ bhaddaṁ yānaṁ abhiruhitvā bhaddehi bhaddehi yānehi vesāliyā niyyāsi. Yena sako ārāmo tena pāyāsi.
- **EN**: She had the finest carriages harnessed. Then she mounted a fine carriage and, along with other fine carriages, set out from Vesālī for her own park.

## dn16:2.14.3
- **PLI**: Yāvatikā yānassa bhūmi, yānena gantvā, yānā paccorohitvā pattikāva yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi.
- **EN**: She went by carriage as far as the terrain allowed, then descended and approached the Buddha on foot. She bowed and sat down to one side.

## dn16:2.14.4
- **PLI**: Ekamantaṁ nisinnaṁ kho ambapāliṁ gaṇikaṁ bhagavā dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṁsesi.
- **EN**: The Buddha educated, encouraged, fired up, and inspired her with a Dhamma talk.

## dn16:2.14.5
- **PLI**: Atha kho ambapālī gaṇikā bhagavatā dhammiyā kathāya sandassitā samādapitā samuttejitā sampahaṁsitā bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then she said to the Buddha,

## dn16:2.14.6
- **PLI**: “adhivāsetu me, bhante, bhagavā svātanāya bhattaṁ saddhiṁ bhikkhusaṅghenā”ti.
- **EN**: “Sir, would the Buddha, together with the mendicant Saṅgha, please accept tomorrow’s meal from me?”

## dn16:2.14.7
- **PLI**: Adhivāsesi bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena.
- **EN**: The Buddha consented with silence.

## dn16:2.14.8
- **PLI**: Atha kho ambapālī gaṇikā bhagavato adhivāsanaṁ viditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṁ katvā pakkāmi.
- **EN**: Then, knowing that the Buddha had consented, Ambapālī rose from her seat, bowed, and respectfully circled the Buddha, keeping him on her right, before leaving.

- **EN**: Some of the Licchavis were in blue, of blue color, clad in blue, adorned with blue. And some were similarly colored in yellow, red, or white.
- **NOTE**: The text spends more time on the Vajjis’ appearance than on Ambapālī’s. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thig15.1/en/sujato#12.1'>Thig 15.1:12.1</a> the nun Isidāsī recalls how she used to do the makeup for her husband every day, like his own personal beautician.

## dn16:2.16.1  `pts-cs2.16`
- **PLI**: Atha kho ambapālī gaṇikā daharānaṁ daharānaṁ licchavīnaṁ akkhena akkhaṁ cakkena cakkaṁ yugena yugaṁ paṭivaṭṭesi.
- **EN**: Then Ambapālī the courtesan collided with those Licchavi youths, axle to axle, wheel to wheel, yoke to yoke.
- **NOTE**: There are various reading for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>paṭivaṭṭesi</i>. The commentary glosses with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pahari</i> (“struck, collided”).

## dn16:2.16.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho te licchavī ambapāliṁ gaṇikaṁ etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: The Licchavis said to her,

## dn16:2.16.3
- **PLI**: “kiṁ, je ambapāli, daharānaṁ daharānaṁ licchavīnaṁ akkhena akkhaṁ cakkena cakkaṁ yugena yugaṁ paṭivaṭṭesī”ti?
- **EN**: “What, you wench Ambapālī, are you doing colliding with us axle to axle, wheel to wheel, yoke to yoke?”
- **NOTE**: The derogatory indeclinable vocative <i lang='pi' translate='no'>je</i> is otherwise used of the “naughty maid” Kāḷī at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato#9.13'>MN 21:9.13</a>. “Wench” is a term meant to insult on the basis of servitude or promiscuity, so it seems appropriate. The Licchavis are not distinguishing themselves here; given their youth, wealth, licentiousness, and evident familiarity with Ambapālī, it seems safe to conclude that they had been her clients.

## dn16:2.16.4
- **PLI**: “Tathā hi pana me, ayyaputtā, bhagavā nimantito svātanāya bhattaṁ saddhiṁ bhikkhusaṅghenā”ti.
- **EN**: “Well, masters, it’s because I’ve invited the Buddha for tomorrow’s meal together with the mendicant Saṅgha.”

## dn16:2.16.5
- **PLI**: “Dehi, je ambapāli, etaṁ bhattaṁ satasahassenā”ti.
- **EN**: “Wench, give us that meal for a hundred thousand!”

## dn16:2.16.6
- **PLI**: “Sacepi me, ayyaputtā, vesāliṁ sāhāraṁ dassatha, evamahaṁ taṁ bhattaṁ na dassāmī”ti.
- **EN**: “Masters, even if you were to give me Vesālī with her provinces, I still wouldn’t give that meal to you.”
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dassatha</i> is second future plural of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dadāti</i>. The Vinaya parallel at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd6/en/sujato#30.4.5'>Kd 6:30.4.5</a> uses optative forms instead. | “With provinces” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sāhāraṁ</i> (literally “with food”, commentary: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sajanapadaṁ</i>) refers to the holdings around the city that supplied food and other necessities.

## dn16:2.16.7
- **PLI**: Atha kho te licchavī aṅguliṁ phoṭesuṁ:
- **EN**: Then the Licchavis snapped their fingers, saying,

## dn16:2.16.8
- **PLI**: “jitamha vata bho ambakāya, jitamha vata bho ambakāyā”ti.
- **EN**: “We’ve been beaten by the aunty! We’ve been beaten by the aunty!”
- **NOTE**: Despite the context, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ambakā</i> has no etymological relation to <i lang='pi' translate='no'>amba</i> (“mango”) but is a term for “woman, mother”, used here as a play on words.

## dn16:2.19.8

- **EN**: The Buddha accepted the park.
- **NOTE**: An <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ārāma</i> is a tended and pleasant ground, a “park”. Here it has previously been referred to as “grove” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vana</i>) and “mango grove”. Usually in the suttas, however, it is a name for a place where monastics live. In English, monastics don’t live in parks, they live in monasteries. So once a place has been dedicated for the Saṅgha, I translate <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ārāma</i> as “monastery”.
