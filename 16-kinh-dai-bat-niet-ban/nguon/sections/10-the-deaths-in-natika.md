# Muc 2.5 — The Deaths in Ñātika  (34 đoạn)

## dn16:2.5.0
- **PLI**: 9. Anāvattidhammasambodhiparāyaṇa
- **EN**: 9. The Deaths in Ñātika

## dn16:2.5.1  `bj8.144, cck10.117, csp1ed7.78, csp2ed7.78, dr10.110, ms7D_330, msdiv156, ndp7.74, pts-cs2.5, pts-vp-pli2.92, sya10.109, vri2.72`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā koṭigāme yathābhirantaṁ viharitvā āyasmantaṁ ānandaṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: When the Buddha had stayed in the village of Koṭi as long as he pleased, he said to Ānanda,

## dn16:2.5.2
- **PLI**: “āyāmānanda, yena nātikā tenupaṅkamissāmā”ti.
- **EN**: “Come, Ānanda, let’s go to the land of the Ñātikas.”
- **NOTE**: Ñātika (also spelled <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nātika</i>, or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nādika</i>; Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jñātṛka</i>; Prākrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāyika</i>) was the clan to which the Jain leader Mahāvīra (called Nāṭaputta, i.e. Ñātiputta) belonged. Here it is unusually spelled in plural, which means “the land of the Ñātika clan”. | Despite the variant spellings, the commentary supports Ñātika, explaining that it is one of two villages by a lake originally settled by the sons of two brothers, hence being “of the relatives” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñātika</i>).

## dn16:2.5.3
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi.
- **EN**: “Yes, sir,” Ānanda replied.

## dn16:2.5.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ yena nātikā tadavasari.
- **EN**: Then the Buddha together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants arrived in the land of the Ñātikas,

## dn16:2.5.5
- **PLI**: Tatrapi sudaṁ bhagavā nātike viharati giñjakāvasathe.
- **EN**: where he stayed in the brick house at Ñātika.
- **NOTE**: Here Ñātika is in singular and appears to be the name of a town. Thus Ñātika is first of all a name of a clan, then the land they live in, then their chief town. | Over a millennium previously, the Indus Valley Civilization had built cities of fired brick with standardized size and construction methods, but in the Buddha’s day most buildings were wood. This is the only brick building mentioned in the suttas; brick construction is also discussed in the Vinaya.

## dn16:2.6.1  `pts-cs2.6`
- **PLI**: Atha kho āyasmā ānando yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then Venerable Ānanda went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him,

## dn16:2.6.2
- **PLI**: “sāḷho nāma, bhante, bhikkhu nātike kālaṅkato, tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo?
- **EN**: “Sir, the monk named Sāḷha has passed away in Ñātika. Where has he been reborn in his next life?
- **NOTE**: It is unprecedented to discuss the spiritual destinies of an entire town like this. Normally this question is only asked when specific individuals known to the Buddha have passed away. Likely the passage was created in the wake of the passing of Mahāvīra to show that even his own people were enthusiastic Buddhists. This portion of the text is also found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn55.8/en/sujato'>SN 55.8</a>.

## dn16:2.7.1  `bj8.146, cck10.118, csp1ed7.79, csp2ed7.79, dr10.111, mc10.84, mc10.85, ms7D_331, msdiv157, pts-cs2.7, pts-vp-pli2.93, sya10.110, vri2.73`
- **PLI**: “Sāḷho, ānanda, bhikkhu āsavānaṁ khayā anāsavaṁ cetovimuttiṁ paññāvimuttiṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja vihāsi.
- **EN**: “Ānanda, the monk Sāḷha had realized the undefiled freedom of heart and freedom by wisdom, having realized it with his own insight in this very life due to the ending of defilements.

## dn16:2.7.2
- **PLI**: Nandā, ānanda, bhikkhunī pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyinī anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: The nun Nandā had ended the five lower fetters. She’s been reborn spontaneously, and will be extinguished there, not liable to return from that world.

- **EN**: The layman Sudatta had ended three fetters, and weakened greed, hate, and delusion. He’s a once-returner; he will come back to this world once only, then make an end of suffering.

## dn16:2.7.12
- **PLI**: subhaddo, ānanda, upāsako pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātiko tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: and Subhadda had ended the five lower fetters. They’ve been reborn spontaneously, and will be extinguished there, not liable to return from that world.
- **NOTE**: The suttas consistently depict lay folk as attaining the third stage of awakening, non-return, but not the highest stage of arahantship, except in rare cases on the deathbed. The tradition extended this by saying that if a lay person becomes an arahant, they must ordain or die that day, but this is not supported in the early texts. The point is not whether you receive an ordination, but whether you let go of attachments.

## dn16:2.7.13
- **PLI**: Paropaññāsaṁ, ānanda, nātike upāsakā kālaṅkatā, pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyino anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: Over fifty laymen in Ñātika have passed away having ended the five lower fetters. They’ve been reborn spontaneously, and will be extinguished there, not liable to return from that world.

## dn16:2.7.14
- **PLI**: Sādhikā navuti, ānanda, nātike upāsakā kālaṅkatā tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṁ tanuttā sakadāgāmino sakideva imaṁ lokaṁ āgantvā dukkhassantaṁ karissanti.
- **EN**: More than ninety laymen in Ñātika have passed away having ended three fetters, and weakened greed, hate, and delusion. They’re once-returners, who will come back to this world once only, then make an end of suffering.

## dn16:2.7.15
- **PLI**: Sātirekāni, ānanda, pañcasatāni nātike upāsakā kālaṅkatā, tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā sotāpannā avinipātadhammā niyatā sambodhiparāyaṇā.
- **EN**: More than five hundred laymen in Ñātika have passed away having ended three fetters. They’re stream-enterers, not liable to be reborn in the underworld, assured, destined for awakening.
- **NOTE**: The commentary to the Saṁyutta attributes the large number of deaths to a recent plague (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ahivātaroga</i>, “viper’s breath disease”). This deadly contagion was known to kill off entire families (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#50.1.1'>Kd 1:50.1.1</a>). According to the commentaries, the same disease once afflicted Vesālī, prompting the teaching of the Ratanasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp2.1/en/sujato'>Snp 2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/kp6/en/sujato'>Kp 6</a>).
