## dn16:3.13.1  `bj8.168, dr10.127, ms7D_366, msdiv171, ndp7.85, pts-cs3.13, pts-vp-pli2.108`
- **PLI**: “Aṭṭha kho ime, ānanda, hetū, aṭṭha paccayā mahato bhūmicālassa pātubhāvāya.
- **EN**: “Ānanda, there are these eight causes and reasons for a great earthquake.

## dn16:3.13.2
- **PLI**: Katame aṭṭha?
- **EN**: What eight?

## dn16:3.13.3
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ, ānanda, mahāpathavī udake patiṭṭhitā, udakaṁ vāte patiṭṭhitaṁ, vāto ākāsaṭṭho. Hoti kho so, ānanda, samayo, yaṁ mahāvātā vāyanti. Mahāvātā vāyantā udakaṁ kampenti. Udakaṁ kampitaṁ pathaviṁ kampeti.
- **EN**: This great earth is established on water, the water is established on air, and the air stands in space. At a time when a great wind blows, it stirs the water, and the water stirs the earth.
- **NOTE**: So far as it goes, this is a naturalistic explanation. The “water element” has the quality of softening, while the “air element” is traditionally understood as “movement”. Thus in modern terms this means, “When underground forces disturb a region of instability.”

## dn16:3.13.4
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ paṭhamo hetu paṭhamo paccayo mahato bhūmicālassa pātubhāvāya.
- **EN**: This is the first cause and reason for a great earthquake.

## dn16:3.14.1  `ms7D_367, pts-cs3.14`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, samaṇo vā hoti brāhmaṇo vā iddhimā cetovasippatto, devo vā mahiddhiko mahānubhāvo, tassa parittā pathavīsaññā bhāvitā hoti, appamāṇā āposaññā. So imaṁ pathaviṁ kampeti saṅkampeti sampakampeti sampavedheti.
- **EN**: Furthermore, there is an ascetic or brahmin with psychic power who has achieved mastery of the mind, or a god who is mighty and powerful. They’ve developed a limited perception of earth and a limitless perception of water. They make the earth shake and rock and tremble.
- **NOTE**: The might of ascetics was legendary in ancient India (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn56/en/sujato#14.2'>MN 56:14.2</a>). The meditation described here might be compared with the “dimensions of mastery” below (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.25.1'>DN 16:3.25.1</a>).

## dn16:3.14.2

- **EN**: This is the second cause and reason for a great earthquake.

## dn16:3.15.1  `cck10.136, csp1ed7.91, csp2ed7.91, mc10.97, ms7D_368, pts-cs3.15`

- **EN**: Furthermore, when the being intent on awakening passes away from the host of joyful gods, he’s conceived in his mother’s womb, mindful and aware. Then the earth shakes and rocks and trembles.
- **NOTE**: At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#1.17.7'>DN 14:1.17.7</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn123/en/sujato#7.6'>MN 123:7.6</a> it is, rather, the entire galaxy that trembles, perhaps indicating that the enhanced miracle is a later development.

## dn16:3.20.1  `bj8.170, cck10.137, ms7D_373, pts-cs3.20, pts-vp-pli2.109`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, yadā tathāgato anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbāyati, tadāyaṁ pathavī kampati saṅkampati sampakampati sampavedhati.
- **EN**: Furthermore, when the Realized One becomes fully extinguished in the element of extinguishment with no residue, the earth shakes and rocks and trembles.
- **NOTE**: This refers to the Buddha’s death. The Pali here uses both <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parinibbāna</i>. Sometimes it is said that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbāna</i> is the attainment of arahantship, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parinibbāna</i> is the death of an arahant, but this distinction is not consistently applied in the suttas. | Regarding <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nibbānadhātuyā</i> (“the element of extinguishment”), in Pali the case is ambiguous. It is sometimes translated “by means of” or “through”, which assumes the instrumental; but the Sanskrit is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nirvāṇadhātau</i>, which must be locative.
