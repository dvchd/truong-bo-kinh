- **PLI**: Idhānanda, ariyasāvako buddhe aveccappasādena samannāgato hoti:
- **EN**: It’s when a noble disciple has experiential confidence in the Buddha:
- **NOTE**: “Experiential” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>avecca</i>, literally “having undergone”. “Experiential confidence” is the faith of a stream-enterer, who has seen for themselves.

## dn16:2.9.4
- **PLI**: ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘That Blessed One is perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.’
- **NOTE**: They have experiential confidence in the Buddha as a teacher because they have followed his path and realized the results that he speaks of.

## dn16:2.9.5  `ms7D_335`
- **PLI**: Dhamme aveccappasādena samannāgato hoti:
- **EN**: They have experiential confidence in the teaching:

## dn16:2.9.6
- **PLI**: ‘svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opaneyyiko paccattaṁ veditabbo viññūhī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The teaching is well explained by the Buddha—apparent in the present life, immediately effective, inviting inspection, relevant, so that sensible people can know it for themselves.’
- **NOTE**: A stream-enterer has direct experience of the four noble truths, so they have confirmed that the teaching is indeed realizable in this very life.

## dn16:2.9.7  `dr10.113, mc10.86, ms7D_336, pts-vp-pli2.94, vri2.74`

## dn16:2.9.8
- **PLI**: ‘suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho yadidaṁ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā, esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The Saṅgha of the Buddha’s disciples is practicing the way that’s good, direct, systematic, and proper. It consists of the four pairs, the eight individual persons. This is the Saṅgha of the Buddha’s disciples that is worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods, worthy of hospitality, worthy of a religious donation, worthy of greeting with cupped palms, and is the supreme field of merit for the world.’
- **NOTE**: The suttas distinguish between two senses of Saṅgha. The “mendicant Saṅgha” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhusaṅgha</i>) is the conventional community of monks and nuns. The “Saṅgha of disciples” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sāvakasaṅgha</i>) is classified as fourfold according to the stages of awakening: stream-entry, once-return, non-return, and perfection. Each of these stages is further subdivided into those of the path who are practicing for realization and those of the fruit who have realized. These are referred to as “noble disciples”, four of the path and four of the fruit, making eight individuals in total.

- **EN**: And how is a mendicant mindful?
- **NOTE**: In the Gradual Training the mendicant “establishes mindfulness” to begin meditation. Here this practice is expanded, and in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn22/en/sujato'>DN 22</a> it is expanded even further.

## dn16:2.12.5

- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of the body—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world.
- **NOTE**: The locative idiom <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kāye kāyānupassī</i> indicates that the meditator focuses on a particular aspect of body contemplation, such as observing the breath, awareness of the body parts, or reflection on the four elements. | “Rid of desire and aversion for the world” refers to clearing the mind through the previous practice of sense restraint.
