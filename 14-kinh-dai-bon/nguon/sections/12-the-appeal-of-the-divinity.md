- **EN**: ‘This principle I have discovered is deep, hard to see, hard to understand, peaceful, sublime, beyond the scope of logic, subtle, comprehensible to the astute.
- **NOTE**: A similar account is told of Gotama Buddha at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn6.1/en/sujato#1.4'>SN 6.1:1.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#19.2'>MN 26:19.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn85/en/sujato#43.2'>MN 85:43.2</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#5.2.2'>Kd 1:5.2.2</a>.

## dn14:3.1.5
- **PLI**: Ālayarāmā kho panāyaṁ pajā ālayaratā ālayasammuditā.
- **EN**: But people like clinging, they love it and enjoy it.
- **NOTE**: Here “clinging” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ālaya</i>, from a root meaning “to stick”.

## dn14:3.1.6
- **PLI**: Ālayarāmāya kho pana pajāya ālayaratāya ālayasammuditāya duddasaṁ idaṁ ṭhānaṁ yadidaṁ idappaccayatāpaṭiccasamuppādo.
- **EN**: It’s hard for them to see this topic; that is, specific conditionality, dependent origination.
- **NOTE**: “Specific conditionality” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>idappaccayatā</i>) refers to the fact that dependent origination traces the specific conditions for other specific things: this conditions that. It is not a general principle of universal conditionality (“everything is interconnected”).

## dn14:3.1.7
- **PLI**: Idampi kho ṭhānaṁ duddasaṁ yadidaṁ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānaṁ.
- **EN**: It’s also hard for them to see this topic; that is, the stilling of all activities, the letting go of all attachments, the ending of craving, fading away, cessation, extinguishment.
- **NOTE**: “Stilling of all activities” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sabbasaṅkhārasamatho</i>) is the cessation of all conditioned energies or phenomena. | “Attachment” here is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upadhi</i>, which is both the things of the world to which we cling, and the inner clinging. The Jains defined <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upadhi</i> similarly (Tattvārthasūtra 9.26). The dominant meaning in Sanskrit is “fraud”, which perhaps informs the Buddhist sense.

## dn14:3.1.8
- **PLI**: Ahañceva kho pana dhammaṁ deseyyaṁ, pare ca me na ājāneyyuṁ; so mamassa kilamatho, sā mamassa vihesā’ti.
- **EN**: And if I were to teach this principle, others might not understand me, which would be wearying and troublesome for me.’
- **NOTE**: The commentary is careful to specify that the Buddha means physical exhaustion only.

- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pamuñcantu saddhaṁ</i> has long troubled translators, as the basic sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pamuñcantu</i> is “release”. The problem is a long-standing one, for Sanskrit variants include <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pramodantu</i> (“celebrate”) or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>praṇudantu kāṅkṣāḥ</i> (“dispel doubts”).
