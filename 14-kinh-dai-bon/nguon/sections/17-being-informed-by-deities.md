- **PLI**: Ekamidāhaṁ, bhikkhave, samayaṁ ukkaṭṭhāyaṁ viharāmi subhagavane sālarājamūle.
- **EN**: This one time, mendicants, I was staying near Ukkaṭṭhā in the Subhaga Forest at the root of a magnificent sal tree.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha now returns to the present life. Ukkaṭṭhā is the home of Pokkharasāti (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#2.13.1'>DN 3:2.13.1</a>) and the site of the astonishing and cosmically significant discourses <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato'>MN 1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato'>MN 49</a>.

## dn14:3.29.2
- **PLI**: Tassa mayhaṁ, bhikkhave, rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṁ cetaso parivitakko udapādi:
- **EN**: As I was in private retreat this thought came to mind,

## dn14:3.29.3
- **PLI**: ‘na kho so sattāvāso sulabharūpo, yo mayā anāvutthapubbo iminā dīghena addhunā aññatra suddhāvāsehi devehi.
- **EN**: ‘It’s not easy to find an abode of sentient beings where I haven’t previously abided in all this long time, except the gods of the pure abodes.
- **NOTE**: Only non-returners are born in the pure abodes (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>suddhāvāsā</i>), from where they realize full awakening.

## dn14:3.29.4
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ yena suddhāvāsā devā tenupasaṅkameyyan’ti.
- **EN**: Why don’t I go to see them?’

## dn14:3.29.5
- **PLI**: Atha khvāhaṁ, bhikkhave, seyyathāpi nāma balavā puriso samiñjitaṁ vā bāhaṁ pasāreyya, pasāritaṁ vā bāhaṁ samiñjeyya; evameva—ukkaṭṭhāyaṁ subhagavane sālarājamūle antarahito avihesu devesu pāturahosiṁ.
- **EN**: Then, as easily as a strong person would extend or contract their arm, I vanished from the Subhaga Forest and reappeared with the gods of Aviha.

## dn14:3.29.6
- **PLI**: Tasmiṁ, bhikkhave, devanikāye anekāni devatāsahassāni anekāni devatāsatasahassāni yenāhaṁ tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā maṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ aṭṭhaṁsu. Ekamantaṁ ṭhitā kho, bhikkhave, tā devatā maṁ etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: In that order of gods, many thousands, many hundreds of thousands of deities approached me, bowed, stood to one side, and said to me,
- **NOTE**: “Order of gods” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>devanikāya</i>. I use “deity” to distinguish <i lang='pi' translate='no'>devatā</i> from <i lang='pi' translate='no'>deva</i> but there is no substantial difference.
