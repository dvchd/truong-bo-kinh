# Muc 2.18 — Vipassī’s Reflections  (103 đoạn)

## dn14:2.18.0
- **PLI**: 11. Bodhisattaabhinivesa
- **EN**: 11. Vipassī’s Reflections

## dn14:2.18.1  `bj8.46, dr10.36, mc10.27, ms7D_112, msdiv57, pts-cs2.18, pts-vp-pli2.31`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, vipassissa bodhisattassa vāsūpagatassa rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṁ cetaso parivitakko udapādi:
- **EN**: Then as Vipassī, the one intent on awakening, was in private retreat in his dwelling, this thought came to his mind,
- **NOTE**: This is the only place <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vāsūpagata</i> (“entered his dwelling”) is added to this stock phrase.

## dn14:2.18.2
- **PLI**: ‘kicchaṁ vatāyaṁ loko āpanno, jāyati ca jīyati ca mīyati ca cavati ca upapajjati ca, atha ca panimassa dukkhassa nissaraṇaṁ nappajānāti jarāmaraṇassa,
- **EN**: ‘Alas, this world has fallen into trouble. It’s born, grows old, dies, passes away, and is reborn, yet it doesn’t understand how to escape from this suffering, from old age and death.
- **NOTE**: At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.4/en/sujato'>SN 12.4</a>–10 this same reflection is attributed to each of the seven past Buddhas, kicking off an investigation into dependent origination in reverse order, starting with the outcome: suffering. Here this is treated as a meditative contemplation, whereas the next sutta, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15/en/sujato'>DN 15</a> Mahānidānasutta, delves into the philosophical implications.

## dn14:2.18.3
- **PLI**: kudāssu nāma imassa dukkhassa nissaraṇaṁ paññāyissati jarāmaraṇassā’ti?
- **EN**: Oh, when will an escape be found from this suffering, from old age and death?’

## dn14:2.18.4  `ms7D_113`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, vipassissa bodhisattassa etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then Vipassī thought,

## dn14:2.18.5
- **PLI**: ‘kimhi nu kho sati jarāmaraṇaṁ hoti, kiṁpaccayā jarāmaraṇan’ti?
- **EN**: ‘When what exists is there old age and death? What is a requirement for old age and death?’
- **NOTE**: The reflection shows how the bodhisatta is still digging into the trauma of discovering the reality of old age and death.

## dn14:2.18.6
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, vipassissa bodhisattassa yoniso manasikārā ahu paññāya abhisamayo:
- **EN**: Then, through rational application of mind, Vipassī penetrated with wisdom,
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Yoniso maniskāra</i> (“rational application of mind”) is a distinctively Buddhist term that literally means “applying the mind by way of source”. It is mostly used in investigating causality, although over time it came to have a more general sense of “reflection, inquiry, attention”.

## dn14:2.18.7
- **PLI**: ‘jātiyā kho sati jarāmaraṇaṁ hoti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘When rebirth exists there’s old age and death. Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death.’
- **NOTE**: Here begins the sequence of dependent origination. I give basic definitions of terms here, and more details in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15/en/sujato'>DN 15</a>. See also my notes in the Nidānasaṁyutta, especially <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.1/en/sujato'>SN 12.1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.2/en/sujato'>SN 12.2</a>. | Rebirth is a necessary antecedent condition for old age and death. Note that it is not a <em>sufficient</em> condition for old age, for many die when young.

## dn14:2.18.11
- **PLI**: ‘bhave kho sati jāti hoti, bhavapaccayā jātī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘When continued existence exists there’s rebirth. Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth.’
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Bhava</i> means “being, existence, life” in the sense of “past and future lives”. It refers to the ongoing process of continued existence, transmigrating through life after life. By itself, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhava</i> has a positive connotation, and represents the longing that many people have to continue to exist after death in a permanent and happy state. The Buddha, however, situates it as just one more dimension of the flow of conditions.

- **NOTE**: “Name and form” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāmarūpa</i>) is a Vedic concept referring to the multiplicity of material forms (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rūpa</i>) and associated names (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāma</i>), especially the individual “sentient organisms” such as gods and humans (Rig Veda 5.43.10, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.6.1, Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.3.2), which are ultimately absorbed into the divine, like rivers in the ocean (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 3.2.8, Praśna Upaniṣad 6.5). The Buddha treated “name” analytically as feeling, perception, intention, contact, and application of mind, and “form” as the four elements and derived matter (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15/en/sujato#20.8'>DN 15:20.8</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn9/en/sujato#52.1'>MN 9:52.1</a>–54.7, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.2/en/sujato#11.1'>SN 12.2:11.1</a>).

Thus far the analysis agrees with Yājñavalkya, who says that the manifold appearances in the world arise from consciousness (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>etebhyo bhūtebhyaḥ samutthāya</i>, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.12).

Consciousness (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>viññāṇa</i>) is not a fundamental reality (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahadbhūta</i>) underlying multiplicity, but rather a conditioned process that exists only together with name and form.

## dn14:2.19.1  `cck10.41, csp1ed7.28, csp2ed7.28, ms7D_123, msdiv58, pts-cs2.19, pts-vp-pli2.33`

## dn14:2.19.2
- **PLI**: ‘paccudāvattati kho idaṁ viññāṇaṁ nāmarūpamhā, nāparaṁ gacchati.
- **EN**: ‘This consciousness turns back from name and form, and doesn’t go beyond that.’
- **NOTE**: Dependent origination normally continues with two further factors: choices and ignorance. The full series does not appear in the Dīghanikāya. This truncated series emphasizes the mutuality of name and form with consciousness, but does not preclude the normal linear series. Each presentation of dependent origination reveals a different aspect of a complex, ramified process.

## dn14:2.19.3
- **PLI**: Ettāvatā jāyetha vā jiyyetha vā miyyetha vā cavetha vā upapajjetha vā, yadidaṁ
- **EN**: This is the extent to which one may be reborn, grow old, die, pass away, or reappear. That is:

## dn14:2.19.4
- **PLI**: nāmarūpapaccayā viññāṇaṁ, viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṁ, nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṁ, saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā, vedanāpaccayā taṇhā, taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṁ, upādānapaccayā bhavo, bhavapaccayā jāti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṁ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti.
- **EN**: Name and form are requirements for consciousness. Consciousness is a requirement for name and form. Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields. The six sense fields are requirements for contact. Contact is a requirement for feeling. Feeling is a requirement for craving. Craving is a requirement for grasping. Grasping is a requirement for continued existence. Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth. Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress to come to be.
- **NOTE**: Note the use of repetition to sum up the main doctrinal teachings. This serves to lock the sequence in memory and ensure no items are missing or displaced, while for one who is reciting the text from memory it provides an opportunity to reflect and apply the meaning in their own experience.

## dn14:2.22.5
- **PLI**: iti saṅkhārā, iti saṅkhārānaṁ samudayo, iti saṅkhārānaṁ atthaṅgamo;
- **EN**: Such are choices, such is the origin of choices, such is the disappearance of choices.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṅkhāra</i> in early Buddhism has three main doctrinal senses. (1) The broadest sense is “conditioned phenomena”, which we find in the Dīghanikāya at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#6.10.10'>DN 16:6.10.10</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn17/en/sujato#2.16.1'>DN 17:2.16.1</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn34/en/sujato#1.8.59'>DN 34:1.8.59</a>. This essentially means “everything except Nibbāna”. (2) Sometimes it is a physical or mental “process” or “activity” as at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn18/en/sujato#24.1'>DN 18:24.1</a>, where it refers to the gradual stilling of energies in the development of meditation. (3) In the five aggregates and dependent origination it has the sense of “morally potent volitions or choices” and is a synonym for <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cetanā</i> (“intention”). It is defined as good, bad, and imperturbable choices (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#1.10.77'>DN 33:1.10.77</a>), the latter of which refers to the kamma of the fourth <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> and above. In this sense it is the moral “choices” for good or ill that propel consciousness into a new rebirth.
