## dn14:1.36.1  `ms7D_87, msdiv39, pts-cs1.36, vri2.16`
- **PLI**: Jātassa kho pana, bhikkhave, vipassissa kumārassa kammavipākajaṁ dibbacakkhu pāturahosi yena sudaṁ samantā yojanaṁ passati divā ceva rattiñca.
- **EN**: From when he was born, Prince Vipassī had the power of clairvoyance which manifested as a result of past deeds, by which he could see for a league all around both by day and by night.
- **NOTE**: Normally clairvoyance and related abilities are said to arise due to the power of the fourth <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>, whereas here it comes naturally due to past kamma. After <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> this ability is empowered by the radiant mind clear of hindrances, whereas here it seems to be a more limited ability to see clearly and in the dark.

## dn14:1.37.1  `dr10.23, ms7D_88, msdiv40, pts-cs1.37`
- **PLI**: Jāto kho pana, bhikkhave, vipassī kumāro animisanto pekkhati seyyathāpi devā tāvatiṁsā.
- **EN**: And he was unblinkingly watchful, like the gods of the thirty-three.

## dn14:1.37.2
- **PLI**: ‘Animisanto kumāro pekkhatī’ti kho, bhikkhave, vipassissa kumārassa ‘vipassī vipassī’ tveva samaññā udapādi.
- **EN**: And because it was said that he was unblinkingly watchful, he came to be known as ‘Vipassī’.
- **NOTE**: Vipassī’s name is simply the personal form of the word made famous in Buddhist meditation, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i>. This is usually rendered as “insight”, but the sense here is more like “clear seeing”. | Pali frequently blurs the line between names and epithets. Here we see how a name is given, not at birth, but as a descriptive nickname that caught on.

## dn14:1.37.3  `cck10.26, csp1ed7.18, csp2ed7.18, ms7D_89, msdiv41, pts-vp-pli2.21, sya10.24`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, bandhumā rājā atthakaraṇe nisinno vipassiṁ kumāraṁ aṅke nisīdāpetvā atthe anusāsati.
- **EN**: Then while King Bandhumā was sitting in court, he’d sit Prince Vipassī in his lap and explain the case to him.

## dn14:1.37.4
- **PLI**: Tatra sudaṁ, bhikkhave, vipassī kumāro pituaṅke nisinno viceyya viceyya atthe panāyati ñāyena.
- **EN**: And sitting there in his father’s lap, Vipassī would thoroughly consider the case and draw a conclusion using a logical system.

## dn14:1.37.5
- **PLI**: Viceyya viceyya kumāro atthe panāyati ñāyenāti kho, bhikkhave, vipassissa kumārassa bhiyyoso mattāya ‘vipassī vipassī’ tveva samaññā udapādi.
- **EN**: So this was all the more reason for him to be known as ‘Vipassī’.
- **NOTE**: Indian epistemology acknowledges two fundamental sources of knowledge: direct perception (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paccakkha</i>) and inference (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anumāna</i>). This passage shows that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i> includes both. I render <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vipassanā</i> with “discernment” in an attempt to capture both nuances, rather than the standard “insight”.
