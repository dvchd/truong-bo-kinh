# Muc 1.31 — The Thirty-Two Marks of a Great Man  (59 đoạn)

## dn14:1.31.0
- **PLI**: 3. Dvattiṁsamahāpurisalakkhaṇā
- **EN**: 3. The Thirty-Two Marks of a Great Man

## dn14:1.31.1  `cck10.20, csp1ed7.14, csp2ed7.14, dr10.18, ms7D_50, msdiv33, ndp7.15, pts-cs1.31, pts-vp-pli2.16, sya10.19, vri2.13`
- **PLI**: Jāte kho pana, bhikkhave, vipassimhi kumāre bandhumato rañño paṭivedesuṁ:
- **EN**: When Prince Vipassī was born, they announced it to King Bandhumā,
- **NOTE**: Many of the details of the following account were later incorporated into the life of Gotama, under the principle that the major events of the lives of Buddhas follow a natural order. Nonetheless, they are not always consistent with other accounts in early texts. For example, in the Attadaṇḍasutta the Buddha says his going forth was prompted by disillusionment and fear due to chronic conflict and warfare (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp4.15/en/sujato'>Snp 4.15</a>).

## dn14:1.31.2
- **PLI**: ‘putto te, deva, jāto, taṁ devo passatū’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Your Majesty, your son is born! Let Your Majesty examine him!’

## dn14:1.31.3
- **PLI**: Addasā kho, bhikkhave, bandhumā rājā vipassiṁ kumāraṁ, disvā nemitte brāhmaṇe āmantāpetvā etadavoca:
- **EN**: When the king had examined the prince, he had the brahmin soothsayers summoned and said to them,
- **NOTE**: In <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.11/en/sujato'>Snp 3.11</a>, the newborn Siddhattha is examined by the dark hermit Asita. These two versions are combined in later accounts.

## dn14:1.31.4
- **PLI**: ‘passantu bhonto nemittā brāhmaṇā kumāran’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Gentlemen, please examine the prince.’

## dn14:1.31.5
- **PLI**: Addasaṁsu kho, bhikkhave, nemittā brāhmaṇā vipassiṁ kumāraṁ, disvā bandhumantaṁ rājānaṁ etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: When they had examined him they said to the king,

## dn14:1.31.6
- **PLI**: ‘attamano, deva, hohi, mahesakkho te putto uppanno, lābhā te, mahārāja, suladdhaṁ te, mahārāja, yassa te kule evarūpo putto uppanno.
- **EN**: ‘Rejoice, O King! An illustrious son is born to you. You are fortunate, so very fortunate, to have a son such as this born in this family!

## dn14:1.31.7
- **PLI**: Ayañhi, deva, kumāro dvattiṁsamahāpurisalakkhaṇehi samannāgato, yehi samannāgatassa mahāpurisassa dveva gatiyo bhavanti anaññā.
- **EN**: For the prince possesses the thirty-two marks of a great man. A great man who possesses these has only two possible destinies, no other.
- **NOTE**: Asita did not look at the 32 marks, and he predicted only one destiny: that he would become a Buddha.

## dn14:1.31.8
- **PLI**: Sace agāraṁ ajjhāvasati, rājā hoti cakkavattī dhammiko dhammarājā cāturanto vijitāvī janapadatthāvariyappatto sattaratanasamannāgato.
- **EN**: If he stays at home he becomes a king, a wheel-turning monarch, a just and principled king. His dominion extends to all four sides, he achieves stability in the country, and he possesses the seven treasures.

## dn14:1.31.9
- **PLI**: Tassimāni sattaratanāni bhavanti.
- **EN**: He has the following seven treasures:
- **NOTE**: Various “treasures” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ratana</i>) or “gems” of a king are discussed in such texts as Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 5.3.1.

## dn14:1.31.10  `bj8.24`

- **EN**: the wheel, the elephant, the horse, the jewel, the woman, the householder, and the commander as the seventh treasure.

## dn14:1.32.8  `csp1ed7.15, csp2ed7.15, dr10.19, ms7D_53`
- **PLI**: Imassa, deva, kumārassa heṭṭhā pādatalesu cakkāni jātāni sahassārāni sanemikāni sanābhikāni sabbākāraparipūrāni. Yampi imassa, deva, kumārassa heṭṭhā pādatalesu cakkāni jātāni sahassārāni sanemikāni sanābhikāni sabbākāraparipūrāni, idampissa mahāpurisassa mahāpurisalakkhaṇaṁ bhavati.
- **EN**: On the soles of his feet there are thousand-spoked wheels, with rims and hubs, complete in every detail.
- **NOTE**: These are solar symbols, evoking the sun as the “twelve-spoked wheel of truth” (Rig Veda 1.164.11). They leave marks that were seen by Doṇa (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.36/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 4.36:1.3</a>). They are often depicted in Buddhist art, signifying the perfection and completeness of the traces that the Buddha leaves behind in his teachings and practice. Bṛhatsaṁhitā 69.17 lists several auspicious marks, including the wheel.

## dn14:1.32.25  `cck10.23, ms7D_70, ndp7.16`
- **PLI**: Ayañhi, deva, kumāro nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo yāvatakvassa kāyo tāvatakvassa byāmo, yāvatakvassa byāmo, tāvatakvassa kāyo …pe…
- **EN**: He has the proportional circumference of a banyan tree: the span of his arms equals the height of his body.
- **NOTE**: Bṛhatsaṁhitā 69.13 has the same proportions without the simile. These are the normal human proportions, yet we cannot touch our knees without bending. The only way these marks could be reconciled is if the arms were extra long and the length of the legs below the knees was extra long as well. And this is exactly what we are told: the ankles are stretched and long, and the calves are like those of an antelope, whose rear calves are long proportionate to the thigh. Thus in this regard the marks appear to be internally consistent, though not describing normal human anatomy.

## dn14:1.32.27  `ms7D_72`
- **PLI**: Ayañhi, deva, kumāro rasaggasaggī …pe…
- **EN**: He has ridged taste buds.
- **NOTE**: “Ridged taste buds” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rasaggasaggī</i>. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Rasa</i> can mean either “taste” or “nutrition”, but the use of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ojā</i> in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.9.8'>DN 30:2.9.8</a> confirms the latter. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Gasa</i> is “swallow” and per <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.7.4'>DN 30:2.7.4</a> it is the “conveyance of savor” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rasaharaṇīyo</i>). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Agga</i> often means “best”, but this is derived from the primary sense of “peak”. The descriptors <i lang='pi' translate='no'>uddhagga</i> (“raised”) at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.7.4'>DN 30:2.7.4</a> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>susaṇṭhitā</i> (“prominent”) at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn30/en/sujato#2.9.8'>DN 30:2.9.8</a> confirm that the latter is meant. The mark refers to taste buds raised in noticeable ridges on the tongue that absorb nutrition and aid digestion. It has often been interpreted as “excellent (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aggī</i>) sense (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>gasa</i>) of taste (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rasa</i>)”, but this, being imperceptible to others, is rather a secondary consequence of the mark.
