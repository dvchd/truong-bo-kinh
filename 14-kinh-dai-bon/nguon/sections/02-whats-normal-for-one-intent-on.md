# Muc 1.17 — What’s Normal For One Intent on Awakening  (47 đoạn)

## dn14:1.17.0
- **PLI**: 2. Bodhisattadhammatā
- **EN**: 2. What’s Normal For One Intent on Awakening

## dn14:1.17.1  `ms7D_34, msdiv17, pts-cs1.17`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, vipassī bodhisatto tusitā kāyā cavitvā sato sampajāno mātukucchiṁ okkami.
- **EN**: When Vipassī, the being intent on awakening, passed away from the host of joyful gods, he was conceived in his mother’s womb, mindful and aware.
- **NOTE**: These characteristics of the birth of the Buddha-to-be are also found in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn123/en/sujato'>MN 123</a>, where Ānanda quotes the Buddha, although the wording is a little different and three extra details are added at the start. In addition, this detail and that about emerging mindfully from the womb are found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.127/en/sujato'>AN 4.127</a>. | Normally in early Pali, the word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bodhisatta</i> is reserved for the Buddha-to-be once he has left home and is practicing “intent on awakening” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn26/en/sujato#13.1'>MN 26:13.1</a>). This passage extends the usage back as far as the end of the immediate past life.

## dn14:1.17.2
- **PLI**: Ayamettha dhammatā.
- **EN**: This is normal in such a case.
- **NOTE**: “Normal” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhammatā</i>, a natural principle. This entire passage differs from the earliest concept of the bodhisatta (“one intent on awakening”), which in early texts is typically applied to Siddhattha after he left the home.

## dn14:1.17.3  `bj8.18, cck10.15, dr10.13, ms7D_35, msdiv18, ndp7.12, sya10.14, vri2.10`
- **PLI**: Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yadā bodhisatto tusitā kāyā cavitvā mātukucchiṁ okkamati.
- **EN**: It’s normal that, when the being intent on awakening passes away from the host of joyful gods, he is conceived in his mother’s womb.

## dn14:1.17.4
- **PLI**: Atha sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya appamāṇo uḷāro obhāso pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṁ devānubhāvaṁ.
- **EN**: And then—in this world with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, gods and humans—an immeasurable, magnificent light appears, surpassing the glory of the gods.

## dn14:1.17.5
- **PLI**: Yāpi tā lokantarikā aghā asaṁvutā andhakārā andhakāratimisā, yatthapime candimasūriyā evaṁmahiddhikā evaṁmahānubhāvā ābhāya nānubhonti, tatthapi appamāṇo uḷāro obhāso pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṁ devānubhāvaṁ.
- **EN**: Even in the boundless gloom of interstellar space—so utterly dark that even the luminosity of the moon and the sun, so mighty and powerful, makes no impression—an immeasurable, magnificent light appears, surpassing the glory of the gods.
- **NOTE**: The commentary identifies this realm of “utter darkness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>andhakāratimisā</i>) with a cold hell realm. There is a corresponding Purāṇic hell called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>andhatāmisra</i>. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Asaṁvutā</i> was translated by Ñāṇamoḷī as “abysmal”, but this relies on a commentarial cosmology that is not found in the suttas. The sense, rather, is “boundless”. The root harks back to the Vedic serpent Vṛtra who wraps the world in darkness. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Nānubhonti</i> (“makes no impression”) is glossed in the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.127/en/sujato'>AN 4.127</a> as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nappahonti</i> (“ineffective”).

## dn14:1.17.6
- **PLI**: Yepi tattha sattā upapannā, tepi tenobhāsena aññamaññaṁ sañjānanti: ‘aññepi kira, bho, santi sattā idhūpapannā’ti.
- **EN**: And the sentient beings reborn there recognize each other by that light: ‘So, it seems other sentient beings have been reborn here!’
- **NOTE**: The light is a physical one, not just a metaphor. From this, it appears that sentient beings may be spontaneously reborn in interstellar space. Compare the problem of the “Boltzmann brain” in physics.

## dn14:1.17.7
- **PLI**: Ayañca dasasahassī lokadhātu saṅkampati sampakampati sampavedhati. Appamāṇo ca uḷāro obhāso loke pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṁ devānubhāvaṁ.
- **EN**: And this ten-thousandfold galaxy shakes and rocks and trembles. And an immeasurable, magnificent light appears in the world, surpassing the glory of the gods.

## dn14:1.17.8
- **PLI**: Ayamettha dhammatā.
- **EN**: This is normal in such a case.

## dn14:1.17.9  `csp1ed7.11, csp2ed7.11, mc10.11, ms7D_36, msdiv19`
- **PLI**: Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yadā bodhisatto mātukucchiṁ okkanto hoti, cattāro naṁ devaputtā catuddisaṁ rakkhāya upagacchanti: ‘mā naṁ bodhisattaṁ vā bodhisattamātaraṁ vā manusso vā amanusso vā koci vā viheṭhesī’ti.
- **EN**: It’s normal that, when the being intent on awakening is conceived in his mother’s womb, four godlings approach to guard the four quarters, so that no human or non-human or anyone at all shall harm the being intent on awakening or his mother.
- **NOTE**: These are the Four Great Kings, regarded as protector deities. | The term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>okkanti</i> (“conception”) is a synonym of “rebirth” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn15.4.3/en/sujato'>DN 15.4.3</a>). It literally means “descent”. Its usage in this context is anticipated by Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.1, which describes how, as a man is dying, his self “descends” into the heart. He loses sense awareness, the tip of his heart lights up and he departs, “rising” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>utkrāmanta</i>) with his life-breath, and “descending” with consciousness into a new life, taking his knowledge, deeds, and past experiences.

- **EN**: This is normal in such a case.
