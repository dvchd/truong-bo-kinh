# Muc 13 — The Four Noble Fruits  (11 đoạn)

## dn6:13.0
- **PLI**: 2.2. Catuariyaphala
- **EN**: 2.2. The Four Noble Fruits

## dn6:13.1  `dr9.211, ms6D_612, msdiv373, ndp6.133, pts-cs13`
- **PLI**: “Katame pana te, bhante, dhammā uttaritarā ca paṇītatarā ca, yesaṁ sacchikiriyāhetu bhikkhū bhagavati brahmacariyaṁ carantī”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, what are those finer things?”

## dn6:13.2
- **PLI**: “Idha, mahāli, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā sotāpanno hoti avinipātadhammo niyato sambodhiparāyaṇo.
- **EN**: “Firstly, Mahāli, with the ending of three fetters a mendicant is a stream-enterer, not liable to be reborn in the underworld, assured, destined for awakening.
- **NOTE**: This is the first description of the four stages of awakening which are featured throughout the Pali canon. The three fetters are identity view, doubt, and misapprehension of precepts and observances (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn2/en/sujato#11.3'>MN 2:11.3</a>).

## dn6:13.3
- **PLI**: Ayampi kho, mahāli, dhammo uttaritaro ca paṇītataro ca, yassa sacchikiriyāhetu bhikkhū mayi brahmacariyaṁ caranti.
- **EN**: This is one of the finer things for the sake of which the mendicants lead the spiritual life under me.

## dn6:13.4  `cck9.200, km15.68, ms6D_613`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, mahāli, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṁ tanuttā sakadāgāmī hoti, sakideva imaṁ lokaṁ āgantvā dukkhassantaṁ karoti.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant—with the ending of three fetters, and the weakening of greed, hate, and delusion—is a once-returner. They come back to this world once only, then make an end of suffering.

## dn6:13.5
- **PLI**: Ayampi kho, mahāli, dhammo uttaritaro ca paṇītataro ca, yassa sacchikiriyāhetu bhikkhū mayi brahmacariyaṁ caranti.
- **EN**: This too is one of the finer things.

## dn6:13.6  `km15.69, ms6D_614, sya9.200`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, mahāli, bhikkhu pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti, tattha parinibbāyī, anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: Furthermore, with the ending of the five lower fetters, a mendicant is reborn spontaneously and will become extinguished there, not liable to return from that world.
- **NOTE**: The five lower fetters are the three mentioned above, plus sensual desire and ill will (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.13/en/sujato#1.5'>AN 10.13:1.5</a>). This is the non-returner, who spends their last life in an exalted Brahmā realm. Compare Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.15.1: one who lives properly “attains the Brahmā realm and does not return again” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmalokam abhisaṁpadyate na ca punar āvartate</i>) | A “spontaneous” rebirth is one that occurs without gestation in the womb, like most <i lang='pi' translate='no'>devas</i>, or for that matter, Boltzmann brains (see note on <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn12/en/sujato#32.3'>MN 12:32.3</a>).

## dn6:13.8  `csp1ed6.149, csp2ed6.149, mc9.157, ms6D_615`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, mahāli, bhikkhu āsavānaṁ khayā anāsavaṁ cetovimuttiṁ paññāvimuttiṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant has realized the undefiled freedom of heart and freedom by wisdom, and lives having realized it with their own insight in this very life due to the ending of defilements.
- **NOTE**: This is the arahant, the “worthy” or “perfected” one. Elsewhere it is said they abandon the five higher fetters: desire for rebirth in the realm of luminous form, desire for rebirth in the formless realm, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.13/en/sujato#2.2'>AN 10.13:2.2</a>).

- **EN**: These are the finer things, for the sake of which the mendicants lead the spiritual life under me.”
