## dn2:67.3
- **PLI**: So pacchābhattaṁ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto nisīdati pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā.
- **EN**: After the meal, they return from almsround, sit down cross-legged, set their body straight, and bring mindfulness to the present.
- **NOTE**: For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parimukhaṁ</i> (“present”) we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pratimukha</i> in Sanskrit, which can mean “presence” or the reflection of the face. Late canonical Pali explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parimukhaṁ</i> as “the tip of the nose or the reflection of the face (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mukhanimitta</i>)”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Parimukha</i> in Sanskrit is rare, but it appears in Pāṇini 4.4.29, which the commentary illustrates with the example of a servant “in the presence” of their master (cp. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.8/en/sujato'>SN 47.8</a>). So it seems the sense is “before the face” or more generally “in the presence”. | To “bring mindfulness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā</i>) is to “do satipaṭṭhāna”. The root sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upaṭṭhāna</i> is to “stand near” or “attend” (like a nurse on a patient, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn47.13/en/sujato#1.3'>SN 47.13:1.3</a>), and the Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>upasthāna</i> is commonly used in the sense “homage” of the gods, based on the idea of “attending” the sacred flame. A literal translation of the phrase might be, “having made memory attend to what is present”.

## dn2:68.1  `csp1ed6.68, csp2ed6.68, dr9.95, km14.181, lv1.193, ms6D_308, msdiv217, pts-cs68`
- **PLI**: So abhijjhaṁ loke pahāya vigatābhijjhena cetasā viharati, abhijjhāya cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up covetousness for the world, they meditate with a heart rid of covetousness, cleansing the mind of covetousness.
- **NOTE**: Covetousness (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhijjha</i>) has been curbed by sense restraint, and now is fully abandoned.

## dn2:68.2
- **PLI**: Byāpādapadosaṁ pahāya abyāpannacitto viharati sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī, byāpādapadosā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up ill will and malevolence, they meditate with a mind rid of ill will, full of sympathy for all living beings, cleansing the mind of ill will.
- **NOTE**: Likewise ill will (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>byāpādapadosa</i>), which was called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>domanassa</i> in the formula for sense restraint.

## dn2:68.3
- **PLI**: Thinamiddhaṁ pahāya vigatathinamiddho viharati ālokasaññī, sato sampajāno, thinamiddhā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up dullness and drowsiness, they meditate with a mind rid of dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and aware, cleansing the mind of dullness and drowsiness.
- **NOTE**: “Mindfulness and situational awareness” has a prominent role in abandoning dullness.

## dn2:68.4
- **PLI**: Uddhaccakukkuccaṁ pahāya anuddhato viharati, ajjhattaṁ vūpasantacitto, uddhaccakukkuccā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up restlessness and remorse, they meditate without restlessness, their mind settled internally, cleansing the mind of restlessness and remorse.
- **NOTE**: Restlessness hankers for the future and is countered by contentment. Remorse digs up the past and is countered by ethical purity.

## dn2:68.5
- **PLI**: Vicikicchaṁ pahāya tiṇṇavicikiccho viharati, akathaṅkathī kusalesu dhammesu, vicikicchāya cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up doubt, they meditate having gone beyond doubt, not undecided about skillful qualities, cleansing the mind of doubt.
- **NOTE**: The meditator set out on their path after gaining faith in the Buddha.
