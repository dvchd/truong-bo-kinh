# 4.3.3.3 — Các thần thông  (9 đoạn)

## dn2:87.0
- **PLI**: 4.3.3.3. Iddhividhañāṇa
- **EN**: 4.3.3.3. Psychic Powers

## dn2:87.1  `bj7.136, csp1ed6.74, csp2ed6.74, km14.195, lv1.211, lv1.212, ms6D_329, msdiv238, ndp6.69, pts-cs87, pts-vp-pli1.78, vri1.69`
- **PLI**: So evaṁ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte iddhividhāya cittaṁ abhinīharati abhininnāmeti.
- **EN**: When their mind has become immersed in samādhi like this—purified, bright, flawless, rid of corruptions, pliable, workable, steady, and imperturbable—they project it and extend it toward psychic power.
- **NOTE**: Here begin the “six direct knowledges” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>chaḷabhiññā</i>), which are found commonly throughout the early texts. | “Psychic powers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>iddhi</i>) were much cultivated in the Buddha’s day, but the means to acquire them varied: devotion to a god, brutal penances, or magic rituals. The Buddha taught that the mind developed in <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> was capable of things that are normally incomprehensible.

## dn2:87.2
- **PLI**: So anekavihitaṁ iddhividhaṁ paccanubhoti—ekopi hutvā bahudhā hoti, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hoti; āvibhāvaṁ tirobhāvaṁ tirokuṭṭaṁ tiropākāraṁ tiropabbataṁ asajjamāno gacchati seyyathāpi ākāse; pathaviyāpi ummujjanimujjaṁ karoti seyyathāpi udake; udakepi abhijjamāne gacchati seyyathāpi pathaviyā; ākāsepi pallaṅkena kamati seyyathāpi pakkhī sakuṇo; imepi candimasūriye evaṁmahiddhike evaṁmahānubhāve pāṇinā parāmasati parimajjati; yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṁ vatteti.
- **EN**: They wield the many kinds of psychic power: multiplying themselves and becoming one again; materializing and dematerializing; going unobstructed through a wall, a rampart, or a mountain as if through space; diving in and out of the earth as if it were water; walking on water as if it were earth; flying cross-legged through the sky like a bird; touching and stroking with the hand the sun and moon, so mighty and powerful; controlling the body as far as the realm of divinity.
- **NOTE**: Only a few of these are attested as events in the early texts. The most common is the ability to “materialize and dematerialize”, exhibited by the Buddha (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.30/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 8.30:2.1</a>), some disciples (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn37/en/sujato#6.1'>MN 37:6.1</a>), and deities (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn67/en/sujato#8.1'>MN 67:8.1</a>). The Pali is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>āvibhāvaṁ tirobhāvaṁ</i>, literally “manifest state, hidden state”. Also found in Chāndogya Upaniṣad 7.26.1.

## dn2:88.1  `bj7.138, cck9.101, dr9.104, km14.196, lv1.213, lv1.214, mc9.79, ms6D_330, msdiv239, pts-cs88, pts-vp-pli1.79, sya9.104`
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi, mahārāja, dakkho kumbhakāro vā kumbhakārantevāsī vā suparikammakatāya mattikāya yaṁ yadeva bhājanavikatiṁ ākaṅkheyya, taṁ tadeva kareyya abhinipphādeyya.
- **EN**: Suppose a deft potter or their apprentice had some well-prepared clay. They could produce any kind of pot that they like.
- **NOTE**: These similes hark back to the descriptions of the purified mind as pliable and workable.

## dn2:88.2
- **PLI**: Seyyathā vā pana, mahārāja, dakkho dantakāro vā dantakārantevāsī vā suparikammakatasmiṁ dantasmiṁ yaṁ yadeva dantavikatiṁ ākaṅkheyya, taṁ tadeva kareyya abhinipphādeyya.
- **EN**: Or suppose a deft ivory-carver or their apprentice had some well-prepared ivory. They could produce any kind of ivory item that they like.

## dn2:88.3
- **PLI**: Seyyathā vā pana, mahārāja, dakkho suvaṇṇakāro vā suvaṇṇakārantevāsī vā suparikammakatasmiṁ suvaṇṇasmiṁ yaṁ yadeva suvaṇṇavikatiṁ ākaṅkheyya, taṁ tadeva kareyya abhinipphādeyya.
- **EN**: Or suppose a deft goldsmith or their apprentice had some well-prepared gold. They could produce any kind of gold item that they like.
- **NOTE**: This simile is extended in detail at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.101/en/sujato'>AN 3.101</a>.

## dn2:88.4
- **PLI**: Evameva kho, mahārāja, bhikkhu evaṁ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte iddhividhāya cittaṁ abhinīharati abhininnāmeti.
- **EN**: In the same way, when their mind has become immersed in samādhi like this—purified, bright, flawless, rid of corruptions, pliable, workable, steady, and imperturbable—they project it and extend it toward psychic power.
