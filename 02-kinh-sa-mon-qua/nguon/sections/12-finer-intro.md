# 4.3 — Những quả vi tế hơn  (17 đoạn)

## dn2:39.0
- **PLI**: 4.3. Paṇītatarasāmaññaphala
- **EN**: 4.3. The Finer Fruits of the Ascetic Life

## dn2:39.1  `cck9.80, km14.155, mc9.63, ms6D_270, msdiv189, pts-cs39, sya9.82`
- **PLI**: “Sakkā pana, bhante, aññampi diṭṭheva dhamme sandiṭṭhikaṁ sāmaññaphalaṁ paññapetuṁ imehi sandiṭṭhikehi sāmaññaphalehi abhikkantatarañca paṇītatarañcā”ti?
- **EN**: “But sir, can you point out a fruit of the ascetic life that’s apparent in this very life which is better and finer than these?”

## dn2:39.2
- **PLI**: “Sakkā, mahārāja.
- **EN**: “I can, Great King.

## dn2:39.3
- **PLI**: Tena hi, mahārāja, suṇohi, sādhukaṁ manasi karohi, bhāsissāmī”ti.
- **EN**: Well then, listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.”
- **NOTE**: Having established the king’s genuine interest and understanding, the Buddha prepares him for the long discourse to follow.

## dn2:39.4
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto bhagavato paccassosi.
- **EN**: “Yes, sir,” replied the king.

## dn2:39.5  `csp1ed6.59, csp2ed6.59, lv1.165, ms6D_271, msdiv190, ndp6.55`
- **PLI**: Bhagavā etadavoca:
- **EN**: The Buddha said this:

## dn2:40.1
- **PLI**: “idha, mahārāja, tathāgato loke uppajjati arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā.
- **EN**: “Consider when a Realized One arises in the world, perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.
- **NOTE**: This is the start of the teaching on the Gradual Training, encompassing ethics (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sīla</i>), meditation (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>), and wisdom (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paññā</i>). Only the ethics portion appeared in the Brahmajālasutta, while all three are restated in all the remaining suttas of this chapter, although in truncated form. | It is exceedingly rare for a Buddha to appear.

## dn2:40.2
- **PLI**: So imaṁ lokaṁ sadevakaṁ samārakaṁ sabrahmakaṁ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṁ pajaṁ sadevamanussaṁ sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti.
- **EN**: He has realized with his own insight this world—with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, gods and humans—and he makes it known to others.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha realizes the truth by his own understanding, not through divine intervention or other metaphysical means.

## dn2:40.3
- **PLI**: So dhammaṁ deseti ādikalyāṇaṁ majjhekalyāṇaṁ pariyosānakalyāṇaṁ sātthaṁ sabyañjanaṁ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṁ parisuddhaṁ brahmacariyaṁ pakāseti.
- **EN**: He teaches Dhamma that’s good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, meaningful and well-phrased. And he reveals a spiritual practice that’s entirely full and pure.
- **NOTE**: It is good when first heard, when practicing, and when one has realized the fruits.

## dn2:41.1  `dr9.82, km14.156, lv1.166, ms6D_272, msdiv191, pts-cs41, pts-vp-pli1.63, sya9.83`
- **PLI**: Taṁ dhammaṁ suṇāti gahapati vā gahapatiputto vā aññatarasmiṁ vā kule paccājāto.
- **EN**: A householder hears that teaching, or a householder’s child, or someone reborn in a good family.
- **NOTE**: The word “householder” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>gahapati</i>) informally refers to any lay person, but more specifically indicates someone who owns a house, i.e. a person of standing. The renunciate life is not just for slaves or workers wishing to escape their station.

## dn2:41.2
- **PLI**: So taṁ dhammaṁ sutvā tathāgate saddhaṁ paṭilabhati.
- **EN**: They gain faith in the Realized One

## dn2:41.3
- **PLI**: So tena saddhāpaṭilābhena samannāgato iti paṭisañcikkhati:
- **EN**: and reflect:

## dn2:41.4
- **PLI**: ‘sambādho gharāvāso rajopatho, abbhokāso pabbajjā.
- **EN**: ‘Life at home is cramped and dirty, life gone forth is wide open.

## dn2:41.5
- **PLI**: Nayidaṁ sukaraṁ agāraṁ ajjhāvasatā ekantaparipuṇṇaṁ ekantaparisuddhaṁ saṅkhalikhitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ carituṁ.
- **EN**: It’s not easy for someone living at home to lead the spiritual life utterly full and pure, like a polished shell.

## dn2:41.6
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajeyyan’ti.
- **EN**: Why don’t I shave off my hair and beard, dress in ocher robes, and go forth from the lay life to homelessness?’

## dn2:41.7  `bj7.110, ms6D_273, msdiv192`
- **PLI**: So aparena samayena appaṁ vā bhogakkhandhaṁ pahāya mahantaṁ vā bhogakkhandhaṁ pahāya appaṁ vā ñātiparivaṭṭaṁ pahāya mahantaṁ vā ñātiparivaṭṭaṁ pahāya kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajati.
- **EN**: After some time they give up a large or small fortune, and a large or small family circle. They shave off hair and beard, dress in ocher robes, and go forth from the lay life to homelessness.

## dn2:42.1  `cck9.81, lv1.167, ms6D_274, msdiv193, pts-cs42, vri1.56`
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pabbajito samāno pātimokkhasaṁvarasaṁvuto viharati ācāragocarasampanno, aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī, samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesu, kāyakammavacīkammena samannāgato kusalena, parisuddhājīvo sīlasampanno, indriyesu guttadvāro, satisampajaññena samannāgato, santuṭṭho.
- **EN**: Once they’ve gone forth, they live restrained in the monastic code, conducting themselves well and resorting for alms in suitable places. Seeing danger in the slightest fault, they keep the rules they’ve undertaken. They act skillfully by body and speech. They’re purified in livelihood and accomplished in ethical conduct. They guard the sense doors, have mindfulness and situational awareness, and are content.
- **NOTE**: This serves as a table of contents for the teachings to come. | Nowadays, the “monastic code” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pātimokkha</i>) means the list of rules for monks and nuns found in the Vinayapiṭaka. In the early texts, however, it has three main meanings. Sometimes it does refer to the list of rules, as at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an10.36/en/sujato#1.6'>AN 10.36:1.6</a>. Here it refers to the code of conduct that follows, which is a non-legalistic set of guidelines that preceded the Vinayapiṭaka. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#3.28.1'>DN 14:3.28.1</a> it refers to the verses summarizing monastic conduct known as the “Ovāda Pātimokkha”.
