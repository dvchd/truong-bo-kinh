# Mục 2 — Cuộc bàn luận với Jīvaka Komārabhacca  (25 đoạn)

## dn2:8.0
- **PLI**: 2. Komārabhaccajīvakakathā
- **EN**: 2. A Discussion With Jīvaka Komārabhacca

## dn2:8.1  `bj7.86, dr9.62, km14.119, lv1.124, ms6D_233, msdiv157, ndp6.43, pts-cs8, sya9.64, vri1.44`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena jīvako komārabhacco rañño māgadhassa ajātasattussa vedehiputtassa avidūre tuṇhībhūto nisinno hoti.
- **EN**: Now at that time Jīvaka Komārabhacca was sitting silently not far from the king.
- **NOTE**: His absence of speech signifies his wisdom. The narrative creates a dramatic expectation through his stillness, an exquisitely Buddhist aesthetic choice.

## dn2:8.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto jīvakaṁ komārabhaccaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then the king said to him,

## dn2:8.3
- **PLI**: “tvaṁ pana, samma jīvaka, kiṁ tuṇhī”ti?
- **EN**: “But my dear Jīvaka, why are you silent?”

## dn2:8.4
- **PLI**: “Ayaṁ, deva, bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho amhākaṁ ambavane viharati mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ aḍḍhateḷasehi bhikkhusatehi.
- **EN**: “Your Majesty, this Blessed One, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha is staying in my mango grove together with a large Saṅgha of 1,250 mendicants.
- **NOTE**: According to the Vinaya, a monastery is normally offered to the “Saṅgha of the four quarters” and becomes their inalienable property. In the suttas this is not so clear, and it seems that Jīvaka still regarded the property as his. In practice there would have been a variety of arrangements, as there are today.

## dn2:8.5
- **PLI**: Taṁ kho pana bhagavantaṁ evaṁ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato:
- **EN**: He has this good reputation:

## dn2:8.6
- **PLI**: ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘That Blessed One is perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.’
- **NOTE**: The first appearance of the famous <i lang='pi' translate='no'>iti pi so</i> formula. It is still recited in praise of the Buddha in Theravada communities.

## dn2:8.7
- **PLI**: Taṁ devo bhagavantaṁ payirupāsatu.
- **EN**: Let Your Majesty pay homage to him.

## dn2:8.8
- **PLI**: Appeva nāma devassa bhagavantaṁ payirupāsato cittaṁ pasīdeyyā”ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully in so doing your mind will find peace.”

## dn2:8.9  `cck9.62, km14.120, lv1.125, ms6D_234, msdiv158`
- **PLI**: “Tena hi, samma jīvaka, hatthiyānāni kappāpehī”ti.
- **EN**: “Well then, my dear Jīvaka, have the elephants readied.”

## dn2:9.1
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, devā”ti kho jīvako komārabhacco rañño māgadhassa ajātasattussa vedehiputtassa paṭissuṇitvā pañcamattāni hatthinikāsatāni kappāpetvā rañño ca ārohaṇīyaṁ nāgaṁ, rañño māgadhassa ajātasattussa vedehiputtassa paṭivedesi:
- **EN**: “Yes, Your Majesty,” replied Jīvaka. He had around five hundred female elephants readied, in addition to the king’s bull elephant for riding. Then he informed the king,

## dn2:9.2
- **PLI**: “kappitāni kho te, deva, hatthiyānāni,
- **EN**: “The elephants are ready, Your Majesty.

## dn2:9.3
- **PLI**: yassadāni kālaṁ maññasī”ti.
- **EN**: Please go at your convenience.”

## dn2:10.1  `mc9.50, ms6D_235, msdiv159, pts-cs10`
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto pañcasu hatthinikāsatesu paccekā itthiyo āropetvā ārohaṇīyaṁ nāgaṁ abhiruhitvā ukkāsu dhāriyamānāsu rājagahamhā niyyāsi mahaccarājānubhāvena, yena jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavanaṁ tena pāyāsi.
- **EN**: Then King Ajātasattu had women mounted on each of the five hundred female elephants, while he mounted his bull elephant. With attendants carrying torches, he set out in full royal pomp from Rājagaha to Jīvaka’s Mango Grove.
- **NOTE**: Indian kings were guarded by armed women inside the harem (Kauṭilya’s Arthaśāstra 1.21.1) and also while on hunt (Megasthenes’s Indica, via Strabo XV. i. 53–56). This passage may be the earliest evidence for this long-lasting practice. See also <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn23/en/sujato#15.4'>DN 23:15.4</a>.

## dn2:10.2  `csp1ed6.47, csp2ed6.47, dr9.63, km14.121, lv1.126, ms6D_236, pts-vp-pli1.50, sya9.65`
- **PLI**: Atha kho rañño māgadhassa ajātasattussa vedehiputtassa avidūre ambavanassa ahudeva bhayaṁ, ahu chambhitattaṁ, ahu lomahaṁso.
- **EN**: But as he drew near the mango grove, the king became frightened, scared, his hair standing on end.

## dn2:10.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto bhīto saṁviggo lomahaṭṭhajāto jīvakaṁ komārabhaccaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: He said to Jīvaka,

## dn2:10.4
- **PLI**: “kacci maṁ, samma jīvaka, na vañcesi?
- **EN**: “My dear Jīvaka, I hope you’re not deceiving me!

## dn2:10.5
- **PLI**: Kacci maṁ, samma jīvaka, na palambhesi?
- **EN**: I hope you’re not betraying me!

## dn2:10.6
- **PLI**: Kacci maṁ, samma jīvaka, na paccatthikānaṁ desi?
- **EN**: I hope you’re not turning me over to my enemies!

## dn2:10.7
- **PLI**: Kathañhi nāma tāva mahato bhikkhusaṅghassa aḍḍhateḷasānaṁ bhikkhusatānaṁ neva khipitasaddo bhavissati, na ukkāsitasaddo na nigghoso”ti.
- **EN**: For how on earth can there be no sound of coughing or clearing throats or any noise in such a large Saṅgha of 1,250 mendicants?”
- **NOTE**: The silence of the Buddha’s assembly is often contrasted with the rowdy gatherings of other ascetics, for example that of Poṭṭhapāda at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn9/en/sujato#3.1'>DN 9:3.1</a>.

## dn2:10.8  `ms6D_237, vri1.45`
- **PLI**: “Mā bhāyi, mahārāja, mā bhāyi, mahārāja.
- **EN**: “Do not fear, Great King, do not fear!

## dn2:10.9
- **PLI**: Na taṁ, deva, vañcemi;
- **EN**: I am not deceiving you,

## dn2:10.10
- **PLI**: na taṁ, deva, palambhāmi;
- **EN**: or betraying you,

## dn2:10.11
- **PLI**: na taṁ, deva, paccatthikānaṁ demi.
- **EN**: or turning you over to your enemies.

## dn2:10.12
- **PLI**: Abhikkama, mahārāja, abhikkama, mahārāja, ete maṇḍalamāḷe dīpā jhāyantī”ti.
- **EN**: Go forward, Great King, go forward! Those are lamps shining in the pavilion.”
- **NOTE**: This is a double pun. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dīpa</i> means “lamp” or “island, refuge”, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāyati</i> means “burning” or “meditating”. So it could be rendered, “those are saviors meditating in the pavilion”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Jhāyati</i> is the verb form of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i> (“absorption”), which is the central practice of meditation described below.
