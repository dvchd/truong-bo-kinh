## dn2:29.1  `cck9.73, csp1ed6.54, csp2ed6.54, km14.142, lv1.149, mc9.58, ms6D_256, msdiv177, pts-cs29, vri1.51`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, bhante, nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto maṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: He said:

## dn2:29.2
- **PLI**: ‘idha, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti.
- **EN**: ‘Great king, consider a Jain ascetic who is restrained in the fourfold constraint.
- **NOTE**: While this is a genuine Jain teaching, it has not been identified as a “fourfold restraint”. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn25/en/sujato#16.3'>DN 25:16.3</a> preserves another “fourfold restraint” that is closer to that found in Jainism. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn12/en/sujato#44.1'>MN 12:44.1</a> the Buddha says he once practiced a “four-factored spiritual path” that consisted of Jain-like austerities.

## dn2:29.3
- **PLI**: Kathañca, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti?
- **EN**: And how is a Jain ascetic restrained in the fourfold constraint?

## dn2:29.4
- **PLI**: Idha, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho sabbavārivārito ca hoti, sabbavāriyutto ca, sabbavāridhuto ca, sabbavāriphuṭo ca.
- **EN**: It’s when a Jain ascetic is restrained in all that is to be restrained, is bridled in all that is to be restrained, has shaken off evil in all that is to be restrained, and is curbed in all that is to be restrained.
- **NOTE**: At Isibhāsiyāiṁ 29.19, Vardhamāna (Mahāvīra) teaches that a sage is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>savva-vārīhiṁ vārie</i>, “restrained in all restraints”, which clearly parallels our current passage. In that passage, “restraint” refers to stopping the influx of defilements through the five senses, neither delighting in the pleasant nor loathing the unpleasant. Similarly we find <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vāriya-savva-vāri</i> in the commentary to Sūyagaḍa 1.6.28. | Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vāri</i> as future passive participle (cf. Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vārya</i>). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dhuta</i> in the sense “shaken off (evil by means of ascetic practices)” is a characteristic Jain term. | For <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sabbavāriphuṭo</i> compare <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ophuṭo</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#15.5'>MN 99:15.5</a>. In both cases <i lang='pi' translate='no'>phuṭ</i> appears in a string of terms from the root <i lang='pi' translate='no'>var</i>, and is possibly a corrupted form, or at least has the same meaning.

## dn2:29.5
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti.
- **EN**: That’s how a Jain ascetic is restrained in the fourfold constraint.

## dn2:29.6
- **PLI**: Yato kho, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho evaṁ cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti;
- **EN**: When a Jain ascetic is restrained in the fourfold constraint,

## dn2:29.7
- **PLI**: ayaṁ vuccati, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho gatatto ca yatatto ca ṭhitatto cā’ti.
- **EN**: they’re called a knotless one who is self-realized, self-controlled, and steadfast.’

## dn2:30.1  `dr9.75, km14.143, lv1.150, ms6D_257, msdiv178, ndp6.51, pts-cs30, pts-vp-pli1.58, sya9.76`

- **EN**: And so, when I asked the Jain Ñātika about the fruits of the ascetic life apparent in the present life, he answered with the fourfold constraint.

## dn2:30.2

## dn2:30.4

## dn2:30.5

## dn2:30.6

## dn2:30.7
- **PLI**: Anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā anattamano anattamanavācaṁ anicchāretvā tameva vācaṁ anuggaṇhanto anikkujjanto uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkamiṁ.
- **EN**: I was displeased, but did not express my displeasure. Neither accepting what he said nor contradicting it, I rose from my seat and left.
