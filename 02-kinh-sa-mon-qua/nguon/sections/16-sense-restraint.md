# 4.3.2.1 — Hộ trì các giác quan  (13 đoạn)

## dn2:64.0.1
- **PLI**: 4.3.2. Samādhi
- **EN**: 4.3.2. Immersion

## dn2:64.0.2
- **PLI**: 4.3.2.1. Indriyasaṁvara
- **EN**: 4.3.2.1. Sense Restraint

## dn2:64.1  `cck9.91, dr9.93, km14.177, km14.178, km14.179, lv1.188, lv1.189, lv1.190, mc9.71, ms6D_304, msdiv213, pts-cs64, sya9.94`
- **PLI**: Kathañca, mahārāja, bhikkhu indriyesu guttadvāro hoti?
- **EN**: And how does a mendicant guard the sense doors?
- **NOTE**: Here begins the series of practices that build on moral fundamentals to lay the groundwork for meditation.

## dn2:64.2
- **PLI**: Idha, mahārāja, bhikkhu cakkhunā rūpaṁ disvā na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī.
- **EN**: When a mendicant sees a sight with their eyes, they don’t get caught up in the features and details.

## dn2:64.3
- **PLI**: Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṁ cakkhundriyaṁ asaṁvutaṁ viharantaṁ abhijjhā domanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṁ, tassa saṁvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati cakkhundriyaṁ, cakkhundriye saṁvaraṁ āpajjati.
- **EN**: If the faculty of sight were left unrestrained, bad unskillful qualities of covetousness and displeasure would become overwhelming. For this reason, they practice restraint, protecting the faculty of sight, and achieving its restraint.
- **NOTE**: It is not that one cannot see things, but that, mindful of its effect, one avoids unnecessary stimulation. | “Covetousness and bitterness” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>abhijjhā domanassā</i>) are the strong forms of desire and aversion caused by lack of restraint.

## dn2:64.4
- **PLI**: Sotena saddaṁ sutvā …pe…
- **EN**: When they hear a sound with their ears …

## dn2:64.5
- **PLI**: ghānena gandhaṁ ghāyitvā …pe…
- **EN**: When they smell an odor with their nose …

## dn2:64.6
- **PLI**: jivhāya rasaṁ sāyitvā …pe…
- **EN**: When they taste a flavor with their tongue …

## dn2:64.7
- **PLI**: kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṁ phusitvā …pe…
- **EN**: When they feel a touch with their body …

## dn2:64.8
- **PLI**: manasā dhammaṁ viññāya na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī.
- **EN**: When they know an idea with their mind, they don’t get caught up in the features and details.

## dn2:64.9
- **PLI**: Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṁ manindriyaṁ asaṁvutaṁ viharantaṁ abhijjhā domanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṁ, tassa saṁvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati manindriyaṁ, manindriye saṁvaraṁ āpajjati.
- **EN**: If the faculty of mind were left unrestrained, bad unskillful qualities of covetousness and displeasure would become overwhelming. For this reason, they practice restraint, protecting the faculty of mind, and achieving its restraint.

## dn2:64.10
- **PLI**: So iminā ariyena indriyasaṁvarena samannāgato ajjhattaṁ abyāsekasukhaṁ paṭisaṁvedeti.
- **EN**: When they have this noble sense restraint, they experience an unsullied bliss inside themselves.
- **NOTE**: Their happiness deepens, as they see that not only their actions but also their mind is becoming free of anything unwholesome.

## dn2:64.11
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho, mahārāja, bhikkhu indriyesu guttadvāro hoti.
- **EN**: That’s how a mendicant guards the sense doors.
