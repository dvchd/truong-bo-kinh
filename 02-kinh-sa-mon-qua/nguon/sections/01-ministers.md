# Mục 1 — Cuộc bàn luận với các quan đại thần  (37 đoạn)

## dn2:1.0
- **PLI**: 1. Rājāmaccakathā
- **EN**: 1. A Discussion With the King’s Ministers

## dn2:1.1  `bj7.82, cck9.59, csp1ed6.44, csp2ed6.44, dr9.59, km14.114, km14.115, lv1.117, lv1.118, mc9.47, ms6D_226, msdiv150, ndp6.41, pts-cs1, pts-vp-pli1.47, sya9.61, vri1.42`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta with its commentary was translated by Bhikkhu Bodhi in his <em>The Fruits of Recluseship</em>.

## dn2:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavane mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ aḍḍhateḷasehi bhikkhusatehi.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha in the Mango Grove of Jīvaka Komārabhacca, together with a large Saṅgha of 1,250 mendicants.
- **NOTE**: This monastery belonged to the Buddha’s doctor, Jīvaka, who appears later in the sutta. His story is told in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd8/en/sujato'>Kd 8</a>, where we learn that he was raised by Prince Abhaya of Magadha, a Jain (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn58/en/sujato'>MN 58</a>) who was also interested in the teachings of Pūraṇa Kassapa (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn46.56/en/sujato'>SN 46.56</a>). <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn55/en/sujato'>MN 55</a> on eating meat is addressed to him.

## dn2:1.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto tadahuposathe pannarase komudiyā cātumāsiniyā puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā rājāmaccaparivuto uparipāsādavaragato nisinno hoti.
- **EN**: Now, at that time it was the sabbath—the Komudī full moon on the fifteenth day of the fourth month—and King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of the princess of Videha, was sitting upstairs in the royal longhouse surrounded by his ministers.
- **NOTE**: The Komudī was an especially celebrated full moon on the last month of the rainy season (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kattikā</i>, October/November), when the skies were clear, the lotuses (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kumudā</i>) were in bloom, and the moon was in conjunction with the Pleiades, which gave the month its name.

## dn2:1.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto tadahuposathe udānaṁ udānesi:
- **EN**: Then Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of the princess of Videha, expressed this heartfelt sentiment,
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ajātasattu</i> (“one against whom no foe is born”) was the son of Bimbisāra and heir to the Haryaṅka dynasty of Magadha. In inscription and Jain tradition he is also called Kūṇika. Jain tradition holds that his mother was Cellaṇā daughter of Ceṭaka, a Licchavī ruler from Vesālī. This begs the question as to why Cellaṇā was said to be “of Videha”; perhaps he married a Videhan princess to forge an alliance with the Licchavī’s northern neighbors. In any case, this is more plausible than the Buddhist tradition that his mother was Kosalan.

## dn2:1.5
- **PLI**: “ramaṇīyā vata bho dosinā ratti, abhirūpā vata bho dosinā ratti, dassanīyā vata bho dosinā ratti, pāsādikā vata bho dosinā ratti, lakkhaññā vata bho dosinā ratti.
- **EN**: “Oh, sirs, this moonlit night is so very delightful, so beautiful, so glorious, so lovely, so striking.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pāsādikā</i> here does not mean “tranquil”; it is part of a stock list of terms meaning “beautiful, attractive” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn4/en/sujato#13.7'>DN 4:13.7</a>). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Lakkhaññā</i> is unique in early Pali. It is probably a synonym in the sense of “possessing remarkable features, striking”, rather than “auspicious”.

## dn2:1.6
- **PLI**: Kaṁ nu khvajja samaṇaṁ vā brāhmaṇaṁ vā payirupāseyyāma, yaṁ no payirupāsato cittaṁ pasīdeyyā”ti?
- **EN**: Now, what ascetic or brahmin might I pay homage to today, paying homage to whom my mind might find peace?”
- **NOTE**: The king seeks redemption through his own actions; it is not that the ascetic has any special power to bring peace to his mind. | The sutta is deliberately holding back the reason for the king’s distress. Compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.50/en/sujato'>AN 5.50</a>, where the reason for King Muṇḍa seeking solace is stated up front.

## dn2:2.1  `lv1.119, ms6D_227, msdiv151, pts-cs2`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, aññataro rājāmacco rājānaṁ māgadhaṁ ajātasattuṁ vedehiputtaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he had spoken, one of the king’s ministers said to him,
- **NOTE**: Though the king mentioned “ascetics and brahmins”, his advisers only recommend famous teachers of the ascetics (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇa</i>). For a shorter survey of their doctrines, see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn2.30/en/sujato'>SN 2.30</a>.

## dn2:2.2
- **PLI**: “ayaṁ, deva, pūraṇo kassapo saṅghī ceva gaṇī ca gaṇācariyo ca ñāto yasassī titthakaro sādhusammato bahujanassa rattaññū cirapabbajito addhagato vayoanuppatto.
- **EN**: “Your Majesty, this Pūraṇa Kassapa leads an order and a community, and tutors a community. He’s a well-known and famous religious founder, deemed holy by many people. He is of long standing, long gone forth; he is advanced in years and has reached the final stage of life.
- **NOTE**: A little-known teacher of the inefficacy of action and consequence, Pūraṇa was poorly regarded even by his own students (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#6.19'>MN 77:6.19</a>). He is said to have advocated a doctrine of six classes of rebirth (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.57/en/sujato'>AN 6.57</a>. The same text reverentially mentions Makkhali Gosāla, and it seems Pūraṇa, like Gosāla and Pakudha, was a defining teacher of the Ājīvakas. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pūraṇa</i> means “filling” (not <i lang='pi' translate='no'>purāṇa</i>, “ancient”).

## dn2:2.3
- **PLI**: Taṁ devo pūraṇaṁ kassapaṁ payirupāsatu.
- **EN**: Let Your Majesty pay homage to him.

## dn2:2.4
- **PLI**: Appeva nāma devassa pūraṇaṁ kassapaṁ payirupāsato cittaṁ pasīdeyyā”ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully in so doing your mind will find peace.”

## dn2:2.5
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto tuṇhī ahosi.
- **EN**: But when he had spoken, the king kept silent.
- **NOTE**: The reason for the king’s silence is revealed later. The narrative is full of foreshadowing.

## dn2:3.1  `cck9.60, dr9.60, km14.116, mc9.48, ms6D_228, msdiv152, pts-cs3, pts-vp-pli1.48, sya9.62, vri1.43`

## dn2:3.2
- **PLI**: “ayaṁ, deva, makkhali gosālo saṅghī ceva gaṇī ca gaṇācariyo ca ñāto yasassī titthakaro sādhusammato bahujanassa rattaññū cirapabbajito addhagato vayoanuppatto.
- **EN**: “Your Majesty, this bamboo-staffed ascetic Gosāla leads an order and a community, and tutors a community. He’s a well-known and famous religious founder, deemed holy by many people. He is of long standing, long gone forth; he is advanced in years and has reached the final stage of life.
- **NOTE**: Founder of the Ājīvakas, who became the third largest ascetic movement after Buddhism and Jainism. None of their texts survive, but their teachings can be partially reconstructed from Buddhist and Jain sources. He practiced with Mahāvīra for six years before an acrimonious split, following which he developed his doctrine of hard determinism. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Makkhali</i> denotes a kind of ascetic bearing a bamboo staff, so (like the similar appellations <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nigaṇṭha</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇa</i>) it indicates his affiliation. This being so, and noting that Buddhist Sanskrit texts spell his name as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gośālīputra</i> etc., the second name (like <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nātaputta</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gotama</i>) might indicate his clan. However, I can find no trace of such a clan, and both Jain and Buddhist tradition, albeit unreliably, say the name arose because he was born in a cowshed.

## dn2:7.2
- **PLI**: “ayaṁ, deva, nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto saṅghī ceva gaṇī ca gaṇācariyo ca ñāto yasassī titthakaro sādhusammato bahujanassa rattaññū cirapabbajito addhagato vayoanuppatto.
- **EN**: “Your Majesty, this Jain ascetic of the Ñātika clan leads an order and a community, and tutors a community. He’s a well-known and famous religious founder, deemed holy by many people. He is of long standing, long gone forth; he is advanced in years and has reached the final stage of life.
- **NOTE**: The Jain leader Mahāvīra Vardhamāna is known as Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta in Pali texts. He is regarded as the 24th supreme leader of the Jains, although only he and his predecessor Pārśvanātha (not mentioned in the Pali) are historical. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Nigaṇṭha</i> means “knotless” (i.e. without attachments). As a term for Jain ascetics it is also found in Jain literature. Nātaputta indicates his clan the Ñātikas (Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jñātiputra</i>; Prākrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāyaputta</i>). The Pali tradition has confused <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ñāti</i> (“family”) with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāṭa</i> (“dancer”). Thus Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta means “the Jain monk of the Ñātika clan”. | Jainism and Buddhism are the only ancient <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samaṇa</i> movements to survive to the present day. The primary Jain teaching is the practice of non-violence while burning off past kamma by fervent austerities in order to reach omniscient liberation.
