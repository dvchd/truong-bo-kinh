# Muc 35 — The Eight Liberations  (26 đoạn)

## dn15:35.0
- **PLI**: 6. Aṭṭhavimokkha
- **EN**: 6. The Eight Liberations

## dn15:35.1  `cck10.90, csp1ed7.60, csp2ed7.60, dr10.85, ms7D_238, msdiv129, pts-cs35, pts-vp-pli2.71, sya10.84, vri2.55`
- **PLI**: Aṭṭha kho ime, ānanda, vimokkhā.
- **EN**: Ānanda, there are these eight liberations.
- **NOTE**: The eight liberations (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vimokkhā</i>) are an alternative way of describing the meditative experiences of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>. Elsewhere they are listed at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.33.1'>DN 16:3.33.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn33/en/sujato#3.1.168'>DN 33:3.1.168</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn34/en/sujato#2.1.191'>DN 34:2.1.191</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.66/en/sujato'>AN 8.66</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn77/en/sujato#22.1'>MN 77:22.1</a>, and referred to at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.189/en/sujato#1.8'>AN 4.189:1.8</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag20.1/en/sujato#33.1'>Thag 20.1:33.1</a>. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an8.120/en/sujato'>AN 8.120</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn137/en/sujato#27.1'>MN 137:27.1</a> they are listed but not called the eight liberations.

## dn15:35.2
- **PLI**: Katame aṭṭha?
- **EN**: What eight?

## dn15:35.3
- **PLI**: Rūpī rūpāni passati
- **EN**: Having physical form, they see forms.
- **NOTE**: Someone sees a meditative vision based on the perception of their own body, such as through mindfulness of breathing or one’s own body parts. The first three liberations all cover the four <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhānas</i>.

## dn15:35.4
- **PLI**: ayaṁ paṭhamo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the first liberation.

## dn15:35.5
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ arūpasaññī bahiddhā rūpāni passati,
- **EN**: Not perceiving form internally, they see forms externally.
- **NOTE**: A meditator grounds their practice on some external focus, such as a light, the sight of a corpse, or an external element such as earth.

## dn15:35.6
- **PLI**: ayaṁ dutiyo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the second liberation.

## dn15:35.7
- **PLI**: Subhanteva adhimutto hoti,
- **EN**: They’re focused only on beauty.
- **NOTE**: This is a practice based on wholly pure and exalted meditation, such as the meditation on love, or the sight of a pure brilliant color like the sky.

## dn15:35.8
- **PLI**: ayaṁ tatiyo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the third liberation.

## dn15:35.9
- **PLI**: Sabbaso rūpasaññānaṁ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṁ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṁ amanasikārā ‘ananto ākāso’ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: Going totally beyond perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of impingement, not focusing on perceptions of diversity, aware that ‘space is infinite’, they enter and remain in the dimension of infinite space.

## dn15:35.10
- **PLI**: ayaṁ catuttho vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the fourth liberation.

## dn15:35.11
- **PLI**: Sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṁ samatikkamma ‘anantaṁ viññāṇan’ti viññāṇañcāyatanaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: Going totally beyond the dimension of infinite space, aware that ‘consciousness is infinite’, they enter and remain in the dimension of infinite consciousness.

## dn15:35.12
- **PLI**: ayaṁ pañcamo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the fifth liberation.

## dn15:35.13
- **PLI**: Sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṁ samatikkamma ‘natthi kiñcī’ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: Going totally beyond the dimension of infinite consciousness, aware that ‘there is nothing at all’, they enter and remain in the dimension of nothingness.

## dn15:35.14
- **PLI**: ayaṁ chaṭṭho vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the sixth liberation.

## dn15:35.15
- **PLI**: Sabbaso ākiñcaññāyatanaṁ samatikkamma nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: Going totally beyond the dimension of nothingness, they enter and remain in the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.

## dn15:35.16
- **PLI**: ayaṁ sattamo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the seventh liberation.

## dn15:35.17
- **PLI**: Sabbaso nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṁ samatikkamma saññāvedayitanirodhaṁ upasampajja viharati,
- **EN**: Going totally beyond the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception, they enter and remain in the cessation of perception and feeling.
- **NOTE**: The “cessation of perception and feeling” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññāvedayitanirodha</i>) is a culminating meditation state of supreme subtlety that often leads directly to awakening (but see <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an5.166/en/sujato'>AN 5.166</a>). The state itself, like all meditation states, is temporary, but afterwards the defilements can be eliminated forever. This liberating insight is the consequence of the balanced development of all eight factors of the path.

## dn15:35.18
- **PLI**: ayaṁ aṭṭhamo vimokkho.
- **EN**: This is the eighth liberation.

## dn15:35.19
- **PLI**: Ime kho, ānanda, aṭṭha vimokkhā.
- **EN**: These are the eight liberations.

## dn15:36.1  `bj8.108, mc10.64, ms7D_239, msdiv130, ndp7.57, pts-cs36`
- **PLI**: Yato kho, ānanda, bhikkhu ime aṭṭha vimokkhe anulomampi samāpajjati, paṭilomampi samāpajjati, anulomapaṭilomampi samāpajjati, yatthicchakaṁ yadicchakaṁ yāvaticchakaṁ samāpajjatipi vuṭṭhātipi.
- **EN**: When a mendicant enters into and withdraws from these eight liberations—in forward order, in reverse order, and in forward and reverse order—wherever they wish, whenever they wish, and for as long as they wish;
- **NOTE**: This passage emphasizes that this person is fully adept and has mastered all the states of meditation. The Buddha claimed such mastery (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an9.41/en/sujato#16.1'>AN 9.41:16.1</a>), and retained the ability even on his deathbed (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#6.8.1'>DN 16:6.8.1</a>).

## dn15:36.2
- **PLI**: Āsavānañca khayā anāsavaṁ cetovimuttiṁ paññāvimuttiṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati, ayaṁ vuccatānanda, bhikkhu ubhatobhāgavimutto.
- **EN**: and when they realize the undefiled freedom of heart and freedom by wisdom, and live having realized it with their own insight in this very life due to the ending of defilements, they’re called a mendicant who is freed both ways.
- **NOTE**: Here we see the terms “one who is freed” used in two ways. All arahants have “freedom of heart” (by means of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>) and “freedom by wisdom” (the realization of the Dhamma). At the same time, one who emphasizes <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> is said to have “freedom of heart” in contrast with one who emphasizes wisdom, who has “freedom by wisdom”. One who has consummate mastery of both <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> and wisdom is said to be “freed both ways”.

## dn15:36.3
- **PLI**: Imāya ca, ānanda, ubhatobhāgavimuttiyā aññā ubhatobhāgavimutti uttaritarā vā paṇītatarā vā natthī”ti.
- **EN**: And, Ānanda, there is no other freedom both ways that is better or finer than this.”
