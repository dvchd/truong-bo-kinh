# Muc 14 — The Courses of Fervent Mortification  (24 đoạn)

## dn8:14.0
- **PLI**: 3. Tapopakkamakathā
- **EN**: 3. The Courses of Fervent Mortification

- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, acelo kassapo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he had spoken, Kassapa said to the Buddha:

## dn8:14.2
- **PLI**: “imepi kho, āvuso gotama, tapopakkamā etesaṁ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṁ sāmaññasaṅkhātā ca brahmaññasaṅkhātā ca.
- **EN**: “Reverend Gotama, those ascetics and brahmins consider these courses of fervent mortification to be what makes someone a true ascetic or brahmin.
- **NOTE**: What follows is a description of ascetic practices undertaken by the Jains and similar groups. | The phrase “course of fervent mortification” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>tapopakkama</i>) is unique to this sutta. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pakkama</i> means “stepping out”.

## dn8:14.3
- **PLI**: Acelako hoti, muttācāro, hatthāpalekhano, naehibhaddantiko, natiṭṭhabhaddantiko, nābhihaṭaṁ, na uddissakataṁ, na nimantanaṁ sādiyati.
- **EN**: They go naked, ignoring conventions. They lick their hands, and don’t come or wait when called. They don’t consent to food brought to them, or food prepared on their behalf, or an invitation for a meal.
- **NOTE**: Buddhist mendicants may not receive food in their hands, nor lick them while eating. Followers of the practices listed here would have walked steadily and randomly for alms, accepting only what was given at the time.

## dn8:14.4
- **PLI**: So na kumbhimukhā paṭiggaṇhāti, na kaḷopimukhā paṭiggaṇhāti, na eḷakamantaraṁ, na daṇḍamantaraṁ, na musalamantaraṁ, na dvinnaṁ bhuñjamānānaṁ, na gabbhiniyā, na pāyamānāya, na purisantaragatāya, na saṅkittīsu, na yattha sā upaṭṭhito hoti, na yattha makkhikā saṇḍasaṇḍacārinī, na macchaṁ, na maṁsaṁ, na suraṁ, na merayaṁ, na thusodakaṁ pivati.
- **EN**: They don’t receive anything from a pot or bowl; or from someone who keeps sheep, or who has a weapon or a shovel in their home; or where a couple is eating; or where there is a woman who is pregnant, breastfeeding, or who lives with a man; or where there’s a hound waiting or flies buzzing. They accept no fish or meat or beer or wine, and drink no fermented gruel.
- **NOTE**: Keeping sheep (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>eḷaka</i>, for slaughter) goes against the Jain principle of non-violence, as does keeping weapons (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>daṇḍa</i>). | A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>musala</i> often means “pestle”, but it can also be a “shovel”; at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn81/en/sujato#18.12'>MN 81:18.12</a> it is regarded as a virtue to not use one to dig the soil (which is regarded as being alive in Jainism). | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Thusodaka</i> is an alcoholic porridge fermented from grain-husks, mentioned alongside <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sovīraka</i> in the Pali commentaries and Carakasaṁhitā 27g.191.

## dn8:14.5
- **PLI**: So ekāgāriko vā hoti ekālopiko, dvāgāriko vā hoti dvālopiko … sattāgāriko vā hoti sattālopiko;
- **EN**: They go to just one house for alms, taking just one mouthful, or two houses and two mouthfuls, up to seven houses and seven mouthfuls.

## dn8:14.6
- **PLI**: ekissāpi dattiyā yāpeti, dvīhipi dattīhi yāpeti … sattahipi dattīhi yāpeti;
- **EN**: They feed on one saucer a day, two saucers a day, up to seven saucers a day.

## dn8:14.7
- **PLI**: ekāhikampi āhāraṁ āhāreti, dvīhikampi āhāraṁ āhāreti … sattāhikampi āhāraṁ āhāreti. Iti evarūpaṁ addhamāsikampi pariyāyabhattabhojanānuyogamanuyutto viharati.
- **EN**: They eat once a day, once every second day, up to once a week, and so on, even up to once a fortnight. They live committed to the practice of eating food at set intervals.

## dn8:14.12
- **PLI**: kesamassulocakopi hoti kesamassulocanānuyogamanuyutto,
- **EN**: They tear out hair and beard, committed to this practice.
- **NOTE**: Jain ascetics tear out their hair at ordination, rather than shaving.

## dn8:14.13
- **PLI**: ubbhaṭṭhakopi hoti āsanapaṭikkhitto,
- **EN**: They constantly stand, turning down seats.
- **NOTE**: Remaining in one posture for months or years at a time is one of the most difficult practices.

## dn8:14.14

## dn8:14.21
- **PLI**: vekaṭikopi hoti vikaṭabhojanānuyogamanuyutto,
- **EN**: They eat unnatural things, committed to the practice of eating unnatural foods.
- **NOTE**: At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd6/en/sujato#14.6.3'>Kd 6:14.6.3</a> the four “great unnaturals” (or “filthy edibles”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>mahāvikaṭa</i>) are said to be feces, urine, ash, and clay. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn12/en/sujato#49.3'>MN 12:49.3</a> the Buddha said he ate the “unnatural things” of feces and urine when undertaking ascetic practices.

## dn8:14.22
- **PLI**: apānakopi hoti apānakattamanuyutto,
- **EN**: They don’t drink, committed to the practice of not drinking liquids.

## dn8:14.23
- **PLI**: sāyatatiyakampi udakorohanānuyogamanuyutto viharatī”ti.
- **EN**: They’re devoted to ritual bathing three times a day, including at dusk.”
- **NOTE**: This seems out of place here. It was a Brahmanical practice (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn7.21/en/sujato'>SN 7.21</a>), as the Jains refused to bathe at all. Indeed, bathing three times a day in the Indian climate would, for most of the year, be quite pleasant.
