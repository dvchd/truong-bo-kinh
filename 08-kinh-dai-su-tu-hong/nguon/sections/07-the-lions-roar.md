## dn8:21.5  `csp1ed6.164, csp2ed6.164, ms6D_675`
- **PLI**: Santi, kassapa, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā tapojigucchāvādā.
- **EN**: There are, Kassapa, some ascetics and brahmins who teach mortification in disgust of sin.
- **NOTE**: “Mortification in disgust of sin” renders <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tapojigucchā</i>; <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tapo</i> is “fervent mortification” and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jigucchā</i> is “loathing, disgust”. It captures the severity with which practitioners regarded the “evil” or “sin” with which they were infected, like a quasi-physical stain on the soul, and the burning flame of white-hot pain required to cauterize their spiritual wounds.

## dn8:21.6
- **PLI**: Te anekapariyāyena tapojigucchāya vaṇṇaṁ bhāsanti.
- **EN**: They praise fervent mortification in disgust of sin in many ways.

- **EN**: But as far as the highest noble fervent mortification in disgust of sin goes, I don’t see anyone who’s my equal, still less my superior.

## dn8:21.8

- **EN**: Rather, I am the one who is superior when it comes to the higher disgust of sin.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha repurposes the concept of “disgust of sin”, which here stands in the place of meditation (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>).
