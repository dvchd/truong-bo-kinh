# P1_FRAME  (36 segments)

## dn1:1.0
- **PLI**: 1. Paribbājakakathā 
- **EN**: 1. Talk on Wanderers 

## dn1:1.1.1  `bj7.2, cck9.1, csp1ed6.1, csp2ed6.1, dr9.1, km14.1, km14.2, lv1.1, lv1.2, mc9.1, ms6D_2, msdiv1, ndp6.3, pts-cs1.1, pts-vp-pli1.1, sya9.1, vri1.1, vri1.2`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard. 
- **NOTE**: Tradition holds that these were the words spoken by Ānanda when reciting the Suttapiṭaka at the First Council following the Buddha’s death. In fact it is a tag signifying that the text has been passed down through oral tradition and the speaker was not present at the events (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn5/en/sujato#21.10'>DN 5:21.10</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn127/en/sujato#17.4'>MN 127:17.4</a>). | This sutta with its commentary was translated by Bhikkhu Bodhi in his <em>The All-Embracing Net of Views</em>. 

## dn1:1.1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā antarā ca rājagahaṁ antarā ca nāḷandaṁ addhānamaggappaṭipanno hoti mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi. 
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was traveling along the road between Rājagaha and Nāḷandā together with a large Saṅgha of five hundred mendicants. 
- **NOTE**: By convention, suttas do not specify the date, so we have scant internal chronology. | Walk about fifteen kilometers north from Rājagaha (modern Rajgir) to reach Nāḷandā. | “Mendicant” is a literal translation of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhikkhu</i>, one who goes for alms. 

## dn1:1.1.3
- **PLI**: Suppiyopi kho paribbājako antarā ca rājagahaṁ antarā ca nāḷandaṁ addhānamaggappaṭipanno hoti saddhiṁ antevāsinā brahmadattena māṇavena. 
- **EN**: The wanderer Suppiya was also traveling along the same road, together with his resident pupil, the student Brahmadatta. 
- **NOTE**: A “wanderer” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paribbājaka</i>) was a homeless religious renunciate—male or female—who wandered seeking alms. Kauṭilya describes their lifestyle as “mastery of the senses, abstaining from ritual activities, living without possessions, abandoning attachments, practicing mendicancy, residing in the wilderness but not in a single place, and purifying himself internally and externally” (Arthaśāstra 1.3.12, Olivelle’s translation). | “Resident pupil” (or elsewhere just “pupil”) is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>antevāsi</i>, a live-in apprentice of a master. | “Student” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>māṇava</i>) is a young man who was learning the Vedas from a master. 

## dn1:1.1.4
- **PLI**: Tatra sudaṁ suppiyo paribbājako anekapariyāyena buddhassa avaṇṇaṁ bhāsati, dhammassa avaṇṇaṁ bhāsati, saṅghassa avaṇṇaṁ bhāsati; 
- **EN**: Meanwhile, Suppiya criticized the Buddha, the teaching, and the Saṅgha in many ways, 
- **NOTE**: Today these are called the Triple Gem that makes up the Buddhist religion; however they are not known by that term in the early texts. 

## dn1:1.1.5
- **PLI**: suppiyassa pana paribbājakassa antevāsī brahmadatto māṇavo anekapariyāyena buddhassa vaṇṇaṁ bhāsati, dhammassa vaṇṇaṁ bhāsati, saṅghassa vaṇṇaṁ bhāsati. 
- **EN**: but his pupil Brahmadatta praised them in many ways. 
- **NOTE**: While the disagreement of student and teacher signifies their confusion, it also represents the diversity of views within the brahmanical caste and the openness with which a student could disagree with their teacher. 

## dn1:1.1.6
- **PLI**: Itiha te ubho ācariyantevāsī aññamaññassa ujuvipaccanīkavādā bhagavantaṁ piṭṭhito piṭṭhito anubandhā honti bhikkhusaṅghañca. 
- **EN**: And so both tutor and pupil followed behind the Buddha and the Saṅgha of mendicants directly contradicting each other. 

## dn1:1.2.1  `cck9.2, csp1ed6.2, csp2ed6.2, dr9.2, km14.3, lv1.3, mc9.2, ms6D_3, msdiv2, ndp6.4, pts-cs1.2, pts-vp-pli1.2, sya9.2`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā ambalaṭṭhikāyaṁ rājāgārake ekarattivāsaṁ upagacchi saddhiṁ bhikkhusaṅghena. 
- **EN**: Then the Buddha took up residence for the night in the royal rest-house in Ambalaṭṭhikā together with the Saṅgha of mendicants. 
- **NOTE**: Ambalaṭṭhikā means “place of mango saplings”. It was a rest-house set up by the king of Rājagaha about a day’s journey from the capital. It must have been sizable.

## dn1:1.3.1  `bj7.4, cck9.3, km14.4, lv1.4, ms6D_4, msdiv3, pts-cs1.3, sya9.3`
- **PLI**: Atha kho sambahulānaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ rattiyā paccūsasamayaṁ paccuṭṭhitānaṁ maṇḍalamāḷe sannisinnānaṁ sannipatitānaṁ ayaṁ saṅkhiyadhammo udapādi: 
- **EN**: Then several mendicants rose at the crack of dawn and sat together in the pavilion, where the topic of judgmentalism came up: 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṅkhiyadhamma</i> is a unique term. The commentary glosses it as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kathādhamma</i>, following which it has been translated as “conversation” or “trend of conversation”. But <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkhā</i> means “appraisal, assessment, evaluation, measuring, calculating”, and here the subject of discussion is the different ways the two parties assess or judge the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha. Compare the “appraisal” of the Buddha at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn19/en/sujato#19.2'>DN 19:19.2</a>. 

## dn1:1.3.2

- **EN**: “It’s incredible, reverends, it’s amazing how the diverse convictions of sentient beings have been well deciphered by the Blessed One, who knows and sees, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha. 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Adhimutti</i> is something that has been decided, a conviction or belief.

## dn1:1.4.1  `csp1ed6.3, csp2ed6.3, dr9.3, km14.5, lv1.5, mc9.3, ms6D_5, msdiv4, pts-cs1.4, vri1.3`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā tesaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ imaṁ saṅkhiyadhammaṁ viditvā yena maṇḍalamāḷo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Nisajja kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi: 
- **EN**: When the Buddha found out about this discussion on judgmentalism among the mendicants, he went to the pavilion, where he sat on the seat spread out and addressed the mendicants: 
- **NOTE**: This would have been an open air pavilion in the rest-house. By convention, when a teacher or other respected person is to sit, a sitting mat is spread out or made ready for them. 

## dn1:1.4.2
- **PLI**: “kāya nuttha, bhikkhave, etarahi kathāya sannisinnā sannipatitā, kā ca pana vo antarākathā vippakatā”ti? 
- **EN**: “Mendicants, what were you sitting talking about just now? What conversation was left unfinished?” 
- **NOTE**: The very first words of the Buddha in the Suttapiṭaka: he asks to hear what others are saying.

## dn1:1.5.1  `cck9.4, dr9.4, km14.6, lv1.6, ms6D_6, msdiv5, ndp6.5, pts-cs1.5, pts-vp-pli1.3, sya9.4`
- **PLI**: “Mamaṁ vā, bhikkhave, pare avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, dhammassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, saṅghassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, tatra tumhehi na āghāto na appaccayo na cetaso anabhiraddhi karaṇīyā. 
- **EN**: “Mendicants, if others criticize me, the teaching, or the Saṅgha, don’t make yourselves resentful, bitter, and exasperated. 
- **NOTE**: The phrasing here is somewhat unusual and specific. They “should not do” what creates bitterness (i.e. judging others). Compare <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn22/en/sujato'>MN 22</a>, where the same phrases are used. In the Buddha’s case, it has the neutral <i lang='pi' translate='no'>hoti</i>, while for the mendicants it uses <i lang='pi' translate='no'>karaṇīya</i>, as here. 

## dn1:1.5.2
- **PLI**: Mamaṁ vā, bhikkhave, pare avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, dhammassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, saṅghassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, tatra ce tumhe assatha kupitā vā anattamanā vā, tumhaṁ yevassa tena antarāyo. 
- **EN**: You’ll get angry and upset, which would be an obstacle for you alone. 
- **NOTE**: Complaining about others does not hurt them, only the one who gets upset. 

## dn1:1.5.3
- **PLI**: Mamaṁ vā, bhikkhave, pare avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, dhammassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, saṅghassa vā avaṇṇaṁ bhāseyyuṁ, tatra ce tumhe assatha kupitā vā anattamanā vā, api nu tumhe paresaṁ subhāsitaṁ dubbhāsitaṁ ājāneyyāthā”ti? 
- **EN**: If others were to criticize me, the teaching, or the Saṅgha, and you got angry and upset, would you be able to understand whether they spoke well or poorly?” 
- **NOTE**: Equanimity is a prerequisite for evaluating facts. 

## dn1:1.5.4
- **PLI**: “No hetaṁ, bhante”. 
- **EN**: “No, sir.”
