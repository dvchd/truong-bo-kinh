## dn1:1.30.0
- **PLI**: 3.1.1. Sassatavāda 
- **EN**: 3.1.1. Eternalism 

## dn1:1.30.1  `bj7.22, lv1.31, ms6D_59, msdiv30, pts-cs1.30`
- **PLI**: Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sassatavādā, sassataṁ attānañca lokañca paññapenti catūhi vatthūhi. 
- **EN**: There are some ascetics and brahmins who are eternalists, who assert that the self and the cosmos are eternal on four grounds. 
- **NOTE**: In such contexts, the “self” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>attā</i>) is a postulated metaphysical entity rather than a simple psychological sense of personal identity. The nature of this “self” or “soul” was endlessly debated. The Buddha rejected all theories of a “self”, and elsewhere it is said that “identity view” underlies all sixty-two views of the Brahmajāla (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn41.3/en/sujato#4.13'>SN 41.3:4.13</a>). | The “cosmos” is the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>loka</i>, otherwise translated as “world”. This sometimes refers to the simple physical realm, sometimes to the world of experience, or else, as here, the vast universe as conceived in ancient Indian thought.

- **EN**: And what are the four grounds on which they rely? 

## dn1:1.31.1  `cck9.17, dr9.17, km14.32, lv1.32, mc9.14, ms6D_60, msdiv31, pts-cs1.31, pts-vp-pli1.14, sya9.17`
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā ātappamanvāya padhānamanvāya anuyogamanvāya appamādamanvāya sammāmanasikāramanvāya tathārūpaṁ cetosamādhiṁ phusati, yathāsamāhite citte (…) anekavihitaṁ pubbenivāsaṁ anussarati. 
- **EN**: It’s when some ascetic or brahmin—by dint of keen, resolute, committed, and diligent effort, and right application of mind—experiences an immersion of the heart of such a kind that they recollect their many kinds of past lives. 
- **NOTE**: “Immersion” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i>) is deep meditative stillness. The word conveys the sense of “gathered”, “collected”, with a secondary sense of “ignited”, “illuminated”. The practice of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> (or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>, “absorption”) has never been regarded as uniquely Buddhist. However, right meditation begins with right view. Since these meditators begin with wrong view, their <i lang='pi' translate='no'>samādhi</i> is “wrong” because it merely reinforces their error. | These are those who rely on the “known” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>viññāta</i>) in the epistemic tetrad (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato#19.1'>MN 1:19.1</a>).

## dn1:1.31.4
- **PLI**: ‘sassato attā ca loko ca vañjho kūṭaṭṭho esikaṭṭhāyiṭṭhito; 
- **EN**: ‘The self and the cosmos are eternal, barren, steady as a mountain peak, standing firm like a pillar. 
- **NOTE**: This is the Upaniṣadic view of the eternal <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ātman</i> that is the immanent soul of the world or cosmos, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>loka</i>. Elsewhere in the suttas such theorists assert that the self and the cosmos are identical (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn24.3/en/sujato#1.3'>SN 24.3:1.3</a>: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>so attā so loko</i>).

- **NOTE**: The eternal “self” is contrasted with the ephemeral lives of beings. | The famous word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṁsara</i> is often understood as a “cycle” of rebirths, but the meaning is, rather, to “wander on” or “transmigrate”. | For the phrase <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sassatisamaṁ</i> (“lasting forever and ever”), compare Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.10.1: “He reaches that world free of sorrow and snow, where he lives forever and ever” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa lokam āgacchaty aśokam ahimam | tasmin vasati śāśvatīḥ samāḥ</i>).

## dn1:1.35.2
- **PLI**: Ye hi keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā sassatavādā sassataṁ attānañca lokañca paññapenti, sabbe te imeheva catūhi vatthūhi, etesaṁ vā aññatarena; natthi ito bahiddhā.

- **NOTE**: I have my doubts about this phrase. Everywhere else, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ito bahiddhā</i> means “outside of the Buddhist community”, not “outside of the cases just considered”. Still, the commentary and the Chinese parallel at T 21 agree on this sense. 

## dn1:1.36.1  `csp1ed6.16, csp2ed6.16, ms6D_71, msdiv36, pts-cs1.36, pts-vp-pli1.17`
- **PLI**: Tayidaṁ, bhikkhave, tathāgato pajānāti: 
- **EN**: The Realized One understands this: 

## dn1:1.36.2
- **PLI**: ‘ime diṭṭhiṭṭhānā evaṅgahitā evaṁparāmaṭṭhā evaṅgatikā bhavanti evaṁabhisamparāyā’ti, 
- **EN**: ‘If you hold on to and attach to these grounds for views it leads to such a destiny in the next life.’ 
- **NOTE**: A “view” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṭṭhi</i>) is a relatively fixed framework for understanding the world; a “theory”. The “grounds for views” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>diṭṭhiṭṭhānāni</i>) are the bases from which the views are derived. In this case these are the meditative experiences or the logical reasoning. 

## dn1:1.36.3
- **PLI**: tañca tathāgato pajānāti, tato ca uttaritaraṁ pajānāti; tañca pajānanaṁ na parāmasati, aparāmasato cassa paccattaññeva nibbuti viditā. 
- **EN**: He understands this, and what goes beyond this. And since he does not misapprehend that understanding, he has realized quenching within himself. 
- **NOTE**: The word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parāmasati</i> means “to take hold” and is often used in the sense “to misapprehend”. 

## dn1:1.36.4
- **PLI**: Vedanānaṁ samudayañca atthaṅgamañca assādañca ādīnavañca nissaraṇañca yathābhūtaṁ viditvā anupādāvimutto, bhikkhave, tathāgato. 
- **EN**: Having truly understood the origin, disappearance, gratification, drawback, and escape from feelings, the Realized One is freed by not grasping. 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Yathābhūtaṁ</i> is often translated as “as it really is”, while I usually render it simply as “truly”. It often has a technical sense of seeing “how things came to be (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhuta</i>)” as a process of conditionality (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.31/en/sujato#7.1'>SN 12.31:7.1</a>). Such direct vision of the truth is an attribute of the stream-enterer, who has realized the first of the four stages of awakening, in contrast with those on the path who still rely on faith or inference (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn25.1/en/sujato'>SN 25.1</a>). Here it refers to the understanding of feelings from a fivefold perspective. Feelings underlie intellectual theories and arguments, which serve to sate cravings and fears.
