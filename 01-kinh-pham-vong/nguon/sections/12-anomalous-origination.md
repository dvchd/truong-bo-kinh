## dn1:2.30.0
- **PLI**: 3.1.5. Adhiccasamuppannavāda 
- **EN**: 3.1.5. Doctrines of Anomalous Origination 

## dn1:2.30.1  `csp1ed6.27, csp2ed6.27, mc9.29, ms6D_110, msdiv67, pts-cs2.30`
- **PLI**: Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā adhiccasamuppannikā adhiccasamuppannaṁ attānañca lokañca paññapenti dvīhi vatthūhi. 
- **EN**: There are some ascetics and brahmins who speculate and theorize about anomalous origination. They assert that the self and the cosmos arose anomalously on two grounds. 
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Adhicca</i> is usually translated in this context as “chance”. The root meaning refers to something that has been “lifted out”, making it a “special exception” or “anomaly”. It isn’t used in Sanskrit for a philosophical theory of chance, for which we find instead <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yadṛcchā</i> (“arbitrariness”, Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 1.2). The theory proposed here is not that the universe in general operates by chance, but that the happenstance of creation is an “anomaly” in the general scheme of things. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.24/en/sujato#3.4'>SN 12.24:3.4</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn12.25/en/sujato#2.4'>SN 12.25:2.4</a> we learn that some philosophers who believed in the doctrine of kamma nonetheless argued that suffering arose anomalously.

- **EN**: And what are the two grounds on which they rely?

- **EN**: When perception arises they pass away from that host of gods.

- **EN**: It’s possible that one of those beings passes away from that host and is reborn in this place. 

## dn1:2.31.4
- **PLI**: Itthattaṁ āgato samāno agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajati. 
- **EN**: Having done so, they go forth from the lay life to homelessness. 

## dn1:2.31.5
- **PLI**: Agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito samāno ātappamanvāya padhānamanvāya anuyogamanvāya appamādamanvāya sammāmanasikāramanvāya tathārūpaṁ cetosamādhiṁ phusati, yathāsamāhite citte saññuppādaṁ anussarati, tato paraṁ nānussarati. 
- **EN**: By dint of keen, resolute, committed, and diligent effort, and right application of mind, they experience an immersion of the heart of such a kind that they recollect the arising of perception, but no further.

- **EN**: They say: 

## dn1:2.31.7

## dn1:2.31.8
- **PLI**: Taṁ kissa hetu? 
- **EN**: Why is that? 

## dn1:2.31.9
- **PLI**: Ahañhi pubbe nāhosiṁ, somhi etarahi ahutvā santatāya pariṇato’ti. 
- **EN**: Because formerly I didn’t exist, whereas now, having not existed, I’ve transformed into the state of existing.’ 
- **NOTE**: The idea of creation from nothingness is discussed at Rig Veda 10.129, Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.2. 

## dn1:2.31.10
