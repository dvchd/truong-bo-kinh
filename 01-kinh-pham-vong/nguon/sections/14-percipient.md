## dn1:2.38.3  `ms6D_120, msdiv76`
- **PLI**: ‘Rūpī attā hoti arogo paraṁ maraṇā saññī’ti naṁ paññapenti. 
- **EN**: They assert: ‘The self is healthy and percipient after death, and it is formed … 
- **NOTE**: Usually a self is conceived of as percipient, so that the subject experiences a continuity. | The term <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aroga</i> (“healthy”) is explained by the commentary as “permanent” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nicca</i>), drawing on the root sense of the word, “unbroken”. However, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>aroga</i> is always used in the sense “free of disease, well, healthy” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn97/en/sujato#2.4'>MN 97:2.4</a>), and this applies to the Brahmanical tradition as well as the Buddhist. Chandogya Upaniṣad 7.26.2 says that one who sees (the self) does not see death, they have no disease or pain. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.12 similarly says that one who sees the self will not suffer in the wake of the body, which Śaṅkāra explains, “Struggling with desires for himself, for his son, for his wife, and so on, he is born and dies again and again, and is diseased when his body is diseased.”
