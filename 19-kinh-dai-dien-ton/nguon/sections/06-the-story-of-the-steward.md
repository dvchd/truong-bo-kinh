# Muc 28.7 — The Story of the Steward  (16 đoạn)

## dn19:28.7.0
- **PLI**: 5. Govindabrāhmaṇavatthu
- **EN**: 5. The Story of the Steward

## dn19:28.8  `ms7D_684, msdiv304`
- **PLI**: ‘Taṁ kiṁ maññanti, bhonto devā tāvatiṁsā, yāva dīgharattaṁ mahāpaññova so bhagavā ahosi.
- **EN**: ‘What do the gods of the thirty-three think about the extent of the Buddha’s great wisdom?

## dn19:29.1  `cck10.280, dr10.261, mc10.198, ms7D_685, ndp7.173, pts-cs29, pts-vp-pli2.231, sya10.264, vri2.170`
- **PLI**: Bhūtapubbaṁ, bho, rājā disampati nāma ahosi.
- **EN**: Once upon a time, there was a king named Disampati.
- **NOTE**: Disampati means “head of the directions”, i.e. king of all the land. He and his story are found nowhere else in the early texts.

## dn19:29.2
- **PLI**: Disampatissa rañño govindo nāma brāhmaṇo purohito ahosi.
- **EN**: He had a brahmin high priest named the Steward.
- **NOTE**: “Steward” is Govinda, literally “lord of cows”. The “high priest” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>purohita</i>) was a hereditary office, a learned ritualist and adviser attached to a specific family. For royal families the post could be highly contested. The closeness of the role is shown in that aristocratic families could be referred to by the lineage of the high priest.

## dn19:29.3
- **PLI**: Disampatissa rañño reṇu nāma kumāro putto ahosi.
- **EN**: Disampati’s son was the prince named Reṇu,
- **NOTE**: While the current sutta does not explicitly identify their realm, Dīpavaṁsa iii 40 records Disampati and Reṇu as kings of Kāsi. This fits with the geographical layout depicted in this sutta, with Kāsi at the center. The story, then, depicts the establishment of Brahmanical kings across India from Kāsi.

## dn19:29.4
- **PLI**: Govindassa brāhmaṇassa jotipālo nāma māṇavo putto ahosi.
- **EN**: while the Steward’s son was the student named Jotipāla.
- **NOTE**: Jotipāla means “guardian of the sacred flame”, i.e. someone who maintains the Vedic fire ritual (see below at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn19/en/sujato#47.26'>DN 19:47.26</a>). There was another Jotipāla in the time of Buddha Kassapa at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn81/en/sujato#6.2'>MN 81:6.2</a>, and another who was a religious founder of the past (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.54/en/sujato#18.1'>AN 6.54:18.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an7.73/en/sujato#2.5'>AN 7.73:2.5</a>). Despite the popularity of the name in Pali, it does not seem to appear in Sanskrit sources. Given that it appears in Pali only in legendary contexts, it is probably a vocational epithet.

## dn19:29.5
- **PLI**: Iti reṇu ca rājaputto jotipālo ca māṇavo aññe ca cha khattiyā iccete aṭṭha sahāyā ahesuṁ.
- **EN**: There were Reṇu the prince, Jotipāla the student, and six other aristocrats; these eight became friends.

## dn19:29.6
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bho, ahorattānaṁ accayena govindo brāhmaṇo kālamakāsi.
- **EN**: In due course the brahmin Steward passed away.

## dn19:29.7
- **PLI**: Govinde brāhmaṇe kālaṅkate rājā disampati paridevesi:
- **EN**: At his passing, King Disampati lamented,

## dn19:29.8
- **PLI**: “yasmiṁ vata, bho, mayaṁ samaye govinde brāhmaṇe sabbakiccāni sammā vossajjitvā pañcahi kāmaguṇehi samappitā samaṅgībhūtā paricārema, tasmiṁ no samaye govindo brāhmaṇo kālaṅkato”ti.
- **EN**: “At a time when I have relinquished all my duties to the brahmin Steward and amuse myself, supplied and provided with the five kinds of sensual stimulation, he passes away!”
- **NOTE**: The king’s only care, it seems, is not for the passing of his friend, but that he no longer gets to indulge in whatever he wants.

## dn19:29.9
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, bho, reṇu rājaputto rājānaṁ disampatiṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he said this, Prince Reṇu said to him,

## dn19:29.10
- **PLI**: “mā kho tvaṁ, deva, govinde brāhmaṇe kālaṅkate atibāḷhaṁ paridevesi.
- **EN**: “Your Majesty, don’t lament too much at the Steward’s passing.

## dn19:29.11
- **PLI**: Atthi, deva, govindassa brāhmaṇassa jotipālo nāma māṇavo putto paṇḍitataro ceva pitarā, alamatthadasataro ceva pitarā;
- **EN**: He has a son named Jotipāla, who is even more astute and expert than his father.

## dn19:29.12
- **PLI**: yepissa pitā atthe anusāsi, tepi jotipālasseva māṇavassa anusāsaniyā”ti.
- **EN**: He should manage the affairs that were managed by his father.”

## dn19:29.13
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, kumārā”ti?
- **EN**: “Is that so, my prince?”

## dn19:29.14
- **PLI**: “Evaṁ, devā”ti.
- **EN**: “That is so, Your Majesty.”
