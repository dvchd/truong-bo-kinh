## dn11:85.11  `bj7.538, ms6D_859`
- **PLI**: ‘Kattha āpo ca pathavī,
- **EN**: “Where do water and earth,
- **NOTE**: The first pair of couplets rephrase the question about where the (absorptions based on) material form cease. This couplet is also found at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn1.27/en/sujato#2.1'>SN 1.27:2.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud1.10/en/sujato#14.1'>Ud 1.10:14.1</a>. | A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gādha</i> is a “ford” across a river (Rig Veda 7.60.7, 7.97.8, 10.106.9; Gopatha Brāhmaṇa 1.5.2a), a safe path on which one has a “footing” to cross the water. In this verse, the verb form <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gādhati</i> means to be “established” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>patiṭṭhita</i>) like a seed in soil, or a beam of light on a floor, or consciousness in name and form.

## dn11:85.12
- **PLI**: tejo vāyo na gādhati;
- **EN**: fire and air have no footing;
- **NOTE**: The verse is syntactically ambiguous, as there are three couplets, each with the question-word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kattha</i>, but only two verbs. I take the first pair of couplets as a distinct verse, with the verb <i lang='pi' translate='no'>gādhati</i> acting as a “lamp” illuminating both the first and second couplets. These are also unified in that they deal only with material qualities.

## dn11:85.13
- **PLI**: Kattha dīghañca rassañca,
- **EN**: where long and short,
- **NOTE**: A similar list of descriptors elsewhere describes things that are not stolen (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.9/en/sujato#45.1'>Snp 3.9:45.1</a>) or the kinds of sentient beings (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp1.8/en/sujato#4.3'>Snp 1.8:4.3</a>). These are aspects of how “form” manifests in desirable or undesirable ways.

## dn11:85.14

- **EN**: tiny and huge, beautiful and ugly?
- **NOTE**: Four terms in this verse are identical with the first four terms in Yājñavalkya’s description of the immutable Brahman as “neither coarse nor fine, neither short nor long” at Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.8.8.

## dn11:85.15

- **NOTE**: This final couplet is distinguished in having a different verb (“cease”) and in including mental qualities (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>nāma</i>). It therefore appears there are two questions with two answers.

## dn11:85.18  `cck9.286, ms6D_861`
- **PLI**: ‘Viññāṇaṁ anidassanaṁ,
- **EN**: “‘Consciousness where no form appears,
- **NOTE**: This couplet is spoken by Baka the Divinity at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#25.1'>MN 49:25.1</a>, so it was probably a free-floating couplet illustrating the Upaniṣadic theory of the Self as infinite consciousness. I think the Buddha is quoting Brahmā here in order to contrast with his own teaching of the cessation of consciousness, to emphasize that <em>even this</em> most sublime form of consciousness will cease. | “Where no form appears” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anidassanaṁ</i>) here is a synonym for “formless” (see eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato#14.8'>MN 21:14.8</a>, “space is formless and invisible”, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ākāso arūpī anidassano</i>). Normally the colors and images seen in the “form” absorptions are described as “appearing” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#3.29.1'>DN 16:3.29.1</a>), so this indicates a formless attainment.

## dn11:85.19
- **PLI**: anantaṁ sabbatopabhaṁ;
- **EN**: infinite, radiant all-round.’
- **NOTE**: “Infinite” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ananta</i>) is the direct qualifier of “consciousness”, but in the Pali it is shifted to the next line to fit the meter. It indicates the second of the formless attainments. Yājñavalkya describes consciousness as infinite in the famous passage at Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.12. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pabhaṁ</i> means “radiant”, as with the deities that are “self-radiant” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sayaṁpabhā</i>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn27/en/sujato#10.3'>DN 27:10.3</a>). This echoes descriptions of the Divinity of consciousness as “radiant” (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.6.1, Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.2). See too <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag20.1/en/sujato#59.6'>Thag 20.1:59.6</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an1.51/en/sujato#1.1'>AN 1.51:1.1</a>, and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.101/en/sujato#1.11'>AN 3.101:1.11</a>, and notes there. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sabbato pabhaṁ</i> (“radiant all-round”) is synonymous with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pariyodāta</i> (“bright”, literally “white all over”), a stock descriptor of the mind of fourth <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jhāna</i>, on which the formless states are based.
