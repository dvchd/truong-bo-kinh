# Muc 7 — Attainments of Vision  (21 đoạn)

## dn28:7.0
- **PLI**: 1.5. Dassanasamāpattidesanā
- **EN**: 1.5. Attainments of Vision

## dn28:7.1  `cck11.116, dr11.116, ms8D_306, msdiv149, pts-cs7, sya11.114`
- **PLI**: Aparaṁ pana, bhante, etadānuttariyaṁ, yathā bhagavā dhammaṁ deseti dassanasamāpattīsu.
- **EN**: And moreover, sir, how the Buddha teaches the attainments of vision is unsurpassable.

## dn28:7.2
- **PLI**: Catasso imā, bhante, dassanasamāpattiyo.
- **EN**: There are these four attainments of vision.

## dn28:7.3
- **PLI**: Idha, bhante, ekacco samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā ātappamanvāya padhānamanvāya anuyogamanvāya appamādamanvāya sammāmanasikāramanvāya tathārūpaṁ cetosamādhiṁ phusati, yathāsamāhite citte imameva kāyaṁ uddhaṁ pādatalā adho kesamatthakā tacapariyantaṁ pūraṁ nānappakārassa asucino paccavekkhati:
- **EN**: Firstly, some ascetic or brahmin—by dint of keen, resolute, committed, and diligent effort, and right application of mind—experiences an immersion of the heart of such a kind that they examine their own body up from the soles of the feet and down from the tips of the hairs, wrapped in skin and full of many kinds of filth.
- **NOTE**: Meditation is described in this way only in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn136/en/sujato#9.1'>MN 136:9.1</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn1/en/sujato#1.31.1'>DN 1:1.31.1</a> where, as here, it is pursued by an “ascetic or brahmin”, who in those cases is not Buddhist. Here, however, it sounds like Sāriputta is talking about a Buddhist practice. The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>asubha</i> contemplation of the parts of the body is described in several places in the canon (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn22/en/sujato#5.1'>DN 22:5.1</a>), but never exactly like this.

## dn28:7.4

- **EN**: ‘In this body there is head hair, body hair, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, diaphragm, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, feces, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, snot, synovial fluid, urine.’

## dn28:7.13
- **PLI**: Purisassa ca viññāṇasotaṁ pajānāti, ubhayato abbocchinnaṁ idha loke patiṭṭhitañca paraloke patiṭṭhitañca.
- **EN**: And they understand of a person that their stream of consciousness is consistent on both sides: established in both this world and the next.
- **NOTE**: This is a unique description of the process of rebirth. Consciousness is a “stream” in that it flows and moves, ever-changing, and is not a static self-same entity. To be “established” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>patiṭṭhita</i>) is to be attached because of craving. | The phrase “consistent on both sides” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ubhayato abbocchinnaṁ</i>) is unique. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ubhayato</i> is used of “both ends” of a pole <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja533/en/sujato#37.4'>Ja 533:37.4</a>. I think the sense is that one’s consciousness is consistent with regards to this world and the next. If one is attached to this world, one will be attached to the next, and vice versa in the next section.
