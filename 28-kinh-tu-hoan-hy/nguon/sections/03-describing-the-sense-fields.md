# Muc 4 — Describing the Sense Fields  (6 đoạn)

## dn28:4.0
- **PLI**: 1.2. Āyatanapaṇṇattidesanā
- **EN**: 1.2. Describing the Sense Fields

## dn28:4.1  `bj9.172, cck11.114, ms8D_297, msdiv146, pts-cs4, pts-vp-pli3.103`
- **PLI**: Aparaṁ pana, bhante, etadānuttariyaṁ, yathā bhagavā dhammaṁ deseti āyatanapaṇṇattīsu.
- **EN**: And moreover, sir, how the Buddha teaches the description of the sense fields is unsurpassable.

## dn28:4.2
- **PLI**: Chayimāni, bhante, ajjhattikabāhirāni āyatanāni.
- **EN**: There are these six interior and exterior sense fields.
- **NOTE**: The primary source is the SN 35, the Linked Discourses on the Six Sense Fields.

## dn28:4.3
- **PLI**: Cakkhuñceva rūpā ca, sotañceva saddā ca, ghānañceva gandhā ca, jivhā ceva rasā ca, kāyo ceva phoṭṭhabbā ca, mano ceva dhammā ca.
- **EN**: The eye and sights, the ear and sounds, the nose and smells, the tongue and tastes, the body and touches, and the mind and ideas.

## dn28:4.4
- **PLI**: Etadānuttariyaṁ, bhante, āyatanapaṇṇattīsu.
- **EN**: This is unsurpassable when it comes to describing the sense fields.

## dn28:4.5
- **PLI**: Taṁ bhagavā asesamabhijānāti, taṁ bhagavato asesamabhijānato uttari abhiññeyyaṁ natthi, yadabhijānaṁ añño samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā bhagavatā bhiyyobhiññataro assa yadidaṁ āyatanapaṇṇattīsu.
- **EN**: The Buddha understands this without exception. There is nothing to be understood beyond this whereby another ascetic or brahmin might be superior in direct knowledge to the Buddha when it comes to describing the sense fields.
