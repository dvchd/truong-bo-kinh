# Muc 16 — On Reaching the Bark  (29 đoạn)

## dn25:16.0
- **PLI**: 2.3. Parisuddhatacappattakathā
- **EN**: 2.3. On Reaching the Bark

## dn25:16.1  `ms8D_155, msdiv70, pts-cs16, sya11.51`

## dn25:16.2
- **PLI**: Sādhu me, bhante, bhagavā tapojigucchāya aggaññeva pāpetu, sāraññeva pāpetū”ti.
- **EN**: Please help me reach the peak and the pith!”

## dn25:16.3  `bj9.80, cck11.52, ms8D_156, pts-vp-pli3.49`
- **PLI**: “Idha, nigrodha, tapassī cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti.
- **EN**: “Nigrodha, take a mortifier who is restrained in the fourfold constraint.
- **NOTE**: A “fourfold restraint” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>cātuyāma</i>, or <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cāujjāma</i> in Jain Ardhamagadhi) is attributed to Jain founder Pārśva in Isibhāsiyāiṁ 31, where it is an abbreviated list: “from refraining from killing to refraining from possessions” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāṇātivāta-veramaṇeṇaṁ jāvā pariggaha-veramaṇeṇaṁ</i>); the missing items are not stealing (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>asteya</i>) and truth (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>satya</i>). The Jain text Uttarādhyayanasūtra 23 shows that Mahāvīra added nakedness to make five. Five are also found in Yogasūtra 2.30 and the Jain Tattvārthasūtra 7: non-harming (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ahiṁsā</i>), truth (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>satya</i>), no stealing (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>asteya</i>), celibacy (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmacarya</i>), and non-possessiveness (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>aparigrahā</i>). | A different “fourfold restraint” is said to be a Jain practice at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato#29.2'>DN 2:29.2</a>.

## dn25:16.4
- **PLI**: Kathañca, nigrodha, tapassī cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti?
- **EN**: And how is a mortifier restrained in the fourfold constraint?

## dn25:16.5
- **PLI**: Idha, nigrodha, tapassī na pāṇaṁ atipāteti, na pāṇaṁ atipātayati, na pāṇamatipātayato samanuñño hoti.
- **EN**: It’s when a mortifier doesn’t kill living creatures, doesn’t get others to kill, and doesn’t approve of killing.
- **NOTE**: This extends the basic precept. The same analysis is applied to the “ten ways of performing deeds” at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.163/en/sujato'>AN 3.163</a>. | The distinction between one’s own action and another’s action is noted at Isibhāsiyāiṁ 31.42, although the wording and sense are different.

## dn25:16.6
- **PLI**: Na adinnaṁ ādiyati, na adinnaṁ ādiyāpeti, na adinnaṁ ādiyato samanuñño hoti.
- **EN**: They don’t steal, get others to steal, or approve of stealing.

## dn25:16.7
- **PLI**: Na musā bhaṇati, na musā bhaṇāpeti, na musā bhaṇato samanuñño hoti.
- **EN**: They don’t lie, get others to lie, or approve of lying.

## dn25:16.8
- **PLI**: Na bhāvitamāsīsati, na bhāvitamāsīsāpeti, na bhāvitamāsīsato samanuñño hoti.
- **EN**: They don’t expect rewards from their practice, they don’t lead others to expect rewards, and they don’t approve of expecting rewards.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Bhāvitamāsīsati</i> is unique. In this and similar contexts, the Pali tradition says <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhāvita</i> (“reward”, normally “developed”) is the “five sense pleasures” as they see it (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhāvitaṁ nāma tesaṁ saññāya pañca kāmaguṇā</i>; cp. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn56/en/sujato'>MN 56</a> commentary: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ettha ca bhāvitanti pañcakāmaguṇā</i>), explaining that this is a usage of the sectarians (Saddanīti ch. 4: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>titthiyasamaye pana bhāvitoti kāmaguṇo vuccati</i>).

## dn25:16.9
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho, nigrodha, tapassī cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti.
- **EN**: That’s how a mortifier is restrained in the fourfold constraint.

## dn25:16.10  `dr11.52, ms8D_157, sya11.52`
- **PLI**: Yato kho, nigrodha, tapassī cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti, aduṁ cassa hoti tapassitāya.
- **EN**: When a mortifier is restrained in the fourfold constraint, that is their mortification.

## dn25:16.11
- **PLI**: So abhiharati no hīnāyāvattati.
- **EN**: They step forward, not falling back.

## dn25:16.12
- **PLI**: So vivittaṁ senāsanaṁ bhajati araññaṁ rukkhamūlaṁ pabbataṁ kandaraṁ giriguhaṁ susānaṁ vanapatthaṁ abbhokāsaṁ palālapuñjaṁ.
- **EN**: They frequent a secluded lodging—a wilderness, the root of a tree, a hill, a ravine, a mountain cave, a charnel ground, a forest, the open air, a heap of straw.

## dn25:16.13
- **PLI**: So pacchābhattaṁ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto nisīdati pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā.
- **EN**: After the meal, they return from almsround, sit down cross-legged, set their body straight, and bring mindfulness to the present.

## dn25:16.14
- **PLI**: So abhijjhaṁ loke pahāya vigatābhijjhena cetasā viharati, abhijjhāya cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up covetousness for the world, they meditate with a heart rid of covetousness, cleansing the mind of covetousness.

## dn25:16.15
- **PLI**: Byāpādappadosaṁ pahāya abyāpannacitto viharati sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī, byāpādappadosā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up ill will and malevolence, they meditate with a mind rid of ill will, full of sympathy for all living beings, cleansing the mind of ill will.

## dn25:16.16
- **PLI**: Thinamiddhaṁ pahāya vigatathinamiddho viharati ālokasaññī sato sampajāno, thinamiddhā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up dullness and drowsiness, they meditate with a mind rid of dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and aware, cleansing the mind of dullness and drowsiness.

## dn25:16.17
- **PLI**: Uddhaccakukkuccaṁ pahāya anuddhato viharati ajjhattaṁ vūpasantacitto, uddhaccakukkuccā cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up restlessness and remorse, they meditate without restlessness, their mind settled internally, cleansing the mind of restlessness and remorse.

## dn25:16.18
- **PLI**: Vicikicchaṁ pahāya tiṇṇavicikiccho viharati akathaṅkathī kusalesu dhammesu, vicikicchāya cittaṁ parisodheti.
- **EN**: Giving up doubt, they meditate having gone beyond doubt, not undecided about skillful qualities, cleansing the mind of doubt.
