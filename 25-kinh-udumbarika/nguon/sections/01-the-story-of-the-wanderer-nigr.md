# Muc 1 — The Story of the Wanderer Nigrodha  (47 đoạn)

## dn25:1.0
- **PLI**: 1. Nigrodhaparibbājakavatthu
- **EN**: 1. The Story of the Wanderer Nigrodha

## dn25:1.1  `bj9.58, cck11.39, csp1ed8.30, csp2ed8.30, dr11.38, ms8D_109, msdiv49, ndp8.29, pts-cs1, pts-vp-pli3.36, sya11.38, vri3.26`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.

## dn25:1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā rājagahe viharati gijjhakūṭe pabbate.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was staying near Rājagaha, on the Vulture’s Peak Mountain.

## dn25:1.3
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena nigrodho paribbājako udumbarikāya paribbājakārāme paṭivasati mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṁ tiṁsamattehi paribbājakasatehi.
- **EN**: Now at that time the wanderer Nigrodha was residing in the lady Udumbarikā’s monastery for wanderers, together with a large assembly of three thousand wanderers.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha refers to the events of this sutta at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn8/en/sujato#23.2'>DN 8:23.2</a>, but apart from that Nigrodha (“Banyan”) appears to be unknown. A “wanderer” may or may not be Brahmanical, but in DN 8 Nigrodha is called <i lang='pi' translate='no'>tapabrahmacārī</i>, which suggests he was a celibate student of a Brahmanical teacher. | The commentary describes <i lang='pi' translate='no'>udumbarikā</i> (“Fig”) as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>devī</i> (“Queen”), while DA 8 says she is a “brahmin lady”. She too does not seem to appear elsewhere.

## dn25:1.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho sandhāno gahapati divā divassa rājagahā nikkhami bhagavantaṁ dassanāya.
- **EN**: Then the householder Sandhāna left Rājagaha in the middle of the day to see the Buddha.
- **NOTE**: Sandhāna (“Conciliation”) was recognized as an eminent lay follower at (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.120-139/en/sujato#1.14'>AN 6.120–139:1.14</a>).

## dn25:1.5
- **PLI**: Atha kho sandhānassa gahapatissa etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then it occurred to him,

## dn25:1.6
- **PLI**: “akālo kho bhagavantaṁ dassanāya.
- **EN**: “It’s the wrong time to see the Buddha,

## dn25:1.7
- **PLI**: Paṭisallīno bhagavā.
- **EN**: as he’s in retreat.

## dn25:1.8
- **PLI**: Manobhāvanīyānampi bhikkhūnaṁ asamayo dassanāya.
- **EN**: And it’s the wrong time to see the esteemed mendicants,

## dn25:1.9
- **PLI**: Paṭisallīnā manobhāvanīyā bhikkhū.
- **EN**: as they’re in retreat.

## dn25:1.10
- **PLI**: Yannūnāhaṁ yena udumbarikāya paribbājakārāmo, yena nigrodho paribbājako tenupasaṅkameyyan”ti.
- **EN**: Why don’t I visit the wanderer Nigrodha at the lady Udumbarikā’s monastery for wanderers?”

## dn25:1.11
- **PLI**: Atha kho sandhāno gahapati yena udumbarikāya paribbājakārāmo, tenupasaṅkami.
- **EN**: So he went to the monastery for the wanderers.

## dn25:2.1  `ms8D_110, msdiv50, pts-cs2, pts-vp-pli3.37, sya11.39`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena nigrodho paribbājako mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṁ nisinno hoti unnādiniyā uccāsaddamahāsaddāya anekavihitaṁ tiracchānakathaṁ kathentiyā.
- **EN**: Now at that time, Nigrodha was sitting together with a large assembly of wanderers making an uproar, a colossal racket. They engaged in all kinds of low talk, such as

## dn25:2.2
- **PLI**: Seyyathidaṁ—rājakathaṁ corakathaṁ mahāmattakathaṁ senākathaṁ bhayakathaṁ yuddhakathaṁ annakathaṁ pānakathaṁ vatthakathaṁ sayanakathaṁ mālākathaṁ gandhakathaṁ ñātikathaṁ yānakathaṁ gāmakathaṁ nigamakathaṁ nagarakathaṁ janapadakathaṁ itthikathaṁ sūrakathaṁ visikhākathaṁ kumbhaṭṭhānakathaṁ pubbapetakathaṁ nānattakathaṁ lokakkhāyikaṁ samuddakkhāyikaṁ itibhavābhavakathaṁ iti vā.
- **EN**: talk about kings, bandits, and chief ministers; talk about armies, threats, and wars; talk about food, drink, clothes, and beds; talk about garlands and fragrances; talk about family, vehicles, villages, towns, cities, and countries; talk about women and heroes; talk at the street market and the place for water pots; talk about the departed; motley talk; tales of land and sea; and talk about being reborn in this or that place.

## dn25:3.1  `csp1ed8.31, csp2ed8.31, dr11.39, ms8D_111, msdiv51, ndp8.30, pts-cs3, vri3.27`
- **PLI**: Addasā kho nigrodho paribbājako sandhānaṁ gahapatiṁ dūratova āgacchantaṁ.
- **EN**: Nigrodha saw Sandhāna coming off in the distance,

## dn25:3.2
- **PLI**: Disvā sakaṁ parisaṁ saṇṭhāpesi:
- **EN**: and hushed his own assembly:

## dn25:3.3
- **PLI**: “appasaddā bhonto hontu, mā bhonto saddamakattha.
- **EN**: “Be quiet, good fellows, don’t make a sound.

## dn25:3.4
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ samaṇassa gotamassa sāvako āgacchati sandhāno gahapati.
- **EN**: The householder Sandhāna, a disciple of the ascetic Gotama, is coming.

## dn25:3.5
- **PLI**: Yāvatā kho pana samaṇassa gotamassa sāvakā gihī odātavasanā rājagahe paṭivasanti, ayaṁ tesaṁ aññataro sandhāno gahapati.
- **EN**: He is included among the white-clothed lay disciples of the ascetic Gotama, who is residing near Rājagaha.

## dn25:3.6
- **PLI**: Appasaddakāmā kho panete āyasmanto appasaddavinītā, appasaddassa vaṇṇavādino.
- **EN**: Such venerables like the quiet, are educated to be quiet, and praise the quiet.

## dn25:3.7
- **PLI**: Appeva nāma appasaddaṁ parisaṁ viditvā upasaṅkamitabbaṁ maññeyyā”ti.
- **EN**: Hopefully if he sees that our assembly is quiet he’ll see fit to approach.”

## dn25:3.8
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṁ.
- **EN**: Then those wanderers fell silent.

## dn25:4.1  `bj9.60, cck11.40, ms8D_112, msdiv52, pts-cs4, pts-vp-pli3.38`

- **EN**: Then Sandhāna went up to the wanderer Nigrodha, and exchanged greetings with him.

## dn25:4.2

- **EN**: When the greetings and polite conversation were over, he sat down to one side and said to Nigrodha,

- **EN**: “The way the wanderers make an uproar as they sit together and talk about all kinds of low topics is one thing.

## dn25:4.4
- **PLI**: Seyyathidaṁ—rājakathaṁ …pe… itibhavābhavakathaṁ iti vā.

## dn25:4.5
- **PLI**: Aññathā kho pana so bhagavā araññavanapatthāni pantāni senāsanāni paṭisevati appasaddāni appanigghosāni vijanavātāni manussarāhasseyyakāni paṭisallānasāruppānī”ti.
- **EN**: It’s quite different to the way the Buddha frequents remote lodgings in the wilderness and the forest that are quiet and still, far from the madding crowd, remote from human settlements, and fit for retreat.”
