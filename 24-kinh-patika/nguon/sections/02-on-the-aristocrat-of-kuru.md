## dn24:1.7.0

## dn24:1.7.1  `bj9.10, csp1ed8.5, csp2ed8.5, dr11.6, ms8D_14, msdiv7, ndp8.7, pts-cs1.7`
- **PLI**: Ekamidāhaṁ, bhaggava, samayaṁ thūlūsu viharāmi uttarakā nāma thūlūnaṁ nigamo.
- **EN**: Bhaggava, this one time I was staying in the land of the Kurus where they have a town named Uttarakā.
- **NOTE**: There are at least eight spellings for this tribe, including <i lang='pi' translate='no'>thulu</i>, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bumu</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>khulu</i>. Below we meet a member of the ruling clan of the tribe named <i lang='pi' translate='no'>korakhattiya</i>. I propose that <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kora</i> is “of Kuru” by way of secondary derivation; cp. Koravya as a regular name for the kings of Kuru. Thus <i lang='pi' translate='no'>korakhattiya</i> is not a personal name, but rather “the aristocrat of Kuru”. The name of the city <i lang='pi' translate='no'>uttarakā</i> means “northern”, and Kuru is indeed northern. Occam’s razor would urge us not to assume the existence of an otherwise unknown tribe on such a dubious term when it can be explained more parsimoniously as an ancient misspelling of Kuru. The Chinese at DA 15 (T 1, 67a15) has <span lang='lzh'>白土</span> (“white clay”), which does not seem to clear it up.

- **PLI**: Yaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, maññasi acelaṁ korakkhattiyaṁ—sādhurūpo ayaṁ samaṇoti. So sattamaṁ divasaṁ alasakena kālaṁ karissati.
- **EN**: That naked ascetic the aristocrat of Kuru, who you imagine to be a holy man, will die of flatulence in seven days.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Alasaka</i> is otherwise unknown in early Pali. Sanskrit medical texts describe it as flatulent indigestion. This agrees with the subcommentary’s <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ajīraṇena āmarogena</i> (“indigestion”). | In this sutta, the Buddha is depicted as making specific predictions about the future, whereas normally he does not and probably cannot. Rather, he makes conditional predictions that if such courses are pursued, such results will follow.
