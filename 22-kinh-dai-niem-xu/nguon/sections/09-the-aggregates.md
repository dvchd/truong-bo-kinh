# Muc 14 — The Aggregates  (14 đoạn)

## dn22:14.0
- **PLI**: 4.2. Dhammānupassanākhandhapabba
- **EN**: 4.2. The Aggregates

## dn22:14.1  `bj8.470, cck10.354, dr10.333, ms7D_1014, msdiv383, pts-cs14, pts-vp-pli2.302, vri2.224`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of principles with respect to the five grasping aggregates.

## dn22:14.2
- **PLI**: Kathañca pana, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu?
- **EN**: And how does a mendicant meditate observing an aspect of principles with respect to the five grasping aggregates?

## dn22:14.3
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu:
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant contemplates:

## dn22:14.4
- **PLI**: ‘iti rūpaṁ, iti rūpassa samudayo, iti rūpassa atthaṅgamo;
- **EN**: ‘Such is form, such is the origin of form, such is the disappearance of form.
- **NOTE**: “Form” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rūpa</i>) is one’s own body and the external material world experienced through the senses. More subtly, it represents the “appearance” of physical phenomena, even when experienced solely in the mind as color, visions, etc.

## dn22:14.5
- **PLI**: iti vedanā, iti vedanāya samudayo, iti vedanāya atthaṅgamo;
- **EN**: Such is feeling, such is the origin of feeling, such is the disappearance of feeling.

## dn22:14.6
- **PLI**: iti saññā, iti saññāya samudayo, iti saññāya atthaṅgamo;
- **EN**: Such is perception, such is the origin of perception, such is the disappearance of perception.
- **NOTE**: “Perception” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>saññā</i>) is the recognition or interpretation of experience in terms of meaningful wholes. We see, for example, “color” yet we perceive a “person”. In the Vinaya we find many examples where a person perceived things in one way, yet they turned out to be something else.

## dn22:14.7
- **PLI**: iti saṅkhārā, iti saṅkhārānaṁ samudayo, iti saṅkhārānaṁ atthaṅgamo,
- **EN**: Such are choices, such is the origin of choices, such is the disappearance of choices.
- **NOTE**: In the five aggregates, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>saṅkhārā</i> is a synonym for “volition” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>cetanā</i>). The traditions later used it as a catch-all category for everything that does not fit in the other aggregates. In the suttas, however, the purpose of the aggregates is not to classify everything that exists, but to contemplate aspects of experience that we tend to identify as a “self”.

## dn22:14.8
- **PLI**: iti viññāṇaṁ, iti viññāṇassa samudayo, iti viññāṇassa atthaṅgamo’ti,
- **EN**: Such is consciousness, such is the origin of consciousness, such is the disappearance of consciousness.’

## dn22:14.9
- **PLI**: iti ajjhattaṁ vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati, bahiddhā vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati, ajjhattabahiddhā vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati.
- **EN**: And so they meditate observing an aspect of principles internally …

## dn22:14.10
- **PLI**: Samudayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati, vayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati, samudayavayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati.
- **EN**: They meditate observing the principles as liable to originate, as liable to fall, and as liable to both originate and vanish.  (= dn22:13.10)

## dn22:14.11
- **PLI**: ‘Atthi dhammā’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti yāvadeva ñāṇamattāya paṭissatimattāya, anissito ca viharati, na ca kiñci loke upādiyati.
- **EN**: Or mindfulness is established that principles exist, to the extent necessary for knowledge and mindfulness. They meditate independent, not grasping at anything in the world.  (= dn22:13.11)

## dn22:14.12
- **PLI**: Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu.
- **EN**: That’s how a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of principles with respect to the five grasping aggregates.

## dn22:14.13
- **PLI**: Khandhapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.
- **EN**: (Sujato gộp đoạn này vào câu trước — dịch trực tiếp từ PLI)
