# Muc 2.5 — Of All Cities  (22 đoạn)

## dn17:2.5.0
- **PLI**: 6. Caturāsītinagarasahassādi
- **EN**: 6. Of All Cities

## dn17:2.5.1  `bj8.290, cck10.231, dr10.217, mc10.163, ms7D_579, msdiv263, ndp7.143, pts-cs2.5, pts-vp-pli2.188, sya10.217, vri2.140`
- **PLI**: Rañño, ānanda, mahāsudassanassa caturāsīti nagarasahassāni ahesuṁ kusāvatīrājadhānippamukhāni;
- **EN**: King Mahāsudassana had 84,000 cities, with the royal capital of Kusāvatī the foremost.

## dn17:2.5.2
- **PLI**: caturāsīti pāsādasahassāni ahesuṁ dhammapāsādappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 palaces, with the Palace of Principle the foremost.
- **NOTE**: “Palace” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>pāsāda</i>, literally “stilt longhouse”.

## dn17:2.5.3
- **PLI**: caturāsīti kūṭāgārasahassāni ahesuṁ mahāviyūhakūṭāgārappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 chambers, with the Chamber of the Grand Tower the foremost.
- **NOTE**: “Chamber” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kūṭāgāra</i>, literally “building with peaked roof”. Normally this means “bungalow”, but in a similar description at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn37/en/sujato#8.9'>MN 37:8.9</a> we hear of a “palace” with a hundred “towers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>niyyūha</i>), each of which has seven hundred “chambers”. Thus the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kūṭāgāra</i> must be a structure many of which can belong to a palace, namely “chamber”, while <i lang='pi' translate='no'>viyūha</i> (spelled <i lang='pi' translate='no'>byūha</i> at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn22.96/en/sujato#3.4'>SN 22.96:3.4</a>) must have the same meaning as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>niyyūha</i>, i.e. “tower”.

## dn17:2.5.4
- **PLI**: caturāsīti pallaṅkasahassāni ahesuṁ sovaṇṇamayāni rūpiyamayāni dantamayāni sāramayāni gonakatthatāni paṭikatthatāni paṭalikatthatāni kadalimigapavarapaccattharaṇāni sauttaracchadāni ubhatolohitakūpadhānāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 couches made of gold, silver, ivory, and hardwood. They were spread with woollen covers—shag-piled, pure white, or embroidered with flowers—and spread with a fine deer hide, with a canopy above and red pillows at both ends.
- **NOTE**: This segment breaks the expected pattern of “foremost” things.

## dn17:2.5.5
- **PLI**: caturāsīti nāgasahassāni ahesuṁ sovaṇṇālaṅkārāni sovaṇṇadhajāni hemajālapaṭicchannāni uposathanāgarājappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 bull elephants with gold adornments and banners, covered with snow gold netting, with the royal bull elephant named Sabbath foremost.

## dn17:2.5.6
- **PLI**: caturāsīti assasahassāni ahesuṁ sovaṇṇālaṅkārāni sovaṇṇadhajāni hemajālapaṭicchannāni valāhakaassarājappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 horses with gold adornments and banners, covered with snow gold netting, with the royal steed named Thundercloud foremost.

## dn17:2.5.7
- **PLI**: caturāsīti rathasahassāni ahesuṁ sīhacammaparivārāni byagghacammaparivārāni dīpicammaparivārāni paṇḍukambalaparivārāni sovaṇṇālaṅkārāni sovaṇṇadhajāni hemajālapaṭicchannāni vejayantarathappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 chariots upholstered with the hide of lions, tigers, and leopards, and cream rugs, with gold adornments and banners, covered with snow gold netting, with the chariot named Triumph foremost.
- **NOTE**: Not the British car of the same name. The chariot is the source of victory in battle.

## dn17:2.5.8
- **PLI**: caturāsīti maṇisahassāni ahesuṁ maṇiratanappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 jewels, with the jewel-treasure foremost.

## dn17:2.5.9
- **PLI**: caturāsīti itthisahassāni ahesuṁ subhaddādevippamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 women, with Queen Subhaddā foremost.

## dn17:2.5.10
- **PLI**: caturāsīti gahapatisahassāni ahesuṁ gahapatiratanappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 householders, with the householder-treasure foremost.

## dn17:2.5.11
- **PLI**: caturāsīti khattiyasahassāni ahesuṁ anuyantāni pariṇāyakaratanappamukhāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 aristocrat vassals, with the commander-treasure foremost.

## dn17:2.5.12
- **PLI**: caturāsīti dhenusahassāni ahesuṁ duhasandanāni kaṁsūpadhāraṇāni;
- **EN**: He had 84,000 milk-cows with silken halter and bronze pails.
- **NOTE**: Read <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dukūlasandanāni</i>, where <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dukūla</i> is “fine cloth, silk” and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sandana</i> is “cord, tether”.

## dn17:2.5.13
- **PLI**: caturāsīti vatthakoṭisahassāni ahesuṁ khomasukhumānaṁ kappāsikasukhumānaṁ koseyyasukhumānaṁ kambalasukhumānaṁ;
- **EN**: He had 8,400,000,000 cloths of delicate linen, cotton, silk, and wool.

## dn17:2.5.14
- **PLI**: rañño, ānanda, mahāsudassanassa caturāsīti thālipākasahassāni ahesuṁ sāyaṁ pātaṁ bhattābhihāro abhihariyittha.
- **EN**: He had 84,000 servings of food, which were presented to him as offerings at daybreak and dusk.

## dn17:2.6.1  `cck10.232, csp1ed7.154, csp2ed7.154, dr10.218, ms7D_580, msdiv264, ndp7.144, pts-cs2.6, pts-vp-pli2.189`

## dn17:2.6.2
- **PLI**: Atha kho, ānanda, rañño mahāsudassanassa etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then King Mahāsudassana thought,

## dn17:2.6.3
- **PLI**: ‘imāni kho me caturāsīti nāgasahassāni sāyaṁ pātaṁ upaṭṭhānaṁ āgacchanti, yannūna vassasatassa vassasatassa accayena dvecattālīsaṁ dvecattālīsaṁ nāgasahassāni sakiṁ sakiṁ upaṭṭhānaṁ āgaccheyyun’ti.
- **EN**: ‘What if instead half of the elephants took turns to attend on me at the end of each century?’
- **NOTE**: An odd detail. Maybe the sound of the elephants disturbed his meditation?
