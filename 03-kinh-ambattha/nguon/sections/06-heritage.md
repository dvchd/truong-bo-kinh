# 2.5 — Bàn luận về dòng dõi Ambaṭṭha  (26 đoạn)

## dn3:1.22.0
- **PLI**: 2.5. Ambaṭṭhavaṁsakathā
- **EN**: 2.5. The Discussion of Ambaṭṭha’s Heritage

## dn3:1.22.1  `csp1ed6.90, csp2ed6.90, dr9.126, ms6D_373, msdiv273, pts-cs1.22, sya9.125`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, te māṇavakā unnādino uccāsaddamahāsaddā ahesuṁ:
- **EN**: When he said this, those young students made an uproar,

## dn3:1.22.2
- **PLI**: “dujjāto kira, bho, ambaṭṭho māṇavo; akulaputto kira, bho, ambaṭṭho māṇavo; dāsiputto kira, bho, ambaṭṭho māṇavo sakyānaṁ. Ayyaputtā kira, bho, ambaṭṭhassa māṇavassa sakyā bhavanti.
- **EN**: “It turns out Ambaṭṭha is ill-born, not a gentleman, son of a Sakyan slavegirl, and that the Sakyans are sons of his masters!

## dn3:1.22.3
- **PLI**: Dhammavādiṁyeva kira mayaṁ samaṇaṁ gotamaṁ apasādetabbaṁ amaññimhā”ti.
- **EN**: And it seems that the ascetic Gotama spoke only the truth, though we presumed to rebuke him!”

## dn3:1.23.1  `cck9.124, km14.234, lv1.262, ms6D_374, msdiv274, pts-cs1.23, pts-vp-pli1.96`
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavato etadahosi:
- **EN**: Then it occurred to the Buddha,

## dn3:1.23.2
- **PLI**: “atibāḷhaṁ kho ime māṇavakā ambaṭṭhaṁ māṇavaṁ dāsiputtavādena nimmādenti, yannūnāhaṁ parimoceyyan”ti.
- **EN**: “These young students put Ambaṭṭha down too much by calling him the son of a slavegirl. Why don’t I get him out of this?”

## dn3:1.23.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho bhagavā te māṇavake etadavoca:
- **EN**: So the Buddha said to the young students,

## dn3:1.23.4
- **PLI**: “mā kho tumhe, māṇavakā, ambaṭṭhaṁ māṇavaṁ atibāḷhaṁ dāsiputtavādena nimmādetha.
- **EN**: “Young students, please don’t put Ambaṭṭha down too much by calling him the son of a slavegirl.

## dn3:1.23.5
- **PLI**: Uḷāro so kaṇho isi ahosi.
- **EN**: That Black Boy was an eminent seer.
- **NOTE**: The contemptuous senses of “black boy” represent the conservative brahmanical view, presented not as endorsement, but as a rhetorical means to undermine Ambaṭṭha’s pride. The Buddha now shows how a man of a supposedly low birth rose to great spiritual eminence.

## dn3:1.23.6
- **PLI**: So dakkhiṇajanapadaṁ gantvā brahmamante adhīyitvā rājānaṁ okkākaṁ upasaṅkamitvā maddarūpiṁ dhītaraṁ yāci.
- **EN**: He went to a southern country and memorized the Divine Spell. Then he approached King Okkāka and asked for the hand of his daughter Maddarūpī.
- **NOTE**: “Divine Spell” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmamanta</i>, a term also found in the commentary to the Morajātaka (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/ja159/en/sujato'>Ja 159</a>), where it refers to a verse which, in Vedic style, worships the sun as the “one king”. In modern Hinduism it is used for a verse of praise for Brahmā. | Kaṇha is one of several “dark hermits” who accrued mighty and lineage-busting powers in the south.

## dn3:1.23.7
- **PLI**: Tassa rājā okkāko:
- **EN**: The king said to him,

## dn3:1.23.8
- **PLI**: ‘ko nevaṁ re ayaṁ mayhaṁ dāsiputto samāno maddarūpiṁ dhītaraṁ yācatī’ti, kupito anattamano khurappaṁ sannayhi.
- **EN**: ‘Who the hell is this son of a slavegirl to ask for the hand of my daughter!’ Angry and upset he fastened a razor-tipped arrow.
- **NOTE**: The Hindu deity Krishna won the hand of his seventh wife Lakṣmaṇā, also known as Madrī, at an archery contest. This detail is too precise to be a coincidence, and proves there must be some shared basis between the two figures.

## dn3:1.23.9
- **PLI**: So taṁ khurappaṁ neva asakkhi muñcituṁ, no paṭisaṁharituṁ.
- **EN**: But he wasn’t able to either shoot it or to relax it.

## dn3:1.23.10  `bj7.166, cck9.125, csp1ed6.91, csp2ed6.91, dr9.127, km14.235, km14.236, lv1.263, lv1.264, lv1.265, mc9.96, ms6D_375, ndp6.84, pts-vp-pli1.97, sya9.126, vri1.84`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, māṇavakā, amaccā pārisajjā kaṇhaṁ isiṁ upasaṅkamitvā etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: Then the ministers and councillors approached the seer Black Boy and said:

## dn3:1.23.11
- **PLI**: ‘sotthi, bhaddante, hotu rañño;
- **EN**: ‘Spare the king, sir,

## dn3:1.23.12
- **PLI**: sotthi, bhaddante, hotu rañño’ti.
- **EN**: spare him!’

## dn3:1.23.13
- **PLI**: ‘Sotthi bhavissati rañño, api ca rājā yadi adho khurappaṁ muñcissati, yāvatā rañño vijitaṁ, ettāvatā pathavī undriyissatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The king will be safe. But if he shoots the arrow downwards, there will be an earthquake across the entire realm.’
- **NOTE**: This draws on the ancient belief that the king’s acts affect the natural order of things.

## dn3:1.23.14
- **PLI**: ‘Sotthi, bhaddante, hotu rañño, sotthi janapadassā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Spare the king, sir, and spare the country!’

## dn3:1.23.15
- **PLI**: ‘Sotthi bhavissati rañño, sotthi janapadassa, api ca rājā yadi uddhaṁ khurappaṁ muñcissati, yāvatā rañño vijitaṁ, ettāvatā satta vassāni devo na vassissatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Both king and country will be safe. But if he shoots the arrow upwards, the heavens will not rain in the entire realm for seven years.’

## dn3:1.23.16
- **PLI**: ‘Sotthi, bhaddante, hotu rañño sotthi janapadassa devo ca vassatū’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Spare the king, sir, spare the country, and let the heavens rain!’
- **NOTE**: This sequence seems to be an etiological myth explaining certain rites of kingship and succession, providing an origin story for this prayer.

## dn3:1.23.17
- **PLI**: ‘Sotthi bhavissati rañño sotthi janapadassa devo ca vassissati, api ca rājā jeṭṭhakumāre khurappaṁ patiṭṭhāpetu, sotthi kumāro pallomo bhavissatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Both king and country will be safe, and the heavens rain will rain. And if the king shoots the crown prince with an arrow, he will be safe and unruffled.’
- **NOTE**: National prosperity is ensured through symbolic regicide. This example was omitted from Frazer’s accounts of such substitute sacrifices in the <em>Golden Bough</em>. Here there is a double substitution: the prince substitutes for the king, then a threat substitutes for the act of killing. This suggests that, even from the legendary perspective of this story within a story, the rite was an ancient one that had evolved through multiple stages.

## dn3:1.23.18
- **PLI**: Atha kho, māṇavakā, amaccā okkākassa ārocesuṁ:
- **EN**: So the ministers said to Okkāka:
- **NOTE**: The use of the bare personal name for the king is unusual.

## dn3:1.23.19
- **PLI**: ‘okkāko jeṭṭhakumāre khurappaṁ patiṭṭhāpetu. Sotthi kumāro pallomo bhavissatī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Okkāka must shoot the crown prince with an arrow. He will be safe and unruffled.’

## dn3:1.23.20
- **PLI**: Atha kho rājā okkāko jeṭṭhakumāre khurappaṁ patiṭṭhapesi, sotthi kumāro pallomo samabhavi.
- **EN**: So King Okkāka shot the crown prince with an arrow. And he was safe and unruffled.

## dn3:1.23.21
- **PLI**: Atha kho tassa rājā okkāko bhīto saṁviggo lomahaṭṭhajāto brahmadaṇḍena tajjito maddarūpiṁ dhītaraṁ adāsi.
- **EN**: Then the king was terrified, shocked, and awestruck. Scared by the divine punishment, he gave the hand of his daughter Maddarūpī.
- **NOTE**: “Divine punishment” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadaṇḍa</i>, harking back to the Divine Spell (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmamantra</i>). The Buddha had his own version of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadaṇḍa</i>, which was to give the silent treatment (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#6.4.1'>DN 16:6.4.1</a>).

## dn3:1.23.22
- **PLI**: Mā kho tumhe, māṇavakā, ambaṭṭhaṁ māṇavaṁ atibāḷhaṁ dāsiputtavādena nimmādetha, uḷāro so kaṇho isi ahosī”ti.
- **EN**: Young students, please don’t put Ambaṭṭha down too much by calling him the son of a slavegirl. That Black Boy was an eminent seer.”
