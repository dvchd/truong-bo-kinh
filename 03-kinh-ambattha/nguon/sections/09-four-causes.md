# 5 — Bốn lý do từ bỏ  (44 đoạn)

## dn3:2.3.0
- **PLI**: 5. Catuapāyamukha
- **EN**: 5. Four Causes of Quitting

## dn3:2.3.1  `csp1ed6.95, csp2ed6.95, ms6D_393, msdiv280, pts-cs2.3, pts-vp-pli1.101`
- **PLI**: Imāya kho, ambaṭṭha, anuttarāya vijjācaraṇasampadāya cattāri apāyamukhāni bhavanti.
- **EN**: There are these four causes of quitting this supreme knowledge and conduct.
- **NOTE**: In later Theravada, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>apāyamukha</i> refers to deeds that cause rebirth in lower realms. However this does not apply in the early texts; the acts described here are not evil. Rather, it means an “opening” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mukha</i>) for “departure” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>apāya</i>).

## dn3:2.3.2
- **PLI**: Katamāni cattāri?
- **EN**: What four?

## dn3:2.3.3
- **PLI**: Idha, ambaṭṭha, ekacco samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā imaññeva anuttaraṁ vijjācaraṇasampadaṁ anabhisambhuṇamāno khārividhamādāya araññāyatanaṁ ajjhogāhati:
- **EN**: Firstly, take some ascetic or brahmin who, not managing to obtain this supreme knowledge and conduct, plunges into a wilderness region carrying their pack with a shoulder-pole, thinking

## dn3:2.3.4
- **PLI**: ‘pavattaphalabhojano bhavissāmī’ti.
- **EN**: they will get by eating fallen fruit.
- **NOTE**: A common practice of pre-Buddhist hermits, who avoided the slightest harm to plants. Buddhist mendicants may also not harm plants, but they rely on alms and only eat fallen fruit in case of famine.

## dn3:2.3.5
- **PLI**: So aññadatthu vijjācaraṇasampannasseva paricārako sampajjati.
- **EN**: In fact they succeed only in serving someone accomplished in knowledge and conduct.
- **NOTE**: The Buddha inverts Ambaṭṭha’s earlier claim that the other three castes only succeed in serving brahmins (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn3/en/sujato#1.14.7'>DN 3:1.14.7</a>).

## dn3:2.3.6
- **PLI**: Imāya kho, ambaṭṭha, anuttarāya vijjācaraṇasampadāya idaṁ paṭhamaṁ apāyamukhaṁ bhavati.
- **EN**: This is the first cause of quitting this supreme knowledge and conduct.

## dn3:2.3.11  `bj7.208, cck9.130, dr9.134, lv1.276, ms6D_395, sya9.131, vri1.89`
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, ambaṭṭha, idhekacco samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā imañceva anuttaraṁ vijjācaraṇasampadaṁ anabhisambhuṇamāno pavattaphalabhojanatañca anabhisambhuṇamāno kandamūlaphalabhojanatañca anabhisambhuṇamāno gāmasāmantaṁ vā nigamasāmantaṁ vā agyāgāraṁ karitvā aggiṁ paricaranto acchati.
- **EN**: Furthermore, take some ascetic or brahmin who, not managing to obtain this supreme knowledge and conduct, or to get by eating fallen fruit, or to get by eating tubers and fruit, sets up a fire chamber in the neighborhood of a village or town and dwells there serving the sacred flame.
- **NOTE**: The “fire chamber” was a building set up for performing the fire ritual. The Buddha stayed in them from time to time (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn75/en/sujato#1.2'>MN 75:1.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#15.2.2'>Kd 1:15.2.2</a>). Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 13.4.1.8 describes how a king enters the fire chamber to spend the night with his wives, the king entering by the east door, the wives by the south. Kauṭilya says that a king should try religious cases there (Arthaśāstra 1.19.31).
