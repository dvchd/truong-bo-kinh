# 2.1 — Lần thứ nhất dùng chữ “tiện dân”  (15 đoạn)

## dn3:1.10.0
- **PLI**: 2.1. Paṭhamaibbhavāda
- **EN**: 2.1. The First Use of the Word “Primitives”

## dn3:1.10.1  `bj7.156, cck9.117, km14.223, lv1.246, lv1.247, ms6D_357, msdiv263, ndp6.79, vri1.79`
- **PLI**: “No hidaṁ, bho gotama.
- **EN**: “No, worthy Gotama.

## dn3:1.10.2
- **PLI**: Gacchanto vā hi, bho gotama, gacchantena brāhmaṇo brāhmaṇena saddhiṁ sallapitumarahati, ṭhito vā hi, bho gotama, ṭhitena brāhmaṇo brāhmaṇena saddhiṁ sallapitumarahati, nisinno vā hi, bho gotama, nisinnena brāhmaṇo brāhmaṇena saddhiṁ sallapitumarahati, sayāno vā hi, bho gotama, sayānena brāhmaṇo brāhmaṇena saddhiṁ sallapitumarahati.
- **EN**: For it is proper for one brahmin to converse with another while both are walking, standing, sitting, or lying down.

## dn3:1.10.3
- **PLI**: Ye ca kho te, bho gotama, muṇḍakā samaṇakā ibbhā kaṇhā bandhupādāpaccā, tehipi me saddhiṁ evaṁ kathāsallāpo hoti, yathariva bhotā gotamenā”ti.
- **EN**: But as to these shavelings, fake ascetics, primitives, black spawn from the feet of our kinsman, I converse with them as I do with the worthy Gotama.”
- **NOTE**: Note the racial connotations of using <i lang='pi' translate='no'>kaṇha</i> (“black”) as a slur. The brahmin caste hailed from the (relatively) fair-skinned Indo-Europeans who entered India from the north. Vedic texts indicate that there was Brahmanical prejudice against dark-skinned natives, but also that they were assimilated and raised to positions of honor.

## dn3:1.11.1  `pts-cs1.11`
- **PLI**: “Atthikavato kho pana te, ambaṭṭha, idhāgamanaṁ ahosi, yāyeva kho panatthāya āgaccheyyātha, tameva atthaṁ sādhukaṁ manasi kareyyātha.
- **EN**: “But Ambaṭṭha, you must have come here for some purpose. You should focus on that.

## dn3:1.11.2
- **PLI**: Avusitavāyeva kho pana, bho, ayaṁ ambaṭṭho māṇavo vusitamānī kimaññatra avusitattā”ti.
- **EN**: Though this Ambaṭṭha is unqualified, he thinks he’s qualified. What is that but lack of qualifications?”
- **NOTE**: Ambaṭṭha is “qualified” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vusita</i>) in scripture, but far from “qualified” in spiritual development. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Vusita</i> is normally an expression of arahantship: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ</i> (“the spiritual journey has been completed”).

## dn3:1.12.1  `csp1ed6.86, csp2ed6.86, dr9.119, km14.224, lv1.248, mc9.91, ms6D_358, msdiv264, pts-cs1.12, pts-vp-pli1.91, sya9.119`
- **PLI**: Atha kho ambaṭṭho māṇavo bhagavatā avusitavādena vuccamāno kupito anattamano bhagavantaṁyeva khuṁsento bhagavantaṁyeva vambhento bhagavantaṁyeva upavadamāno:
- **EN**: When he said this, Ambaṭṭha became angry and upset with the Buddha because of being described as unqualified. He even attacked and badmouthed the Buddha himself, saying,

## dn3:1.12.2
- **PLI**: “samaṇo ca me bho gotamo pāpito bhavissatī”ti bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: “The ascetic Gotama will be worsted!” He said to the Buddha,

## dn3:1.12.3
- **PLI**: “caṇḍā, bho gotama, sakyajāti;
- **EN**: “Worthy Gotama, the Sakyans are rude,

## dn3:1.12.4
- **PLI**: pharusā, bho gotama, sakyajāti;
- **EN**: harsh,

## dn3:1.12.5
- **PLI**: lahusā, bho gotama, sakyajāti;
- **EN**: touchy,

## dn3:1.12.6
- **PLI**: bhassā, bho gotama, sakyajāti;
- **EN**: and argumentative.
- **NOTE**: The PTS reading <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rabhasa</i> means “violent, aggressive”. But the commentary reads <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhassa</i>, explained as “speaking much”. Moreover, the story below does not demonstrate violence.

## dn3:1.12.7
- **PLI**: ibbhā santā ibbhā samānā na brāhmaṇe sakkaronti, na brāhmaṇe garuṁ karonti, na brāhmaṇe mānenti, na brāhmaṇe pūjenti, na brāhmaṇe apacāyanti.
- **EN**: Primitive they are, and primitive they remain! They don’t honor, respect, revere, worship, or venerate brahmins.
- **NOTE**: Ambaṭṭha despises the Sakyans as “primitives” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ibbha</i>) who do not respect Vedic culture. The word <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ibbha</i> (“primitive”) stems from a non-Aryan word for “elephant” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ibha</i>); the sub-commentary glosses it with <i lang='pi' translate='no'>hatthigopakā</i> (“elephant-keepers”). It originally referred to the native inhabitants who tamed elephants; see eg. Chāndogya Upaniṣad 1.10. At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp3.1/en/sujato#18.4'>Snp 3.1:18.4</a> the Buddha describes his own people as “natives” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>niketino</i>), those who have a long connection with the land.

## dn3:1.12.8
- **PLI**: Tayidaṁ, bho gotama, nacchannaṁ, tayidaṁ nappatirūpaṁ, yadime sakyā ibbhā santā ibbhā samānā na brāhmaṇe sakkaronti, na brāhmaṇe garuṁ karonti, na brāhmaṇe mānenti, na brāhmaṇe pūjenti, na brāhmaṇe apacāyantī”ti.
- **EN**: It is neither proper nor appropriate that the Sakyans—primitives that they are—don’t honor, respect, revere, worship, or venerate brahmins.”

## dn3:1.12.9
- **PLI**: Itiha ambaṭṭho māṇavo idaṁ paṭhamaṁ sakyesu ibbhavādaṁ nipātesi.
- **EN**: And that’s how Ambaṭṭha denigrated the Sakyans with the word “primitives” for the first time.
