## dn3:2.16.4
- **PLI**: Atha kho brāhmaṇo pokkharasāti sake nivesane paṇītaṁ khādanīyaṁ bhojanīyaṁ paṭiyādāpetvā yāne āropetvā ukkāsu dhāriyamānāsu ukkaṭṭhāya niyyāsi, yena icchānaṅgalavanasaṇḍo tena pāyāsi.
- **EN**: So Pokkharasāti had delicious fresh and cooked foods prepared in his own home. Then he mounted a carriage and, with attendants carrying torches, set out from Ukkaṭṭhā for the forest near Icchānaṅgala.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Khādanīya</i> and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>bhojanīya</i> are food categories commonly mentioned in Pali. Etymologically they stem from “hard and soft”. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Bhojanīya</i> is defined in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pc37/en/sujato#2.1.10'>Bu Pc 37:2.1.10</a> as grain, porridge, flour products, fish, and meat, thus being foods that are typically eaten cooked and “mooshed up” in with the fingers in the bowl. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Khādanīya</i> is not so readily defined, being essentially everything not included in other categories. But it would have included such “crunchy” things as fruit and vegetables, which may be eaten uncooked.

## dn3:2.17.1  `bj7.218, cck9.138, dr9.144, km14.258, lv1.294, lv1.295, mc9.107, ms6D_418, msdiv293, pts-cs2.17, sya9.139`

- **EN**: “And did you have some discussion with him?”

## dn3:2.17.5
- **PLI**: “Ahu kho me, brāhmaṇa, ambaṭṭhena māṇavena saddhiṁ kocideva kathāsallāpo”ti.

## dn3:2.17.6

## dn3:2.17.7

- **EN**: Then the Buddha informed Pokkharasāti of all they had discussed.

## dn3:2.17.8

- **EN**: Then Pokkharasāti said to the Buddha,

## dn3:2.17.9

- **EN**: “Ambaṭṭha is a fool, worthy Gotama. Please forgive him.”
- **NOTE**: So far has Ambaṭṭha fallen from the learned sage we were introduced to at the start of the sutta.

## dn3:2.17.10
- **PLI**: “Sukhī hotu, brāhmaṇa, ambaṭṭho māṇavo”ti.
- **EN**: “May the student Ambaṭṭha be happy, brahmin.”
- **NOTE**: The Buddha bears no ill will. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Sukhī hotu</i> is one of the most recognizable Pali phrases, but in early texts it is spoken only a few times: by the Buddha at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn21/en/sujato#1.8.8'>DN 21:1.8.8</a> and <a href='https://suttacentral.net/snp5.1/en/sujato#54.1'>Snp 5.1:54.1</a>; by Punabbasu’s Mother at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn10.7/en/sujato#10.1'>SN 10.7:10.1</a>; and by various women at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-ss5/en/sujato#1.4.8'>Bu Ss 5:1.4.8</a>.

## dn3:2.21.1  `cck9.140, lv1.298, ms6D_423, msdiv298, pts-cs2.21, pts-vp-pli1.110, sya9.141`
- **PLI**: Ekamantaṁ nisinnassa kho brāhmaṇassa pokkharasātissa bhagavā anupubbiṁ kathaṁ kathesi, seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: Then the Buddha taught him step by step, with

## dn3:2.21.2
- **PLI**: dānakathaṁ sīlakathaṁ saggakathaṁ; kāmānaṁ ādīnavaṁ okāraṁ saṅkilesaṁ, nekkhamme ānisaṁsaṁ pakāsesi.
- **EN**: a talk on giving, ethical conduct, and heaven. He revealed the drawbacks of sensual pleasures, so sordid and corrupt, and the benefit of renunciation.
- **NOTE**: While all these teachings feature commonly in the suttas, there is no text that depicts this framework in detail.

## dn3:2.21.3
- **PLI**: Yadā bhagavā aññāsi brāhmaṇaṁ pokkharasātiṁ kallacittaṁ muducittaṁ vinīvaraṇacittaṁ udaggacittaṁ pasannacittaṁ, atha yā buddhānaṁ sāmukkaṁsikā dhammadesanā, taṁ pakāsesi—
- **EN**: And when the Buddha knew that Pokkharasāti’s mind was ready, pliable, rid of hindrances, elated, and confident he revealed the special teaching of the Buddhas:

## dn3:2.21.4
- **PLI**: dukkhaṁ samudayaṁ nirodhaṁ maggaṁ.
- **EN**: suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path.
- **NOTE**: This is the briefest expression of the four noble truths.

## dn3:2.21.5
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpi nāma suddhaṁ vatthaṁ apagatakāḷakaṁ sammadeva rajanaṁ paṭiggaṇheyya;
- **EN**: Just as a clean cloth rid of stains would properly absorb dye,

## dn3:2.21.6
- **PLI**: evameva brāhmaṇassa pokkharasātissa tasmiññeva āsane virajaṁ vītamalaṁ dhammacakkhuṁ udapādi:
- **EN**: in that very seat the stainless, immaculate vision of the Dhamma arose in the brahmin Pokkharasāti:
- **NOTE**: This indicates that he became a stream-enterer (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>sotāpanna</i>), the first of four stages of Awakening. Such details of personal attainment are typically found in the narrative rather than the teaching attributed to the Buddha, and hence were added by redactors at some point. They vary considerably in different versions. In this case, the parallel at DA 20 says that he became a stream-enterer and later a non-returner. T 20 said that he understood the teaching and went for refuge, and agrees that he became a non-returner before his death.

## dn3:2.21.7
- **PLI**: “yaṁ kiñci samudayadhammaṁ sabbaṁ taṁ nirodhadhamman”ti.
- **EN**: “Everything that is liable to arise is liable to cease.”
- **NOTE**: This is the insight into universal impermanence and dependent origination.
