# Mở đầu — Bà-la-môn Pokkharasāti ở Ukkaṭṭhā  (12 đoạn)

## dn3:1.1.1  `bj7.150, cck9.112, csp1ed6.82, csp2ed6.82, dr9.114, km14.215, lv1.236, mc9.87, ms6D_347, msdiv254, ndp6.76, pts-cs1.1, pts-vp-pli1.87, sya9.114, vri1.76`
- **PLI**: Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
- **EN**: So I have heard.
- **NOTE**: This sutta marks a turning point where the Buddha’s teachings were embraced by the leading brahmin Pokkharasāti. The suttas that follow reverberate with the consequences of this encounter. He was one of the most influential brahmins of his time, although the Buddha elsewhere denied that he had any special knowledge (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#15.5'>MN 99:15.5</a>). Brahmanical texts confirm that he was a real person, an influential teacher around the time of the Buddha known as Pauṣkarasādi in Sanskrit. He is cited on grammar by Kātyāyana and Patañjali, and in the Taittirīya-prātiśākhya; on allowable food and theft in the Āpastamba Dharmasūtra; and on Vedic ritual in the Śāṅkhāyana-Āraṇyaka. His name identifies him as descended from a man of Puṣkarāvati, capital of Gandhāra. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#10.3'>MN 99:10.3</a> clarifies that he is of the Upamañña lineage.

## dn3:1.1.2
- **PLI**: ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṁ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi yena icchānaṅgalaṁ nāma kosalānaṁ brāhmaṇagāmo tadavasari.
- **EN**: At one time the Buddha was wandering in the land of the Kosalans together with a large Saṅgha of five hundred mendicants when he arrived at a village of the Kosalan brahmins named Icchānaṅgala.
- **NOTE**: Icchānaṅgala was a center east of Sāvatthī for the innovative brahmins of the Kosala region.

## dn3:1.1.3
- **PLI**: Tatra sudaṁ bhagavā icchānaṅgale viharati icchānaṅgalavanasaṇḍe.
- **EN**: He stayed in a forest near Icchānaṅgala.

## dn3:1.2.0
- **PLI**: 1. Pokkharasātivatthu
- **EN**: 1. The Section on Pokkharasāti

## dn3:1.2.1  `cck9.113, km14.216, lv1.237, lv1.238, ms6D_348, msdiv255, pts-cs1.2, pts-vp-pli1.88, sya9.115`
- **PLI**: Tena kho pana samayena brāhmaṇo pokkharasāti ukkaṭṭhaṁ ajjhāvasati sattussadaṁ satiṇakaṭṭhodakaṁ sadhaññaṁ rājabhoggaṁ raññā pasenadinā kosalena dinnaṁ rājadāyaṁ brahmadeyyaṁ.
- **EN**: Now at that time the brahmin Pokkharasāti was living in Ukkaṭṭhā. It was a crown property given by King Pasenadi of Kosala, teeming with living creatures, full of hay, wood, water, and grain, a royal park endowed to a brahmin.
- **NOTE**: Ukkaṭṭhā is mentioned only rarely, and always in the context of extraordinary teachings and events that emphasize the cosmic grandeur of the Buddha against the brahmins (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn14/en/sujato#3.29.1'>DN 14:3.29.1</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn1/en/sujato#1.2'>MN 1:1.2</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn49/en/sujato#2.1'>MN 49:2.1</a>). Sanskrit sources call it a <i lang='pi' translate='no'>droṇamukha</i>, a leading market town accessible by land and water (Divyāvadāna 319.010). At <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#10.3'>MN 99:10.3</a> Pokkharasāti is said to be “of the Subhaga Forest”. | “Royal park” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājadāya</i> (cp. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>migadāya</i>, “deer park”). | A <i lang='pi' translate='no'>brahmadeyya</i> is a gift of land by a king to a brahmin, which was an outstanding feature of Indian feudalism.

## dn3:1.2.2
- **PLI**: Assosi kho brāhmaṇo pokkharasāti:
- **EN**: Pokkharasāti heard:

## dn3:1.2.3
- **PLI**: “samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito kosalesu cārikaṁ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi icchānaṅgalaṁ anuppatto icchānaṅgale viharati icchānaṅgalavanasaṇḍe.
- **EN**: “It seems the ascetic Gotama—a Sakyan, gone forth from a Sakyan family—has arrived at Icchānaṅgala and is staying in a forest nearby.

## dn3:1.2.4
- **PLI**: Taṁ kho pana bhavantaṁ gotamaṁ evaṁ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato—
- **EN**: He has this good reputation:

## dn3:1.2.5
- **PLI**: ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā.’
- **EN**: ‘That Blessed One is perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.’

## dn3:1.2.6
- **PLI**: So imaṁ lokaṁ sadevakaṁ samārakaṁ sabrahmakaṁ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṁ pajaṁ sadevamanussaṁ sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti.
- **EN**: He has realized with his own insight this world—with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, gods and humans—and he makes it known to others.
- **NOTE**: Contrast with his rejection of this possibility at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn99/en/sujato#10.7'>MN 99:10.7</a>.

## dn3:1.2.7
- **PLI**: So dhammaṁ deseti ādikalyāṇaṁ majjhekalyāṇaṁ pariyosānakalyāṇaṁ, sātthaṁ sabyañjanaṁ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṁ parisuddhaṁ brahmacariyaṁ pakāseti.
- **EN**: He teaches Dhamma that’s good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, meaningful and well-phrased. And he reveals a spiritual practice that’s entirely full and pure.

## dn3:1.2.8
- **PLI**: Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṁ arahataṁ dassanaṁ hotī”ti.
- **EN**: It’s good to see such perfected ones.”
- **NOTE**: Pokkharasāti does not care whether the Buddha identified as a follower of the Vedas. The wise do not concern themselves with religious identity.
