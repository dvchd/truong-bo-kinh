## dn29:18.3
- **PLI**: ‘ayaṁ kho āyasmā atthañceva micchā gaṇhāti, byañjanāni ca micchā ropetī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘This venerable misconstrues the meaning and mistakes the phrasing.’

## dn29:18.4
- **PLI**: Tassa neva abhinanditabbaṁ na paṭikkositabbaṁ, anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā so evamassa vacanīyo:
- **EN**: You should neither approve nor reject them, but say,
- **NOTE**: The “meaning” is the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>attha</i>. Here we see the source of the <i lang='pi' translate='no'>atthakathā</i>, which we translate as “commentaries”, but which are literally “discussions on the meaning”. Such discussions are undertaken through a civil and polite inquiry.

## dn29:18.5
- **PLI**: ‘imassa nu kho, āvuso, atthassa imāni vā byañjanāni etāni vā byañjanāni katamāni opāyikatarāni, imesañca byañjanānaṁ ayaṁ vā attho eso vā attho katamo opāyikataro’ti?
- **EN**: ‘Reverend, if this is the meaning, the phrasing may either be this or that: which is more fitting? And if this is the phrasing, the meaning may be either this or that: which is more fitting?’
- **NOTE**: Meaning and phrasing are interdependent, so care must be taken with both. Often it happens that when the phrasing slips, a new and unwarranted meaning is imputed.

## dn29:18.6
- **PLI**: So ce evaṁ vadeyya:
- **EN**: Suppose they reply,

## dn29:18.7
- **PLI**: ‘imassa kho, āvuso, atthassa imāneva byañjanāni opāyikatarāni, yā ceva etāni; imesañca byañjanānaṁ ayameva attho opāyikataro, yā ceva eso’ti.
- **EN**: ‘This phrasing fits the meaning better than that. And this meaning fits the phrasing better than that.’

## dn29:18.8
- **PLI**: So neva ussādetabbo na apasādetabbo, anussādetvā anapasādetvā sveva sādhukaṁ saññāpetabbo tassa ca atthassa tesañca byañjanānaṁ nisantiyā.
- **EN**: Without flattering or rebuking them, you should carefully persuade them by examining that meaning and that phrasing.
