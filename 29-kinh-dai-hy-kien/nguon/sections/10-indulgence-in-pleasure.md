- **EN**: 9. Indulgence in Pleasure

## dn29:23.1  `csp1ed8.108, csp2ed8.108, dr11.146, ms8D_361, msdiv183, pts-cs23, vri3.97`
- **PLI**: Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, cunda, vijjati yaṁ aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ vadeyyuṁ:
- **EN**: It’s possible that wanderers of other religions might say,

## dn29:23.2
- **PLI**: ‘sukhallikānuyogamanuyuttā samaṇā sakyaputtiyā viharantī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘The ascetics who follow the Sakyan live indulging in pleasure.’
- **NOTE**: This repurposes the phrase used right at the beginning of the Buddha’s first teaching, the Dhammacakkappavattasutta (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato#2.3'>SN 56.11:2.3</a>). There he was persuading hard-bitten ascetics that he did not indulge in sensual pleasures. While the affiliation of those five ascetics is not stated explicitly, clearly their practices were similar to the Jains. And given the context, no doubt the Buddha has the Jains in mind here.

## dn29:23.3
- **PLI**: Evaṁvādino, cunda, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā:
- **EN**: You should say to them,

## dn29:23.4
- **PLI**: ‘katamo so, āvuso, sukhallikānuyogo?
- **EN**: ‘What is that indulgence in pleasure?

## dn29:23.5
- **PLI**: Sukhallikānuyogā hi bahū anekavihitā nānappakārakā’ti.
- **EN**: For there are many different kinds of indulgence in pleasure.’

## dn29:23.6  `bj9.218, ms8D_362`

- **EN**: These four kinds of indulgence in pleasure, Cunda, are low, crude, ordinary, ignoble, and pointless. They don’t lead to disillusionment, dispassion, cessation, peace, insight, awakening, and extinguishment.
