# Muc 25 — The Benefits of Indulgence in Pleasure  (15 đoạn)

## dn29:25.0
- **PLI**: 10. Sukhallikānuyogānisaṁsa
- **EN**: 10. The Benefits of Indulgence in Pleasure

## dn29:25.1  `cck11.148, csp1ed8.110, csp2ed8.110, dr11.149, ms8D_376, msdiv185, ndp8.103, pts-cs25, sya11.146, vri3.99`
- **PLI**: Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, cunda, vijjati, yaṁ aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ vadeyyuṁ:
- **EN**: It’s possible that wanderers of other religions might say,

## dn29:25.2
- **PLI**: ‘ime panāvuso, cattāro sukhallikānuyoge anuyuttānaṁ viharataṁ kati phalāni katānisaṁsā pāṭikaṅkhā’ti?
- **EN**: ‘How many fruits and benefits may be expected by those who live indulging in pleasure in these four ways?’

## dn29:25.3
- **PLI**: Evaṁvādino, cunda, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā:
- **EN**: You should say to them,

## dn29:25.4
- **PLI**: ‘ime kho, āvuso, cattāro sukhallikānuyoge anuyuttānaṁ viharataṁ cattāri phalāni cattāro ānisaṁsā pāṭikaṅkhā.
- **EN**: ‘Four benefits may be expected by those who live indulging in pleasure in these four ways.

## dn29:25.5
- **PLI**: Katame cattāro?
- **EN**: What four?

## dn29:25.6
- **PLI**: Idhāvuso, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā sotāpanno hoti avinipātadhammo niyato sambodhiparāyaṇo.
- **EN**: Firstly, with the ending of three fetters a mendicant becomes a stream-enterer, not liable to be reborn in the underworld, assured, destined for awakening.

## dn29:25.7
- **PLI**: Idaṁ paṭhamaṁ phalaṁ, paṭhamo ānisaṁso.
- **EN**: This is the first fruit and benefit.

## dn29:25.8
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, āvuso, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṁ tanuttā sakadāgāmī hoti, sakideva imaṁ lokaṁ āgantvā dukkhassantaṁ karoti.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant—with the ending of three fetters, and the weakening of greed, hate, and delusion—becomes a once-returner. They come back to this world once only, then make an end of suffering.

## dn29:25.9
- **PLI**: Idaṁ dutiyaṁ phalaṁ, dutiyo ānisaṁso.
- **EN**: This is the second fruit and benefit.

## dn29:25.10
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, āvuso, bhikkhu pañcannaṁ orambhāgiyānaṁ saṁyojanānaṁ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti, tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā.
- **EN**: Furthermore, with the ending of the five lower fetters, a mendicant is reborn spontaneously and will become extinguished there, not liable to return from that world.

## dn29:25.11
- **PLI**: Idaṁ tatiyaṁ phalaṁ, tatiyo ānisaṁso.
- **EN**: This is the third fruit and benefit.

## dn29:25.12
- **PLI**: Puna caparaṁ, āvuso, bhikkhu āsavānaṁ khayā anāsavaṁ cetovimuttiṁ paññāvimuttiṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati.
- **EN**: Furthermore, a mendicant realizes the undefiled freedom of heart and freedom by wisdom, and lives having realized it with their own insight in this very life due to the ending of defilements.

## dn29:25.13
- **PLI**: Idaṁ catutthaṁ phalaṁ catuttho ānisaṁso.
- **EN**: This is the fourth fruit and benefit.

## dn29:25.14
- **PLI**: Ime kho, āvuso, cattāro sukhallikānuyoge anuyuttānaṁ viharataṁ imāni cattāri phalāni, cattāro ānisaṁsā pāṭikaṅkhā’ti.
- **EN**: These four benefits may be expected by those who live indulging in pleasure in these four ways.’
- **NOTE**: Some meditation teachers warn of the supposed dangers of becoming attached to the pleasure of meditation. The Buddha’s view was that the pleasure of meditation arises from letting go and leads to Nibbāna.
