- **PLI**: So samateneva sudaṁ janapadaṁ pasāsati.
- **EN**: He just governed the country according to his own ideas.
- **NOTE**: “Own ideas” is <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sa-mata</i>.

## dn26:9.6
- **PLI**: Tassa samatena janapadaṁ pasāsato pubbenāparaṁ janapadā na pabbanti, yathā taṁ pubbakānaṁ rājūnaṁ ariye cakkavattivatte vattamānānaṁ.
- **EN**: So governed, the country’s people did not prosper like before, as they had when former kings proceeded in the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pabbanti</i> is unusual and probably unique in early Pali. It is related to Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>parvati</i> (“fill”) and is explained by the commentary as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>vaḍḍhanti</i> (“grow, prosper”). | I assume <i lang='pi' translate='no'>janapadā</i> (“countries”) is a misspelling of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>jānapadā</i> (“people of the country”), as <i lang='pi' translate='no'>janapada</i> is otherwise in singular.

## dn26:9.7  `csp1ed8.54, csp2ed8.54, ms8D_201, pts-vp-pli3.65`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, amaccā pārisajjā gaṇakamahāmattā anīkaṭṭhā dovārikā mantassājīvino sannipatitvā rājānaṁ khattiyaṁ muddhābhisittaṁ etadavocuṁ:
- **EN**: Then the ministers and councillors, the treasurers and chief ministers, military officers, guardsmen, and professional advisers gathered and said to the king,
- **NOTE**: “Military officers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anīkaṭṭhā</i>) would have been standing officers in the army. According to both the commentary here and Monier-Williams’ Sanskrit Dictionary (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anīkastha</i>), it includes elephant officers, who would have stood at the head of the army. | “Professional advisers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>mantassājīvino</i>) is explained by the commentary as those who make a living from giving wise advice.
