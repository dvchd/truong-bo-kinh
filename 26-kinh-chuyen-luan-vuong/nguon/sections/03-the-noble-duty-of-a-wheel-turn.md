# Muc 5 — The Noble Duty of a Wheel-Turning Monarch  (9 đoạn)

## dn26:5.0
- **PLI**: 2.1. Cakkavattiariyavatta
- **EN**: 2.1. The Noble Duty of a Wheel-Turning Monarch

## dn26:5.1  `bj9.102, cck11.67, dr11.65, ms8D_190, msdiv84, pts-cs5, pts-vp-pli3.61, sya11.65`
- **PLI**: ‘Katamaṁ pana taṁ, deva, ariyaṁ cakkavattivattan’ti?
- **EN**: ‘But Your Majesty, what is the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch?’

## dn26:5.2
- **PLI**: ‘Tena hi tvaṁ, tāta, dhammaṁyeva nissāya dhammaṁ sakkaronto dhammaṁ garuṁ karonto dhammaṁ mānento dhammaṁ pūjento dhammaṁ apacāyamāno dhammaddhajo dhammaketu dhammādhipateyyo dhammikaṁ rakkhāvaraṇaguttiṁ saṁvidahassu antojanasmiṁ balakāyasmiṁ khattiyesu anuyantesu brāhmaṇagahapatikesu negamajānapadesu samaṇabrāhmaṇesu migapakkhīsu.
- **EN**: ‘Well then, my dear, relying only on principle—honoring, respecting, and venerating principle, having principle as your flag, banner, and authority—provide just protection and security for your court, troops, aristocrats, vassals, brahmins and householders, people of town and country, ascetics and brahmins, beasts and birds.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Saṁvidahassu</i> is second middle imperative.

## dn26:5.3
- **PLI**: Mā ca te, tāta, vijite adhammakāro pavattittha.
- **EN**: Do not let injustice prevail in the realm.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Pavattittha</i> is aorist middle. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>(A)dhammakāra</i> (“(in)justice”) doesn’t seem to occur elsewhere in early Pali. It is found in Ja 513 and Ja 498, both times in the context of just rule.

## dn26:5.4
- **PLI**: Ye ca te, tāta, vijite adhanā assu, tesañca dhanamanuppadeyyāsi.
- **EN**: Provide money to the penniless in the realm.
- **NOTE**: “Provide” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anuppadeti</i>) is used elsewhere in the sense of paying back a loan (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.20/en/sujato#3.5'>AN 3.20:3.5</a>), serving honored guests (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/sn7.2/en/sujato#2.6'>SN 7.2:2.6</a>), paying wages (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn31/en/sujato#32.2'>DN 31:32.2</a>), or a king supplying provisions for essential workers to do their job (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn5/en/sujato#11.8'>DN 5:11.8</a>). It is thus more about fulfilling a moral obligation of fairness than offering charity.

## dn26:5.5
- **PLI**: Ye ca te, tāta, vijite samaṇabrāhmaṇā madappamādā paṭiviratā khantisoracce niviṭṭhā ekamattānaṁ damenti, ekamattānaṁ samenti, ekamattānaṁ parinibbāpenti, te kālena kālaṁ upasaṅkamitvā paripuccheyyāsi pariggaṇheyyāsi:
- **EN**: And there are ascetics and brahmins in the realm who refrain from intoxication and negligence, are settled in patience and gentleness, and who tame, calm, and extinguish themselves. From time to time you should go up to them and ask and learn:
- **NOTE**: Where the phrase “from time to time go to them” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>te kālena kālaṁ upasaṅkamitvā</i>) occurs elsewhere in the suttas, it is always followed by “ask and question” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>paripucchati paripañhati</i>). Here, however, the second verb is “you should learn” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pariggaṇheyyāsi</i>).

## dn26:5.6
- **PLI**: “kiṁ, bhante, kusalaṁ, kiṁ akusalaṁ, kiṁ sāvajjaṁ, kiṁ anavajjaṁ, kiṁ sevitabbaṁ, kiṁ na sevitabbaṁ, kiṁ me karīyamānaṁ dīgharattaṁ ahitāya dukkhāya assa, kiṁ vā pana me karīyamānaṁ dīgharattaṁ hitāya sukhāya assā”ti?
- **EN**: “Sirs, what is skillful? What is unskillful? What is blameworthy? What is blameless? What should be cultivated? What should not be cultivated? Doing what leads to my lasting harm and suffering? Doing what leads to my lasting welfare and happiness?”

## dn26:5.7
- **PLI**: Tesaṁ sutvā yaṁ akusalaṁ taṁ abhinivajjeyyāsi, yaṁ kusalaṁ taṁ samādāya vatteyyāsi.
- **EN**: Having heard them, you should shun what is unskillful and proceed having undertaken what is skillful.

## dn26:5.8
- **PLI**: Idaṁ kho, tāta, taṁ ariyaṁ cakkavattivattan’ti.
- **EN**: This is the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.’
