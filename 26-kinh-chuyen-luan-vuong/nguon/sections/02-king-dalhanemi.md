# Muc 2 — King Daḷhanemi  (29 đoạn)

## dn26:2.0
- **PLI**: 2. Daḷhanemicakkavattirājā
- **EN**: 2. King Daḷhanemi

## dn26:2.1  `bj9.98, cck11.64, dr11.63, ms8D_186, msdiv81, pts-cs2, pts-vp-pli3.59, sya11.63, vri3.43`
- **PLI**: Bhūtapubbaṁ, bhikkhave, rājā daḷhanemi nāma ahosi cakkavattī dhammiko dhammarājā cāturanto vijitāvī janapadatthāvariyappatto sattaratanasamannāgato.
- **EN**: Once upon a time, mendicants, there was a king named Daḷhanemi who was a wheel-turning monarch, a just and principled king. His dominion extended to all four sides, he achieved stability in the country, and he possessed the seven treasures.
- **NOTE**: We have heard the story of the wheel-turning monarch Mahāsudassana in <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn17/en/sujato'>DN 17</a>. Daḷhanemi appears only here. His name means “Strong-rim”, such being one of the characteristics of the Wheel. His story of renunciation shares much in common with that of Maghadeva (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn83/en/sujato'>MN 83</a>).

## dn26:2.2
- **PLI**: Tassimāni satta ratanāni ahesuṁ seyyathidaṁ—
- **EN**: He had the following seven treasures:

## dn26:2.3
- **PLI**: cakkaratanaṁ hatthiratanaṁ assaratanaṁ maṇiratanaṁ itthiratanaṁ gahapatiratanaṁ pariṇāyakaratanameva sattamaṁ.
- **EN**: the wheel, the elephant, the horse, the jewel, the woman, the householder, and the commander as the seventh treasure.

## dn26:2.4
- **PLI**: Parosahassaṁ kho panassa puttā ahesuṁ sūrā vīraṅgarūpā parasenappamaddanā.
- **EN**: He had over a thousand sons who were valiant and heroic, crushing the armies of his enemies.

## dn26:2.5
- **PLI**: So imaṁ pathaviṁ sāgarapariyantaṁ adaṇḍena asatthena dhammena abhivijiya ajjhāvasi.
- **EN**: After conquering this land girt by sea, he reigned by principle, without rod or sword.

## dn26:3.1  `cck11.65, csp1ed8.49, csp2ed8.49, ms8D_187, msdiv82, ndp8.47, pts-cs3, pts-vp-pli3.60, sya11.64`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, rājā daḷhanemi bahunnaṁ vassānaṁ bahunnaṁ vassasatānaṁ bahunnaṁ vassasahassānaṁ accayena aññataraṁ purisaṁ āmantesi:
- **EN**: Then, after many years, many hundred years, many thousand years had passed, King Daḷhanemi addressed one of his men,

## dn26:3.2
- **PLI**: ‘yadā tvaṁ, ambho purisa, passeyyāsi dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ osakkitaṁ ṭhānā cutaṁ, atha me āroceyyāsī’ti.
- **EN**: ‘My man, when you see that the heavenly wheel-treasure has receded back from its place, please tell me.’
- **NOTE**: “Receded back” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>osakkitaṁ</i>): like a woman shrinking away from a man’s unwanted advances (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn12/en/sujato#16.12'>DN 12:16.12</a>).

## dn26:3.3
- **PLI**: ‘Evaṁ, devā’ti kho, bhikkhave, so puriso rañño daḷhanemissa paccassosi.
- **EN**: ‘Yes, Your Majesty,’ replied that man.

## dn26:3.4
- **PLI**: Addasā kho, bhikkhave, so puriso bahunnaṁ vassānaṁ bahunnaṁ vassasatānaṁ bahunnaṁ vassasahassānaṁ accayena dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ osakkitaṁ ṭhānā cutaṁ, disvāna yena rājā daḷhanemi tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā rājānaṁ daḷhanemiṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: After many thousand years had passed, that man saw that the heavenly wheel-treasure had receded back from its place. So he went to King Daḷhanemi and said,

## dn26:3.5
- **PLI**: ‘yagghe, deva, jāneyyāsi, dibbaṁ te cakkaratanaṁ osakkitaṁ ṭhānā cutan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Please Your Majesty, you should know that your heavenly wheel-treasure has receded back from its place.’

## dn26:3.6
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, rājā daḷhanemi jeṭṭhaputtaṁ kumāraṁ āmantāpetvā etadavoca:
- **EN**: So the king summoned the crown prince and said,

## dn26:3.7
- **PLI**: ‘dibbaṁ kira me, tāta kumāra, cakkaratanaṁ osakkitaṁ ṭhānā cutaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Dear prince, my heavenly wheel-treasure has receded back from its place.

## dn26:3.8
- **PLI**: Sutaṁ kho pana metaṁ—
- **EN**: I’ve heard that

## dn26:3.9
- **PLI**: yassa rañño cakkavattissa dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ osakkati ṭhānā cavati, na dāni tena raññā ciraṁ jīvitabbaṁ hotīti.
- **EN**: when this happens to a wheel-turning monarch, he does not have long to live.

## dn26:3.10
- **PLI**: Bhuttā kho pana me mānusakā kāmā, samayo dāni me dibbe kāme pariyesituṁ.
- **EN**: I have enjoyed human pleasures. Now it is time for me to seek heavenly pleasures.
- **NOTE**: The same point of view is expressed at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bi-vb-pc21/en/sujato#1.4'>Bi Pc 21:1.4</a>, where sex workers teased nuns, suggesting they enjoy themselves while young and ordain when old. The Buddhist position is that Dhamma can be practiced at any age.

## dn26:3.11
- **PLI**: Ehi tvaṁ, tāta kumāra, imaṁ samuddapariyantaṁ pathaviṁ paṭipajja.
- **EN**: Come, dear prince, rule this land surrounded by ocean!

## dn26:3.12
- **PLI**: Ahaṁ pana kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajissāmī’ti.
- **EN**: I shall shave off my hair and beard, dress in ocher robes, and go forth from the lay life to homelessness.’

## dn26:3.13  `bj9.100, dr11.64, ms8D_188, msdiv83`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, rājā daḷhanemi jeṭṭhaputtaṁ kumāraṁ sādhukaṁ rajje samanusāsitvā kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbaji.
- **EN**: And so, after carefully instructing the crown prince in kingship, King Daḷhanemi shaved off his hair and beard, dressed in ocher robes, and went forth from the lay life to homelessness.
- **NOTE**: The signs of the renunciate predate Buddhism.

## dn26:3.14
- **PLI**: Sattāhapabbajite kho pana, bhikkhave, rājisimhi dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ antaradhāyi.
- **EN**: Seven days later the heavenly wheel-treasure vanished.

## dn26:4.1  `cck11.66, csp1ed8.50, csp2ed8.50, ms8D_189, ndp8.48, pts-cs4, vri3.44`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, aññataro puriso yena rājā khattiyo muddhābhisitto tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā rājānaṁ khattiyaṁ muddhābhisittaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: Then a certain man approached the newly anointed aristocrat king and said,

## dn26:4.2
- **PLI**: ‘yagghe, deva, jāneyyāsi, dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ antarahitan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Please Your Majesty, you should know that the heavenly wheel-treasure has vanished.’

## dn26:4.3
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, rājā khattiyo muddhābhisitto dibbe cakkaratane antarahite anattamano ahosi, anattamanatañca paṭisaṁvedesi.
- **EN**: At that the king was unhappy and experienced unhappiness.

## dn26:4.4
- **PLI**: So yena rājisi tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā rājisiṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: He went to the royal seer and said,
- **NOTE**: The “royal seer” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājisi</i>, Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>rājarṣi</i>) is described by Kauṭilya (Arthaśāstra 1.7.1). Such a king should pursue pleasures in moderation, rely on good advice, and work energetically for the welfare and prosperity of his people.

## dn26:4.5
- **PLI**: ‘yagghe, deva, jāneyyāsi, dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ antarahitan’ti.
- **EN**: ‘Please Your Majesty, you should know that the heavenly wheel-treasure has vanished.’

## dn26:4.6
- **PLI**: Evaṁ vutte, bhikkhave, rājisi rājānaṁ khattiyaṁ muddhābhisittaṁ etadavoca:
- **EN**: When he said this, the royal seer said to him,

## dn26:4.7
- **PLI**: ‘mā kho tvaṁ, tāta, dibbe cakkaratane antarahite anattamano ahosi, mā anattamanatañca paṭisaṁvedesi, na hi te, tāta, dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ pettikaṁ dāyajjaṁ.
- **EN**: ‘Don’t be unhappy at the vanishing of the wheel-treasure. My dear, the wheel-treasure is not inherited from your father.
- **NOTE**: “Inherited from your father” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pettikaṁ dāyajjaṁ</i>) calls back to the four <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satipaṭṭhānas</i> that for a mendicant are “the domain of your fathers” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>pettike visaye</i>). In both cases they are to be earned, not conferred by lineage.

## dn26:4.8
- **PLI**: Iṅgha tvaṁ, tāta, ariye cakkavattivatte vattāhi.
- **EN**: Come now, my dear, proceed in the noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch.
- **NOTE**: “The noble duty of a wheel-turning monarch” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariye cakkavattivatte</i>; this is locative singular). <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ariya</i> (“noble”) qualifies “duty” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>vatta</i>), as it does in the title below, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>cakkavattiariyavatta</i>, rather than “monarch”. Normally <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> is reserved for those who have attained the Buddhist path, but here we might have a rare use of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariya</i> in reference to Indo-Aryan (Vedic) culture. Compare the use of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyaka</i> for Indo-Aryan language (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-pj1/en/sujato#8.4.10'>Bu Pj 1:8.4.10</a>), and <i lang='pi' translate='no'>ariyaṁ āyatanaṁ</i> for the “civilized region” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato#1.28.7'>DN 16:1.28.7</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an6.96/en/sujato#1.3'>AN 6.96:1.3</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/ud8.6/en/sujato#17.9'>Ud 8.6:17.9</a>). However, the commentary says it simply means “flawless” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>niddoso</i>).

## dn26:4.9
- **PLI**: Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ vijjati, yaṁ te ariye cakkavattivatte vattamānassa tadahuposathe pannarase sīsaṁnhātassa uposathikassa uparipāsādavaragatassa dibbaṁ cakkaratanaṁ pātubhavissati sahassāraṁ sanemikaṁ sanābhikaṁ sabbākāraparipūran’ti.
- **EN**: If you do so, it’s possible that—on a fifteenth day sabbath, having bathed your head and gone upstairs in the royal longhouse to observe the sabbath—the heavenly wheel-treasure will appear to you, with a thousand spokes, with rim and hub, complete in every detail.’
