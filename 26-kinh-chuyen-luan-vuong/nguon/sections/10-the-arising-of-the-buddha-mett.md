# Muc 25 — The Arising of the Buddha Metteyya  (17 đoạn)

## dn26:25.0
- **PLI**: 8. Metteyyabuddhuppāda
- **EN**: 8. The Arising of the Buddha Metteyya

## dn26:25.1  `cck11.85, ms8D_229, msdiv107, pts-cs25, pts-vp-pli3.76, sya11.84, vri3.56`
- **PLI**: Asītivassasahassāyukesu, bhikkhave, manussesu metteyyo nāma bhagavā loke uppajjissati arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā.
- **EN**: And the Blessed One named Metteyya will arise in the world—perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed—
- **NOTE**: This is the only sutta appearance of the future Buddha Metteyya (Sanskrit Maitreya, meaning “one full of love”). He became a prominent figure in later Buddhism.

## dn26:25.2
- **PLI**: Seyyathāpāhametarahi loke uppanno arahaṁ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṁ buddho bhagavā.
- **EN**: just as I have arisen today.
- **NOTE**: Throughout, the Buddha is concerned to emphasize that the future Buddha will offer no more or less than his own teaching and practice. He wanted people to practice now, not to establish a cult of a future savior.

## dn26:25.3
- **PLI**: So imaṁ lokaṁ sadevakaṁ samārakaṁ sabrahmakaṁ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṁ pajaṁ sadevamanussaṁ sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedessati, seyyathāpāhametarahi imaṁ lokaṁ sadevakaṁ samārakaṁ sabrahmakaṁ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṁ pajaṁ sadevamanussaṁ sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedemi.
- **EN**: He will realize with his own insight this world—with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, gods and humans—and make it known to others, just as I do today.

## dn26:25.4
- **PLI**: So dhammaṁ desessati ādikalyāṇaṁ majjhekalyāṇaṁ pariyosānakalyāṇaṁ sātthaṁ sabyañjanaṁ kevalaparipuṇṇaṁ parisuddhaṁ brahmacariyaṁ pakāsessati;
- **EN**: He will teach the Dhamma that’s good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, meaningful and well-phrased. And he will reveal a spiritual practice that’s entirely full and pure,

## dn26:25.5
- **PLI**: seyyathāpāhametarahi dhammaṁ desemi ādikalyāṇaṁ majjhekalyāṇaṁ pariyosānakalyāṇaṁ sātthaṁ sabyañjanaṁ kevalaparipuṇṇaṁ parisuddhaṁ brahmacariyaṁ pakāsemi.
- **EN**: just as I do today.

## dn26:25.6
- **PLI**: So anekasahassaṁ bhikkhusaṅghaṁ pariharissati, seyyathāpāhametarahi anekasataṁ bhikkhusaṅghaṁ pariharāmi.
- **EN**: He will lead a Saṅgha of many thousand mendicants, just as I lead a Saṅgha of many hundreds today.
- **NOTE**: The maximum number of monastics cited in the suttas is 1250 at a single gathering.

## dn26:26.1  `bj9.132, cck11.86, csp1ed8.64, csp2ed8.64, dr11.84, ms8D_230, msdiv108, ndp8.61, pts-cs26, pts-vp-pli3.77`
- **PLI**: Atha kho, bhikkhave, saṅkho nāma rājā yo so yūpo raññā mahāpanādena kārāpito.
- **EN**: Then King Saṅkha will have the sacrificial post once built by King Mahāpanāda raised up.
- **NOTE**: This event is found in Bhaddaji’s verses at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag2.22/en/sujato'>Thag 2.22</a>, expanded to a full story at Ja 264. The commentary explains <i lang='pi' translate='no'>yūpa</i> as “palace”, but Bhaddaji’s verses clearly depict a sacrificial post, to which the animal would be tied for slaughter. Such posts are connected with the horse sacrifice, which establishes imperial power. This is but one aspect of the wheel-turning monarch to draw on imagery from the horse sacrifice. Here, of course, no horses are killed.

## dn26:26.2
- **PLI**: Taṁ yūpaṁ ussāpetvā ajjhāvasitvā taṁ datvā vissajjitvā samaṇabrāhmaṇakapaṇaddhikavaṇibbakayācakānaṁ dānaṁ datvā metteyyassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa santike kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajissati.
- **EN**: Having reigned, he will abdicate, offering charity to ascetics and brahmins, paupers, vagrants, supplicants, and beggars. Then, having shaved off his hair and beard and dressed in ocher robes, he will go forth from the lay life to homelessness in the Buddha Metteyya’s presence.
- **NOTE**: <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Ajjhāvasati</i> in this sutta means “reign” not “dwell” (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn26/en/sujato#2.5'>DN 26:2.5</a>).

## dn26:26.3
- **PLI**: So evaṁ pabbajito samāno eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto viharanto nacirasseva—yassatthāya kulaputtā sammadeva agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajanti, tadanuttaraṁ—brahmacariyapariyosānaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharissati.
- **EN**: Soon after going forth, living withdrawn, diligent, keen, and resolute, he will realize the supreme culmination of the spiritual path in this very life. He will live having achieved with his own insight the goal for which gentlemen rightly go forth from the lay life to homelessness.

## dn26:27.1  `ms8D_231, msdiv109, pts-cs27, sya11.85`
- **PLI**: Attadīpā, bhikkhave, viharatha attasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā, dhammadīpā dhammasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā.
- **EN**: Mendicants, live as your own island, your own refuge, with no other refuge. Let the teaching be your island and your refuge, with no other refuge.

## dn26:27.2
- **PLI**: Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu attadīpo viharati attasaraṇo anaññasaraṇo dhammadīpo dhammasaraṇo anaññasaraṇo?
- **EN**: And how does a mendicant do this?

## dn26:27.3
- **PLI**: Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of the body—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world.

## dn26:27.4
- **PLI**: Vedanāsu vedanānupassī …pe…
- **EN**: They meditate observing an aspect of feelings …

## dn26:27.5
- **PLI**: citte cittānupassī …pe…
- **EN**: mind …

## dn26:27.6
- **PLI**: dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ.
- **EN**: principles—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world.

## dn26:27.7
- **PLI**: Evaṁ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu attadīpo viharati attasaraṇo anaññasaraṇo dhammadīpo dhammasaraṇo anaññasaraṇo.
- **EN**: That’s how a mendicant lives with themselves as island and refuge, without other refuge; with the teachings as island and refuge, without other refuge.
