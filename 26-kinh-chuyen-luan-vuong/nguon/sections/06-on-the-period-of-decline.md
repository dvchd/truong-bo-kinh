## dn26:17.16
- **PLI**: adhammarāgo visamalobho micchādhammo.
- **EN**: illicit desire, immoral greed, and wrong religious teachings.
- **NOTE**: These three terms recur in a similar context at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.56/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 3.56:2.1</a>. The commentaries to the two passages give quite different explanations. | “Illicit desire” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>adhammarāgo</i>) probably refers to any form of desire that is illegal or transgressive, such as incest, rape, or pedophilia. | “Immoral greed” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>visamalobho</i>) implies excess and imbalance. Both are, however, explained in the commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.56/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 3.56:2.1</a> as desire for the belongings of others. | <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Micchādhammo</i> is hard to pin down. The commentary here explains it as “men with men and women with women”, but this must be rejected as there is no hint anywhere in early Pali (or elsewhere in early Indian culture) that same sex relations were considered immoral; this is a peculiarity of Abrahamic religions. The commentary to <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an3.56/en/sujato#2.1'>AN 3.56:2.1</a> explains it as “indulgence in what is reckoned an inappropriate basis” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>avatthupaṭisevanasaṅkhātena</i>). This implies intercourse with one of the women mentioned at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/mn41/en/sujato#8.4'>MN 41:8.4</a>, which includes those who are “protected by principle” (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhammarakkhitā</i>). This sense of <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dhamma</i> is explained at <a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-bu-vb-ss5/en/sujato#4.2.16'>Bu Ss 5:4.2.16</a> as “religion”. Here I think the sense is similar: customs or beliefs that legitimize immorality.
