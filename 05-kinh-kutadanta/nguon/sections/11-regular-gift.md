## dn5:22.4  `dr9.196, km15.45, ms6D_570`
- **PLI**: “Yāni kho pana tāni, brāhmaṇa, niccadānāni anukulayaññāni sīlavante pabbajite uddissa diyyanti;
- **EN**: “The regular gifts as propitious sacrifice given specially to ethical renunciates;
- **NOTE**: The <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukula</i> (“propitious”) sacrifice is mentioned several times (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn5/en/sujato#22.4'>DN 5:22.4</a>, <a href='https://suttacentral.net/an4.40/en/sujato#2.2'>AN 4.40:2.2</a>). The Pali commentaries interpret it as “in line with (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>anu-</i>) family (<i lang='pi' translate='no'>kula</i>)”. But nothing in the contexts connects it with family customs, nor is such a term known in Sanskrit. It seems rather to be a misunderstanding of Sanskrit <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukūla</i>, literally “along the riverbank”, and in applied use, “in conformity with”, “favorable to”, as in the favor of the gods. This is the standard reading in Buddhist Sanskrit. Such a sacrifice is characterized, not by family custom, but by harmlessness. Thus <i lang='pi' translate='no'>anukula</i> means “propitious, favored (by the gods and the Dhamma)”.
