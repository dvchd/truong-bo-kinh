- **EN**: Sir, it was more than a few days ago—on the fifteenth day sabbath on the full moon day at the entry to the rainy season—when all the gods of the thirty-three were sitting together in the Hall of Clear Right.
- **NOTE**: It is not just the Vajjis and the Buddhist Saṅgha who meet frequently in a hall to discuss business, but the gods as well. Here we get a rare glimpse into how the heavens work, or more to the point, how depictions of heavenly proceedings act as a template for how things should be on earth.

## dn18:13.5
- **PLI**: dhammassa ca sudhammataṁ.
- **EN**: and the clear rightness of the teaching;
- **NOTE**: In the Suttas, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>sudhamma</i> (“clear right”) is used in two main contexts: as the name of the hall of the gods, and in this expression (it is also a personal name). Both are found in this Sutta, suggesting they have the same meaning. <i lang='pi' translate='no'>Dhammassa ca sudhammataṁ</i> echoes Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 14.2.2.29, <i lang='pi' translate='no'>dharmāsi sudharmeti</i> (“You are a well-supporting support”). This is a ritual invocation of the Sun as that which supports everything here in this world. In Pali, it is something we are frequently urged to “behold” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/thag4.2/en/sujato#4.2'>Thag 4.2:4.2</a>), suggesting the metaphorical link with the Sun’s brilliance is not entirely lost. And as the hall of the gods, the sense of the “Sun” is also fitting. The idea is that the Sun is regular and lawful, and by shedding its light on all of us, it is a clear visible manifestation of what is right and just.
