## dn18:4.2
- **PLI**: “ime kho panāpi ahesuṁ māgadhakā paricārakā bahū ceva rattaññū ca abbhatītā kālaṅkatā.
- **EN**: “But there were also Magadhan devotees—many, and of long standing too—who have passed away.
- **NOTE**: Bearing in mind that none of the nations were actually mentioned in the relevant Mahāparinibbānasutta passage, this whole introduction is framed to emphasize the importance of Magadha to Buddhism, especially given the problematic character of Ajātasattu. In the decades following the Buddha’s death, the Buddhist community adapted to a new political landscape which for a time saw the entirety of Buddhism contained within the sprawling Magadhan empire.

## dn18:4.3
- **PLI**: Suññā maññe aṅgamagadhā aṅgamāgadhakehi paricārakehi abbhatītehi kālaṅkatehi.
- **EN**: You’d think that Aṅga and Magadha were empty of devotees who have passed away!

## dn18:4.4
- **PLI**: Te kho panāpi ahesuṁ buddhe pasannā dhamme pasannā saṅghe pasannā sīlesu paripūrakārino.
- **EN**: But they too had confidence in the Buddha, the teaching, and the Saṅgha, and had fulfilled their ethics.

## dn18:4.5
- **PLI**: Te abbhatītā kālaṅkatā bhagavatā abyākatā;
- **EN**: The Buddha hasn’t declared their passing.

## dn18:4.6

- **EN**: It would be good to do so, for many people would gain confidence, and so be reborn in a good place.

## dn18:4.7
- **PLI**: Ayaṁ kho panāpi ahosi rājā māgadho seniyo bimbisāro dhammiko dhammarājā hito brāhmaṇagahapatikānaṁ negamānañceva jānapadānañca.
- **EN**: That King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha was a just and principled king who benefited the brahmins and householders, and people of town and country.
- **NOTE**: As we known from <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn2/en/sujato'>DN 2</a>, Bimbisāra had recently been murdered by Ajātasattu.

## dn18:4.8
- **PLI**: Apissudaṁ manussā kittayamānarūpā viharanti:
- **EN**: People still sing his praises:

## dn18:4.9
- **PLI**: ‘evaṁ no so dhammiko dhammarājā sukhāpetvā kālaṅkato, evaṁ mayaṁ tassa dhammikassa dhammarañño vijite phāsu viharimhā’ti.
- **EN**: ‘That just and principled king, who made us so happy, has passed away. Life was good under his dominion.’
- **NOTE**: A not-so-subtle hint of the changes under Ajātasattu. The passing of Bimbisāra signifies the crumbling of the social order that prevailed in the Buddha’s lifetime, bringing with it the immediate threat of war and chaos. This sutta establishes a timeless order that persists while worldly conditions fluctuate.

## dn18:4.12

- **EN**: ‘Until his dying day, King Bimbisāra sang the Buddha’s praises!’
- **NOTE**: Elsewhere the suttas say that Bimbisāra went for refuge (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn4/en/sujato#6.33'>DN 4:6.33</a>), while the Vinaya says he was in fact a stream-enterer (<a href='https://suttacentral.net/pli-tv-kd1/en/sujato#22.9.1'>Kd 1:22.9.1</a>), which is confirmed below. The Jains, however, claim him as one of their own but say he went to hell for committing suicide. Like his son Ajātasattu, it is likely that he frequented several teachers in his realm.
