# Muc 26.3 — Mindfulness Meditation  (16 đoạn)

## dn18:26.3.0
- **PLI**: 8. Catusatipaṭṭhāna
- **EN**: 8. Mindfulness Meditation

## dn18:26.4  `bj8.330, cck10.264, csp1ed7.175, csp2ed7.175, dr10.246, mc10.186, ms7D_636, msdiv289, sya10.248`
- **PLI**: “Taṁ kiṁ maññanti, bhonto devā tāvatiṁsā, yāva supaññattā cime tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena cattāro satipaṭṭhānā paññattā kusalassādhigamāya.
- **EN**: “What do the good gods of the thirty-three think? How well described by the Buddha are the four kinds of mindfulness meditation! They are taught for achieving what is skillful.
- **NOTE**: Normally <i lang='pi' translate='no'>satipaṭṭhāna</i> is taught “in order to purify sentient beings, to get past sorrow and crying, to make an end of pain and sadness, to end the cycle of suffering, and to realize extinguishment” (eg. <a href='https://suttacentral.net/dn22/en/sujato#1.7'>DN 22:1.7</a>). Here, in yet another unique phrasing, a more humble goal is sought.

## dn18:26.5
- **PLI**: Katame cattāro?
- **EN**: What four?

## dn18:26.6
- **PLI**: Idha, bho, bhikkhu ajjhattaṁ kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ.
- **EN**: It’s when a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of the body internally—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world.

## dn18:26.7
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ kāye kāyānupassī viharanto tattha sammā samādhiyati, sammā vippasīdati.
- **EN**: As they meditate in this way, they become rightly immersed in that, and rightly serene.

## dn18:26.8
- **PLI**: So tattha sammā samāhito sammā vippasanno bahiddhā parakāye ñāṇadassanaṁ abhinibbatteti.
- **EN**: Then they give rise to knowledge and vision of other people’s bodies externally.
- **NOTE**: Again the liberating dimension is ignored in favor of worldly psychic abilities. This is another unique formulation.

## dn18:26.9
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati …pe…
- **EN**: They meditate observing an aspect of feelings internally …

## dn18:26.10
- **PLI**: bahiddhā paravedanāsu ñāṇadassanaṁ abhinibbatteti.
- **EN**: Then they give rise to knowledge and vision of other people’s feelings externally.

## dn18:26.11
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ citte cittānupassī viharati …pe…
- **EN**: They meditate observing an aspect of the mind internally …

## dn18:26.12
- **PLI**: bahiddhā paracitte ñāṇadassanaṁ abhinibbatteti.
- **EN**: Then they give rise to knowledge and vision of other people’s minds externally.

## dn18:26.13
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ.
- **EN**: They meditate observing an aspect of principles internally—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world.

## dn18:26.14
- **PLI**: Ajjhattaṁ dhammesu dhammānupassī viharanto tattha sammā samādhiyati, sammā vippasīdati.
- **EN**: As they meditate in this way, they become rightly immersed in that, and rightly serene.

## dn18:26.15
- **PLI**: So tattha sammā samāhito sammā vippasanno bahiddhā paradhammesu ñāṇadassanaṁ abhinibbatteti.
- **EN**: Then they give rise to knowledge and vision of other people’s principles externally.

## dn18:26.16
- **PLI**: Ime kho, bho, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena cattāro satipaṭṭhānā paññattā kusalassādhigamāyā”ti.
- **EN**: These are the four kinds of mindfulness meditation taught by the Buddha for achieving what is skillful.”
